# Week 01 – Explaining your end-to-end pipeline

## Outcomes (by end of week)

- Explain your pipeline in 2–3 minutes using PREP and handle 3 follow-ups.
- Use 6–8 chunks naturally (ingestion → consumption, bottleneck, trade-off, SLA).
- Produce ≥3 complex sentences (relative/concessive/participle) in speech.

## Inputs (choose 1–2)

- Video: "How our data pipeline works" (search YouTube; pick one with a transcript)
- Article: Company blog post on your pipeline or similar architecture

## Chunk Bank (8–12)

- end-to-end; from ingestion through consumption
- the bottleneck tends to be …
- we optimize for X at the expense of Y
- backfill; late-arriving data; idempotent
- data contract; breaking change; schema evolution
- SLA/SLO; observability; lineage
- incremental load; checkpoint; watermark
- downstream consumers; semantic layer

## Grammar/Pronunciation Focus

- Grammar: Concessive contrast ("Much as streaming reduces latency, it introduces complexity.")
- Pronunciation: Thought groups and stress on new info

## 90-Minute Daily Schedule

- Warm-up (10 min): 4–3–2 drill on yesterday; 1-min summary
- Input (15 min): watch/read with transcript; note 8–12 key sentences
- Shadowing (15 min): slow → normal → fast; focus linking, stress
- Chunks/SRS (10 min): add 8–12 chunks; 2 original examples each
- Speaking task (25 min): 2–3 min talk + 1 repeat; include 8–10 min Q&A/role-play
- Pronunciation (10 min): micro-drill using shadowed lines
- Log (5 min): 1 chunk used, 1 error to fix, 1 goal for tomorrow

## Exercises & Prompts

- PREP: "We chose streaming over batch." Give Reason, Example, and restate.
- Compare–Contrast–Recommend: Batch vs Streaming on latency, complexity, cost.
- Problem–Solution–Impact: A data quality incident and your mitigation + outcome.
- STAR story: A migration you led; your role and the measurable result.
- Role-play prompt:

  ```text
  You are a tough senior PM interviewing a Data Engineer. Topic: Explaining the current data pipeline and justifying streaming vs batch. Be concise, interrupt sometimes, and ask follow-up why/how questions. After 6–8 minutes, give me 3 precise reformulations I could use.
  ```

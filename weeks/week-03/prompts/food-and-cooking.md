# Full practice conversation (B1) — Food and Cooking Experiences

Direction: Read aloud in a warm, conversational tone.

Speaker 2: Do you like cooking? What kind of food do you usually make?
Speaker 1: I enjoy cooking, especially on weekends when I have more time. I usually make simple dishes like pasta, stir-fries, and soups.
Speaker 2: That sounds good. Where did you learn to cook?
Speaker 1: Mostly from my mom and watching cooking videos on YouTube. I started with basic recipes and gradually tried more complex dishes.
Speaker 2: What's your favorite dish to cook?
Speaker 1: I really like making homemade pizza. It's fun to make the dough and choose different toppings. Plus, it's cheaper than ordering delivery.
Speaker 2: How often do you cook at home versus eating out?
Speaker 1: I cook at home about 5-6 days a week. I only eat out on weekends or when I'm too tired to cook.
Speaker 2: What about grocery shopping? Do you plan your meals ahead?
Speaker 1: I make a shopping list based on what I want to cook that week. It helps me avoid buying things I don't need and saves money.
Speaker 2: Have you ever had a cooking disaster? What happened?
Speaker 1: Oh yes! Once I tried to make bread and forgot to add salt. It tasted terrible - like eating cardboard. I learned to always check the recipe twice.
Speaker 2: What's the most challenging thing you've ever cooked?
Speaker 1: I tried to make a complicated French dish once - it had about 20 ingredients and took 3 hours. It turned out okay, but I probably won't make it again.
Speaker 2: Do you like trying new cuisines or recipes?
Speaker 1: I love trying new things! Recently I made Thai curry for the first time. It was spicy but really delicious. I'm always looking for new recipes to try.
Speaker 2: What's your go-to meal when you're short on time?
Speaker 1: Stir-fry with whatever vegetables I have in the fridge. It's quick, healthy, and you can use almost any ingredients.
Speaker 2: Do you have any cooking tips for beginners?
Speaker 1: Start with simple recipes, read the instructions carefully, and don't be afraid to make mistakes. Everyone burns things when they're learning to cook.

## Optional reformulations (use if you want to sound a bit more polished)

- "I find that cooking at home is both more economical and more satisfying than eating out regularly."
- "Starting with basic recipes and gradually building up your skills is the best way to learn cooking."
- "Having a meal plan and shopping list really helps me stay organized and avoid food waste."
- "Don't let cooking disasters discourage you - they're just part of the learning process."

## Key vocabulary for food and cooking

### Cooking activities
- **Make**: to create or prepare food
- **Cook**: to prepare food by heating it
- **Bake**: to cook food in an oven
- **Stir-fry**: to cook food quickly in a hot pan while stirring

### Food preparation
- **Recipe**: a set of instructions for making a dish
- **Ingredients**: the different foods used to make a dish
- **Dough**: a mixture of flour and liquid used to make bread or pizza
- **Toppings**: ingredients added on top of food

### Cooking experiences
- **Disaster**: a complete failure or very bad result
- **Challenging**: difficult to do or accomplish
- **Go-to**: something you rely on or use frequently
- **Economical**: good value for money

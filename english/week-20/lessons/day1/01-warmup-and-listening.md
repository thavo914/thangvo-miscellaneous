# Day 1 - Exercise 1: Warm-up Reflex & Active Listening (35–45 Mins)

> [!NOTE]
> **Block 1 Focus**: Spontaneous spoken English formulation without internal translation (15 mins) + Acute auditory decoding via dictation (20–30 mins).

---

## 🎙️ Part 1: Speaking Reflex - The Daily Commentator (15 Mins)

### Goal
Build spontaneous English formulation without translating from Vietnamese in your head by narrating your morning setup, backlog tickets, and upcoming priorities out loud.

### Instructions
1. Look around your workstation, open terminal tabs, Jira board, or team Slack channels.
2. Speak continuously out loud for **3 minutes** narrating what you did yesterday, what you are tackling today, and how you will coordinate with your teammates.
3. Intentionally inject at least two of today's target phrases:
   - `be going to go ahead and + [Base Verb]`
   - `would you be able to + [Base Verb]`
   - `have a quick look at`
   - `pull one's weight`
   - `at the conclusion of the meeting`

### 💡 Worked Example (What to Say Out Loud)
> *"Looking at my task queue this morning, the streaming pipeline checkpointing fix is ready for testing. In yesterday's sync, I was busy investigating consumer offset lag. Today, I'm **going to go ahead and deploy** the patch to our staging environment before noon. I also notice a couple of unreviewed pull requests from my teammate Sarah. I'm going to message her: '**Would you be able to go have a quick look** at my configuration changes now, please?' Everyone on our team is eager to **pull their weight**, especially since we have an upcoming release. We have an alignment sync at 10 AM, and I'll confirm the deployment timeline **at the conclusion of the meeting**."*

---

## 🎧 Part 2: Active Listening - TOEIC Dictation (20–30 Mins)

### Goal
Train acoustic decoding, catch rapid spoken reductions (e.g., *gonna*, *would you*, *have a*), and notice natural rhythm markers in realistic workplace English dialogues.

### Instructions
1. Open a TOEIC Short Conversations / Short Talks practice audio clip (Parts 3 or 4).
2. Listen to **3 distinct audio passages**. Pause sentence-by-sentence and transcribe every single word onto paper or into a blank text document.
3. Compare against the audio transcript and highlight missed linking sounds, prepositions, and weak forms with a red pen.

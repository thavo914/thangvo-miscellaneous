# Day 2 - Exercise 2: IELTS Speaking Prep & Topic 1 Mastery (60 Mins)

> [!NOTE]
> **Block 2 Focus**: Structured spoken production across IELTS Parts 1, 2, and 3, rooted in practical workplace collaboration, deadline negotiation, and team alignment.

---

## 🗣️ Part 1: Introductory Interview (3 Questions)

Answer these introductory questions out loud. Aim for 3–4 sentences per answer, using high-frequency B1–B2 vocabulary and natural softeners (`would`, `I think`).

### Question 1: How do you usually handle unexpected requests or sudden tasks at work?
* **Model Answer Framework**:
  > *"Whenever an urgent request comes up unexpectedly, I don't panic or immediately say no. Instead, I take a quick look at my current sprint backlog to see what can be shifted. If it's a tight turnaround, I'll tell my lead that **I think I can make that work, as long as** we push secondary tasks back by a couple of days."*

### Question 2: Do you prefer working independently or collaborating closely with team members?
* **Model Answer Framework**:
  > *"Honestly, I prefer **a mix of both**. When I'm writing complex SQL queries or debugging pipeline logic, having deep focused time alone is essential. However, when we're deciding on architecture or dividing up on-call responsibilities, close collaboration ensures everyone is eager to **pull their weight**."*

### Question 3: How do you feel when a manager asks for your opinion on a technical decision?
* **Model Answer Framework**:
  > *"I really appreciate that level of trust. When a manager says '**It's your call**' regarding an implementation approach, it gives me ownership. I always make sure to evaluate the trade-offs carefully and explain the rationale clearly so the whole team stays aligned."*

## 📇 Part 2: Long Turn (Cue Card)

You have 1 minute to take notes and 2 minutes to speak uninterrupted.

```text
Describe a situation where you had to negotiate a deadline or task priority with a colleague or manager.

You should say:
• Who you had to negotiate with
• What the task or project was
• What compromise or solution you proposed
And explain how the outcome turned out for you and the team.
```

### 💡 Brainstorming Bullet Points & Vocabulary Bank:
* **The Situation**: A product manager asked for three additional aggregation tables right before an end-of-quarter release.
* **The Conflict**: Engineering bandwidth was fully committed; accepting without conditions would jeopardize pipeline stability.
* **The Compromise**: Used `I think I can make that work` combined with `meet halfway` — agreeing to deliver the core metrics immediately while deferring supplementary dashboards to next sprint.
* **The Outcome**: Delivered on time, received **a rave review** from stakeholders, and avoided burning out the team.

## 💬 Part 3: Analytical Discussion (2 Questions)

Engage in a 2-way analytical discussion exploring broader workplace dynamics.

### Question 1: Why is flexibility considered one of the most critical traits in modern engineering teams?
* **Key Arguments to Articulate**:
  1. *Requirements change rapidly*: Market demands, user feedback, and unexpected bugs require teams to pivot without resentment.
  2. *Resource constraints*: Team members get sick or on-call spikes occur; being able to adapt and help out keeps the release pipeline flowing.
  3. *Sentence Starter*: *"In fast-paced environments, rigid planning often fails. Teams that **guarantee flexibility** and know how to **meet halfway** consistently deliver higher quality software."*

### Question 2: How can professionals decline unreasonable requests without damaging working relationships?
* **Key Arguments to Articulate**:
  1. *Acknowledge intent first*: Validate the importance of the stakeholder's goal before explaining constraints.
  2. *Offer alternatives*: Never say a flat "no"; say "Yes, if..." or "Not right now, but we can do X."
  3. *Sentence Starter*: *"The key is using diplomatic language like '**That won't be necessary**' or '**I would recommend** prioritizing X first so we don't spread ourselves too thin.' It shows you care about project success rather than just avoiding work."*

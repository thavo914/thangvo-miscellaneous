## 🎙️ Section 1: Workplace Monologue - Balancing On-Call and Project Deadlines

*Read this monologue aloud, paying close attention to natural breathing boundaries and transitions.*

> *"One of the most valuable lessons I've learned in my engineering career is that being a reliable team member doesn't mean saying yes to every single request. Earlier this year, our data team **was looking to hire** a senior engineer to **fill an open position**, but the recruiting process dragged on for several weeks. During that time, incoming support requests began piling up, and management asked if I could take on extra weekend maintenance shifts.  
> 
> While everyone on the team was eager to **pull their weight**, I knew that stretching myself too thin would lead to sloppy code and pipeline regressions. I sat down with my engineering manager and explained that I would **not be available as much as the job requires** on weekends, but **I thought I could make that work** by dedicating four uninterrupted hours every Thursday to mentor our junior engineer and automate our table backfills.  
> 
> My manager completely respected that honest boundary. By setting realistic expectations early instead of overpromising, we kept production stable and maintained team morale. Knowing when to politely say '**That won't be necessary**' is just as critical as writing clean SQL."*

### Key Vocabulary & Concepts
* **be looking to hire**: Đang tìm kiếm nhân sự mới.
* **fill an open position**: Lấp vào một vị trí đang bỏ trống.
* **pull one's weight**: Đóng góp công sức sòng phẳng, làm tròn trách nhiệm.
* **not be available as much as the job requires**: Không thể đáp ứng nhiều thời gian như công việc đòi hỏi.
* **make that work**: Thu xếp ổn thỏa.

## 📇 Section 2: Band 6.5–7.0 Model Cue Card Answer

*Topic: Describe a situation where you had to negotiate a deadline or task priority with a colleague or manager.*

> *"I'd like to talk about a situation that happened about three months ago, right before our quarterly financial reporting release. My team was responsible for maintaining the main ETL pipelines that supply daily metrics to the executive dashboard.  
> 
> On Tuesday afternoon, our Product Manager scheduled an ad-hoc sync and asked if we could deliver three additional revenue breakdown tables by Thursday morning. We were already working against a **tight turnaround**, and adding those tables on such short notice posed a serious risk of missing our primary deployment deadline.  
> 
> Rather than turning down the request outright, I decided to propose a compromise. I told him: '**I think I can make that work, as long as** we focus exclusively on the core revenue metrics first, and push the secondary customer breakdown tables to the following sprint.' I also asked: '**Would you be able to** confirm which metric is the absolute must-have for the board meeting?'  
> 
> He appreciated the constructive pushback and agreed that **a mix of both** was the most practical way forward. He told me, '**It's your call** on the technical sequencing.' We delivered the core tables on time, and our sprint retrospective received **rave reviews** from management because we balanced speed with system stability."*

### 💡 High-Scoring Linguistic Highlights
* **tight turnaround**: Khoảng thời gian xử lý rất gấp.
* **I think I can make that work, as long as...**: Nhận việc linh hoạt đi kèm điều kiện thực tế.
* **a mix of both**: Phương án dung hòa cả hai bên.
* **It's your call**: Trao quyền tự quyết định.
* **rave reviews**: Những lời đánh giá khen ngợi hết lời.

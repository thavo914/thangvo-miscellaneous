# Day 1: Grammar & Structures (Week 10 - Topic 1 Focus)

This file houses the grammar rules, sentence templates, and drills for the first half of Week 10, focusing on **Topic 1: Small Talk at the Office Coffee Machine & Weekend Plans**.

## 🔍 1. Grammar Focus (3 Target Rules)

### 1. The Future Continuous (Talking about specific weekend/future plans)
- **Quote:** *"Well, this Saturday, I'll be participating in a running race nearby."*
- **Rule Explanation:** We use the Future Continuous to talk about actions that will be in progress at a specific time in the future. It is widely used to share schedule-specific weekend plans.
  - **Formula:** `[Subject] + will be + [Verb-ing]` (Short form: `I'll be / She'll be / We'll be`)
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Avoid using the base verb instead of the `-ing` form after `will be`.
  > - *Incorrect:* "This Saturday afternoon, I **will be play** tennis."
  > - *Correct:* "This Saturday afternoon, I **will be playing** tennis."

---

### 2. Present Perfect with "just" and "already" (Recent updates & achievements)
- **Quote:** *"Hey Sarah! Yeah, just trying to wake up."* / *"I've already heard good reviews about their fresh pasta."*
- **Rule Explanation:** 
  - We use `just` with the Present Perfect to express that an action happened a very short time ago.
  - We use `already` to show that an action has happened sooner than expected, or to confirm that a past event has been completed.
  - **Formula:** `[Subject] + have/has + just/already + [Past Participle]`
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Do not put `just` or `already` after the past participle verb.
  > - *Incorrect:* "I have finished my work **just**."
  > - *Correct:* "I have **just** finished my work."

---

### 3. "Used to" (Contrasting past habits with present routines)
- **Quote:** *"I used to play video games all weekend, but now I prefer to keep active."*
- **Rule Explanation:** We use `used to + base verb` to describe past habits, states, or repeated actions that are no longer true in the present.
  - **Formula:** `[Subject] + used to + [Base Verb]` (Negative: `[Subject] + didn't use to + [Base Verb]`)
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Do not include a "d" in "used to" after "didn't".
  > - *Incorrect:* "I **didn't used to** run in the mornings."
  > - *Correct:* "I **didn't use to** run in the mornings."

## 🧱 2. Sentence Structure Templates (5 Patterns)

### Template 1: Expressing long-term intention with recent lack of time
- **Communicative Function:** Explaining why you haven't done something you want to do.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `I've been meaning to + [Verb Phrase] + but + [Subject] + haven't had the time.`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "I've been meaning to try that new restaurant downtown, but I haven't had the time."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "I've been meaning to clean up our staging database logs, but I haven't had the time."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "I've been meaning to catch up with our product manager, but I haven't had the time."

---

### Template 2: Asking for a casual opinion on a current situation
- **Communicative Function:** Making small talk about a recent change or workplace environment.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `How are you + [Verb-ing] + [Noun Phrase]?`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "How are you liking your new neighborhood?"
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "How are you finding the new database migration tool?"
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "How are you liking the new hybrid work policy?"

---

### Template 3: Cascading casual questions about plans
- **Communicative Function:** Asking a colleague about their weekend or evening plans.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `Are you up to + [Noun/Verb-ing] + this + [Time Period]?`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "Are you up to anything exciting this Saturday?"
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "Are you up to reviewing that pipeline design this afternoon?"
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "Are you up to joining us for dinner this Friday?"

---

### Template 4: Planning around expected conditions/weather
- **Communicative Function:** Making a soft plan based on forecasts or situational expectations.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `It's supposed to + [Verb Phrase], so + [Subject] + might + [Verb Phrase].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "It's supposed to rain on Sunday, so I might just stay in and watch a movie."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "It's supposed to be a low-traffic hour, so we might just deploy the patch tonight."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "It's supposed to be a busy sprint, so we might need to skip the Friday team lunch."

---

### Template 5: Sharing positive anticipation
- **Communicative Function:** Sharing why you are happy or excited about an upcoming event.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `I'm really looking forward to + [Noun/Verb-ing] + because + [Reason].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "I'm really looking forward to sleeping in because this week was exhausting."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "I'm really looking forward to automating this ETL script because it takes hours to run manually."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "I'm really looking forward to the team building event because it will be fun."

## 🛠️ 4. Spot the Mistake (Error Correction Drill)
*Identify the grammatical or vocabulary error in each sentence, and rewrite it correctly.*

1. "I have just completed the staging deploy just now."
   - *Your Correction*: ____________________________________________________________________
2. "Next weekend, I'll be play tennis with a coworker from marketing."
   - *Your Correction*: ____________________________________________________________________
3. "I didn't used to run before, but now I run twice a week to keep active."
   - *Your Correction*: ____________________________________________________________________

<details>
<summary>🔑 Click to reveal Answer Key & Explanations</summary>

1. **Incorrect**: "...deploy **just now**." (redundant with `just`)
   - **Correct**: "I have **just** completed the staging deploy." OR "I completed the staging deploy just now."
   - **Why**: Do not use both `just` (present perfect) and `just now` (simple past time indicator) in the same sentence as it is redundant.
2. **Incorrect**: "...I'll be **play**..."
   - **Correct**: "Next weekend, **I'll be playing** tennis with a coworker from marketing."
   - **Why**: Future continuous requires `will be + verb-ing`.
3. **Incorrect**: "...I didn't **used** to..."
   - **Correct**: "I didn't **use** to run before, but now I run twice a week to keep active."
   - **Why**: In negative sentences with "did not / didn't", the verb drops the "-d" and remains in the base form ("use to").
</details>

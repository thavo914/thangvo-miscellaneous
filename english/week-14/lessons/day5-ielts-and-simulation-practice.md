## 🗣️ 1. IELTS Speaking Practice Questions

### Part 1 (Interview)
1. **Do you often have to deal with technical problems at your work?**
   - *Tip*: Talk about your role as a data engineer and how debugging is part of your daily routine.
2. **How do you feel when a system or tool you are using suddenly crashes?**
   - *Tip*: Describe the initial pressure but emphasize staying calm and ruling out common issues systematically.
3. **Do you think it's important to document errors or problems after they are fixed?**
   - *Tip*: Mention that writing post-mortems helps the team catch bugs early and prevents future failures.

### Part 2 (Cue Card)
**Describe a time when a system, machine, or project you were responsible for had a major problem or failure.**
You should say:
- What the system or project was
- What went wrong and when it happened
- How you and your team resolved the issue
- And explain how you felt after the problem was successfully fixed.

### Part 3 (Analytical Discussion)
1. **Why is it critical for modern companies to have automated monitoring systems rather than relying on manual checks?**
2. **How does team collaboration affect the speed of resolving crisis situations in a workplace?**

## 🎙️ 4. Interactive Shadowing & Pacing Guide

Practice speaking these lines out loud. Use the slashes `/` for pauses, and stress the **capitalized** words.

**Line 1 (Explaining incident timeline)**:
> *"The database had been running **SMOOTHLY** / until two **P-M** / when the replication lag **SPIKED**."*

- **Pacing**: Pause after *smoothly* and *P-M*. Say "P-M" clearly with stress on both letters.
- **Intonation**: Rise on *smoothly* to set the baseline, then drop on *spiked* to indicate the negative event.

**Line 2 (Describing root cause search)**:
> *"It took us **SEVERAL** hours / to track down the **EXACT** issue / which was the main **POINT** / of **FAILURE**."*

- **Pacing**: Make a clean pause after *hours*, *issue*, and *point*. Maintain a steady speed for *to track down the*.
- **Intonation**: Stress **SEVERAL**, **EXACT**, and **FAILURE** to convey the scale of the effort and the core problem.

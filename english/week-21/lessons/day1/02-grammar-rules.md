## 📘 Rule 1: Future Continuous (`will be + V-ing`) for Polite Updates & Routine Availability

* **Quote from Dialogue**: *"I'**ll be stepping away** for 45 minutes this afternoon to meet a client."*
* **Rule Explanation**: 
  While textbooks often teach `will be + V-ing` strictly for "actions in progress at a specific future moment", native speakers in workplace settings use it primarily as a **polite softening tool** to announce personal schedules, routine habits, or upcoming absences without sounding rigid, demanding, or blunt:
  $$\mathbf{[Subject] + will\ be\ /\ 'll\ be\ +\ [Verb\text{-}ing]\ +\ [Context\ /\ Time]}$$
  $$\mathbf{Soft\ Question:\ Will\ you\ be\ +\ [Verb\text{-}ing]\ +\ [Resource\ /\ System]?}$$
* **Common Pitfall**:
  > ❌ **Rigid / Blunt**: *I will work from home tomorrow.* (Sounds like an official proclamation or contract mandate).  
  > ❌ **Broken Form**: *I'll be leave early today.* (Missing `-ing` after `will be`).  
  > ✅ **Natural Native English**: *I'**ll be working from home** tomorrow.* / *I'**ll be leaving early** today.*

## 📘 Rule 2: High-Precision Workplace Prepositions (`in`, `on`, `at`, `by`, `from`)

* **Quote from Dialogue**: *"Believe it or not, I was once **in your shoes**... working **on real distributed systems**."*
* **Rule Explanation**:
  Prepositions establish spatial, organizational, and operational relationships in business English. Mastering these 5 high-frequency patterns prevents clumsy literal translations:
  1. **`in`**: Systems, states, teams, or shoes: *in production*, *in your shoes*, *in our squad*.
  2. **`on`**: Platforms, projects, committees, calls: *on the data team*, *working on the pipeline*, *on the call*.
  3. **`at`**: Physical locations, companies, times: *at Juniper*, *at the office*, *at 3:00 PM*.
  4. **`by`**: Payer/Authorizing entity or deadline: *paid for by corporate*, *replaced by IT*, *by the end of the day*.
  5. **`from`**: Origin or source: *data from our warehouse*, *hear from management*.
* **Common Pitfall**:
  > ❌ **Vietlish Trap**: *paid by company* (Need the article: *paid for by corporate* or *by the company*).  
  > ❌ **Wrong Preposition**: *I am working in this project.* → ✅ *I am working **on** this project.*  
  > ❌ **Wrong Preposition**: *He is in the call.* → ✅ *He is **on** the call.*

1. **by** *(Tác nhân thực hiện dịch vụ / hỗ trợ kỹ thuật: by IT Support).*

4. **at / at** *(Địa điểm cụ thể tại tòa nhà: at the main reception hall; thời gian: at 2:00 PM).*

## 📘 Rule 3: The Privilege & Opportunity Pattern: `get to + Base Verb` vs. `have to`

* **Quote from Dialogue**: *"I'm just eager to **get to experience** working on real distributed systems."*
* **Rule Explanation**:
  Many Vietnamese learners overuse `have to` (obligation: *phải làm*) and miss the powerful native formula **`get to + Base Verb`**, which expresses **privilege, fortunate opportunity, or genuine excitement** (*được dịp, có cơ hội quý báu*):
  $$\mathbf{[Subject] + get(s)\ to\ /\ got\ to\ +\ [Base\ Verb]\ +\ [Object]}$$
* **Comparison**:
  * `have to work on this pipeline`: Phải làm vì bị giao việc (bổn phận, trách nhiệm).
  * `get to work on this pipeline`: Được dịp làm, coi đó là cơ hội học hỏi và phát triển đáng quý.
* **Common Pitfall**:
  > ❌ **Misleading Obligation**: *I have to present our architecture to the CTO tomorrow.* (Sounds like a dreaded chore).  
  > ✅ **Positive Opportunity Reflex**: *I **get to present** our architecture to the CTO tomorrow!* (Signals a career milestone).

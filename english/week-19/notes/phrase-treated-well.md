# 📘 Week 19 Curriculum Note: `be treated well by`

> **Target Week**: Week 19  
> **Type**: Conversational Formula / Workplace Phrase  
> **Core Concept**: To experience good, fair, and respectful behavior from an employer, organization, or person, often including good compensation, respect, and work-life balance. *(Vietnamese: được đối đãi tốt / được công ty chăm lo tốt)*

---

## 🌟 1. Core Explanation & Mechanics

This phrase is frequently used in professional contexts to express job satisfaction, loyalty, or to explain why you are declining another offer. 

* **Formula**: `[Subject] have/has been treated well by [Employer/Organization]` or `[Employer] treats [Subject] well`
* **Key Principle**: Often used in the passive voice ("I have been treated well") to emphasize the experience of the employee. It encompasses salary, benefits, respect, and overall culture.

---

## 📚 2. Categorized Usages & Real-Life Examples

### Domain A: Tech Workplace, Career & Retention
* **Discussing Current Job Satisfaction**:
  * *Example*: *"I'm not actively looking for a new role right now because I have been treated really well by my current company."*
* **Loyalty & Declining Offers (Combined with 'High and Dry')**:
  * *Example*: *"I've been treated well by my current company, so I really don't want to leave them high and dry right in the middle of our cloud migration."*
* **Weighing Job Offers**:
  * *Example*: *"Even though the startup offered a higher base salary, I decided to stay since my current employer has always treated me well, especially regarding remote work flexibility."*
* **1-on-1s and Performance Reviews**:
  * *Example*: *"I really appreciate the support from leadership this year; I feel like our engineering team has been treated very well during the restructuring."*

### Domain B: Daily Life & Customer Experience
* **Brand Loyalty**:
  * *Example*: *"I always buy my marathon training gear from that local running shop because I've always been treated well by their staff."*

---

## ⚠️ 3. Common Pitfalls & Vietlish Traps

| ❌ Common Mistake / Awkward Phrasing | ✅ Natural Native English | Why / Linguistic Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *My company treats to me well.* | *My company **treats me well**.* | The verb "treat" takes a direct object. Do not use the preposition "to". |
| *I have been treated good by them.* | *I have been treated **well** by them.* | Use the adverb "well" to modify the verb "treat", not the adjective "good". |
| *The company has good treat for me.* | *The company **treats me well**.* / *I have **been treated well**.* | "Treat" is rarely used as a noun in this context (unless talking about snacks/gifts). Use the verb form. |

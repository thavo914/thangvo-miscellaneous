# 📘 Week 19 Curriculum Note: `make a special exception`

> **Target Week**: Week 19  
> **Type**: Vocabulary Focus / Workplace Phrase  
> **Core Concept**: To deliberately bypass or break a standard rule, policy, or procedure for a particular case, usually because of unique circumstances. *(Vietnamese: tạo một ngoại lệ đặc biệt)*

---

## 🌟 1. Core Explanation & Mechanics

This phrase is used when rules are strict, but a specific situation warrants leniency or a departure from the norm.

* **Formula**: `make a special exception (for [someone/something])`
* **Key Principle**: Used in administrative, technical, or strict environments to signal that this deviation is a one-time occurrence and does not set a precedent.

---

## 📚 2. Categorized Usages & Real-Life Examples

### Domain A: Data Engineering & Software Development
* **Bypassing Procedures**:
  * *Example*: *"Normally, we don't grant direct write access to the production data warehouse, but I'll make a special exception this time since we need to hotfix that critical dashboard before the board meeting."*
* **Skipping Standard Checks**:
  * *Example*: *"We have a strict policy of rejecting any dbt models that aren't fully tested, but the lead engineer made a special exception because the upstream source schema changed abruptly."*

### Domain B: Personal Interests & Daily Life
* **Sports & Training**:
  * *Example*: *"My coach's strict rule is to always rest the day before a race, but she decided to make a special exception because I felt extremely fresh and wanted to do a light shakeout run."*
  * *Example*: *"The club rarely hands out long-term contracts to players over thirty, but they should definitely make a special exception for Mo Salah given his incredible fitness levels."*
* **Diet & Habits**:
  * *Example*: *"I try to avoid eating heavy meals the night before my long runs, though I make a special exception when my mates invite me out for some authentic Italian pasta."*

---

## ⚠️ 3. Common Pitfalls & Vietlish Traps

| ❌ Common Mistake / Awkward Phrasing | ✅ Natural Native English | Why / Linguistic Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *I will break the rule for you this time.* | *I will **make a special exception** for you.* | "Break the rule" can sound slightly aggressive or unprofessional in a corporate context; "make an exception" sounds professional and reasoned. |
| *We made a special exception to him.* | *We made a special exception **for** him.* | The correct preposition is "for", not "to". |

# 📘 Week 19 Curriculum Note: `reach out for an estimate`

> **Target Week**: Week 19  
> **Type**: Conversational Formula / Operations Phrase  
> **Core Concept**: To contact a vendor, contractor, or service provider to request a preliminary price or cost calculation for a potential project or purchase. *(Vietnamese: liên hệ với [công ty/ai đó] để xin báo giá / ước tính chi phí)*

---

## 🌟 1. Core Explanation & Mechanics

This phrase combines two very common corporate English elements: "reach out to" (a soft, professional way of saying "contact") and "an estimate" (a preliminary calculation of cost, also known as a quote).

* **Formula**: `[Subject] reached out to [Vendor/Person] for an estimate`
* **Key Principle**: Used during the planning or budgeting phase of a project before any official commitments or contracts are signed.

---

## 📚 2. Categorized Usages & Real-Life Examples

### Domain A: Tech Workplace, Vendor Management & Budgeting
* **Software Licensing & SaaS**:
  * *Example*: *"The infrastructure is getting too complex to manage internally, so we reached out to Datadog for an estimate on their enterprise tier."*
* **Consulting & Outsourcing**:
  * *Example*: *"Before we officially approve the cloud migration project, I've reached out to a few AWS consulting partners for an estimate."*
  * *Example*: *"We want to outsource our manual QA testing, so my manager reached out to two different agencies for an estimate."*

### Domain B: Personal Life & Home Projects
* **Contractors & Repairs**:
  * *Example*: *"We are thinking about remodeling the kitchen before the holidays, so my wife reached out to a few local contractors for an estimate."*
  * *Example*: *"My car's engine has been making a weird noise, so I reached out to a couple of mechanics for an estimate before taking it in."*

---

## ⚠️ 3. Common Pitfalls & Vietlish Traps

| ❌ Common Mistake / Awkward Phrasing | ✅ Natural Native English | Why / Linguistic Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *I asked them for the price.* | *I **reached out to them for an estimate**.* | In B2B or professional contexts, "getting an estimate" or "getting a quote" sounds much more professional than "asking for the price". |
| *I contact to them for estimate.* | *I **reached out to** them for **an** estimate.* | "Contact" does not take "to". However, "reach out to" is the ultimate native corporate buzzword for initiating communication. Also, don't forget the article "an". |

# 📘 Week 19 Curriculum Note: `to go`

> **Target Week**: Week 19  
> **Type**: Adjective / Adverb Phrase  
> **Core Concept**: (Of food or drink ordered from a restaurant, café, or counter) packaged to be taken away and consumed elsewhere rather than eaten on the premises. *(Vietnamese: mang đi / mang về)*

---

## 🌟 1. Core Explanation & Mechanics

This is the standard, natural way to request takeaway food or drinks in many English-speaking regions (especially North America, though widely understood everywhere).

* **Formula**: `[food/drink item] to go` or `order [something] to go`
* **Key Principle**: Attached directly after the noun or the verb "order/get". Very commonly used when you are in a rush.

---

## 📚 2. Categorized Usages & Real-Life Examples

### Domain A: Busy Workdays & Office Life
* **Working Through Lunch**:
  * *Example*: *"With our production deployment scheduled for noon, I didn't have time to sit down in the cafeteria, so I ordered a quick sandwich and a coffee to go."*
  * *Example*: *"Our sprint retrospective ran over time, so the team lead kindly ordered lunch to go for all the engineers stuck working through the backlog."*

### Domain B: Personal Interests & Hobbies
* **Pre/Post-Run Fuel**:
  * *Example*: *"Before heading out for my Sunday 25 km marathon training run, I always swing by the local bakery to grab an espresso and a pastry to go."*
  * *Example*: *"After finishing a wet and muddy 10k road race, I picked up a hot bowl of soup to go from the food stall to warm up on my drive home."*
* **Football Matchday Routine**:
  * *Example*: *"The atmosphere outside Anfield before the Champions League clash was buzzing, so we just bought a couple of meat pies to go and ate them near the statue of Bill Shankly."*

---

## ⚠️ 3. Common Pitfalls & Vietlish Traps

| ❌ Common Mistake / Awkward Phrasing | ✅ Natural Native English | Why / Linguistic Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *I would like a coffee to take away.* | *I'd like a coffee **to go**.* | While "takeaway" is standard in British English, "to go" is extremely common universally and very snappy for spoken orders. |
| *Can I bring this food home?* | *Can I get this **to go**?* | "Bring home" sounds like a literal relocation rather than the standard ordering convention. |

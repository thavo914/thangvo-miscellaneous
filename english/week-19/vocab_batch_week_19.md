**1. make a special exception**
*(verb phrase)*: to deliberately bypass or break a standard rule, policy, or procedure for a particular case, usually because of unique circumstances. *(Vietnamese: tạo một ngoại lệ đặc biệt)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Normally, we don't grant direct write access to the production data warehouse, but I'll make a special exception this time since we need to hotfix that critical dashboard before the board meeting.
2. My coach's strict rule is to always rest the day before a race, but she decided to make a special exception because I felt extremely fresh and wanted to do a light shakeout run.
3. The club rarely hands out long-term contracts to players over thirty, but they should definitely make a special exception for Mo Salah given his incredible fitness levels.
4. We have a strict policy of rejecting any dbt models that aren't fully tested, but the lead engineer made a special exception because the upstream source schema changed abruptly.
5. I try to avoid eating heavy meals the night before my long runs, though I make a special exception when my mates invite me out for some authentic Italian pasta.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Bend the rules** — implies slightly breaking a rule without causing harm, usually more informal.
- **Waive the requirement** — formal and often used in official or administrative contexts.
- **Make an allowance** — to permit something outside the normal limits, often due to specific constraints.
- **Deviate from the standard** — a formal way to describe doing something differently from the established process.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Stick to the rules** — to follow regulations strictly without deviation.
- **Enforce the policy** — to strictly apply the existing rules.
- **Show zero tolerance** — to refuse any exceptions to the rules, often in a strict or punitive way.

> Even though the club generally prefers signing younger players, they made a special exception to bring in ________________________ because ________________________.

**2. leave someone high and dry**
*(idiom / verb phrase)*: to leave someone in a difficult or helpless situation, especially without the help or resources they were expecting. *(Vietnamese: bỏ mặc ai đó trong tình thế khó khăn / bơ vơ)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. When the senior architect quit right before the major cloud migration, it left the rest of our data team completely high and dry.
2. I was relying on the aid station at mile 18 for hydration, but they had completely run out of water, leaving me high and dry in the sweltering heat.
3. The referee's controversial decision to send off our key defender early in the first half really left the squad high and dry against a relentless attack.
4. If we deprecate the old API without providing a clear migration guide, we're going to leave our downstream analysts high and dry when their dashboards break.
5. My pacing partner cramped up and decided to drop out at the halfway mark, leaving me high and dry for the hardest part of the marathon.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Leave someone in the lurch** — similar idiom meaning to desert someone in difficulties.
- **Strand someone** — to leave someone without the means to move from somewhere.
- **Abandon someone** — formal and strong word for leaving someone completely.
- **Leave someone to fend for themselves** — to force someone to survive or deal with a situation without help.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Bail someone out** — to help someone out of a difficult situation.
- **Have someone's back** — to be prepared to support and protect someone.
- **Come to the rescue** — to save someone from a difficult or dangerous situation.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> We couldn't believe the vendor abruptly shut down the legacy service—it left our whole data engineering department high and dry just when ________________________.

> I thought my running group would wait for me on the trails, but they sped ahead and left me high and dry while I was ________________________.

**3. to go**
*(adjective / adverb phrase)*: (of food or drink ordered from a restaurant, café, or counter) packaged to be taken away and consumed elsewhere rather than eaten on the premises; takeaway. Also frequently used in spoken ordering patterns like *"I'd like something to go"* or *"a coffee to go"*. *(Vietnamese: mang đi / mang về)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. With our production deployment scheduled for noon, I didn't have time to sit down in the cafeteria, so I ordered a quick sandwich and a coffee to go.
2. Before heading out for my Sunday 25 km marathon training run, I always swing by the local bakery to grab an espresso and a pastry to go.
3. The atmosphere outside Anfield before the Champions League clash was buzzing, so we just bought a couple of meat pies to go and ate them near the statue of Bill Shankly.
4. Our sprint retrospective ran over time, so the team lead kindly ordered lunch to go for all the engineers stuck working through the backlog.
5. After finishing a wet and muddy 10k road race, I picked up a hot bowl of soup to go from the food stall to warm up on my drive home.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Takeout / Takeaway** — standard terms in American English (takeout) and British/Commonwealth English (takeaway).
- **For takeout** — commonly heard in restaurant counter questions (*"For here or for takeout?"*).
- **To carry out** — regional US synonym meaning the exact same thing as to go.
- **Grab-and-go** — describes items that are pre-packaged and ready for instant pickup without waiting for cooking.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **For here / Dine-in** — eating or drinking inside the restaurant establishment.
- **Eat in / Sit down** — sitting down at a table inside the venue to have your meal.
- **Table service** — formal dining where waitstaff take your order and serve you at a table.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> I didn't have time to cook because our ETL pipeline was failing, so I decided to order ________________________ to go.

> Whenever I travel to Liverpool for a match at Anfield, I always make sure to grab ________________________ to go before heading into the ground.

**4. by the way**
*(adverb phrase / discourse marker)*: used to introduce a new subject into a conversation, to add an incidental thought, or to mention an extra piece of relevant information casually. *(Vietnamese: nhân tiện / tiện thể / à mà này)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Thanks for reviewing the SQL query so quickly—by the way, did you notice that the staging pipeline had a schema mismatch this morning?
2. I'm aiming for a sub-four-hour marathon in December; by the way, which running shoes did you end up buying for your long runs?
3. Liverpool have three crucial Premier League fixtures coming up this month; by the way, are you heading over to Anfield for any of them?
4. I've finished backfilling the user activity tables; by the way, don't forget that the cloud database is scheduled for maintenance tonight at 10 PM.
5. My training pace felt remarkably smooth during this morning's 15 km tempo run—by the way, are you joining the local running club this weekend?

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Incidentally** — slightly more formal or professional; common in written and business correspondence.
- **As an aside** — explicitly frames the statement as separate from the main topic.
- **While I think of it / While we're on the subject** — natural spoken connector when a related thought suddenly comes to mind.
- **Just so you know** — shares incidental information, with slightly more emphasis on informing the listener.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **To get back to the point** — steers the discussion back to the main topic after a digression.
- **Focusing on the main issue** — emphasizes staying strictly on topic without side remarks.
- **More importantly** — pivots from minor details to the central priority.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> We wrapped up the sprint planning meeting early; by the way, did anyone remember to ________________________?

> I really enjoyed watching Liverpool's counter-pressing yesterday; by the way, did you see ________________________?

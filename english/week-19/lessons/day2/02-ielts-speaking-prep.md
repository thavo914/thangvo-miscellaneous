# Day 2 - Exercise 2: IELTS Speaking Preparation (30 Mins)

> [!NOTE]
> **Block 2 (Part A)**: Topic-focused IELTS Speaking Part 1, 2, and 3 practice calibrated to Band 6.5 natural spoken fluency.

---

## 🎤 Part 1: Introductory Interview (5 Mins)

Answer these questions aloud in 2–3 clear, breath-friendly sentences without overcomplicating your vocabulary:

1. **Do you prefer working alone or as part of a team?**  
   *Speaking Tip*: Explain why balance matters—mention that working with a team helps share the load and **takes routine pressure off your plate**.
   "To be honest, I think a mix of both works best for me. When I collaborate with a team, sharing the workload takes routine pressure off my plate. However, I still need some quiet time alone when I have to focus on complex tasks."

2. **What makes someone easy to work with in an office?**  
   *Speaking Tip*: Focus on clarity and reliability—someone who is **a great match** for a team is humble, communicates early, and pulls their weight.
   "In my opinion, someone is a great match for any team if they communicate early and clearly. You never have to guess what they're working on, and if a problem pops up, they let yo`u know right away instead of waiting until the last minute."
3. **Have you ever welcomed a new member to your team or study group?**  
   *Speaking Tip*: Use the phrase **bring someone on board** to explain how onboarding them helped everyone.
"Yes, just last quarter we brought a junior colleague on board in our data team. I helped him get familiar with our codebase and daily workflow during his first two weeks. Once he settled in, he was able to take on several routine tasks on his own."

## 📇 Part 2: Individual Long Turn - Cue Card (15 Mins)

Take 1 minute to jot down bullet points, then set a 2-minute timer and deliver your monologue out loud without stopping.

```
Candidate Task Card:

Describe a colleague or teammate you really enjoyed working with.

You should say:
- Who this person was and what role they played
- What project or challenge you worked on together
- What made them a great match for your team
and explain how working alongside them helped take pressure off your plate.
```

"I’d like to talk about a former colleague named Minh, who was a senior data engineer on my team. He was brought on board early last year when our department began expanding.

Around that time, we were facing a pretty demanding challenge. We had to migrate several legacy database pipelines to a modern cloud warehouse within just three months. The deadline was tight, and many of the older queries were poorly documented, which caused a lot of stress for the team.

What made Minh a great match for our team was his calm attitude and clear communication. Even when unexpected pipeline bugs popped up, he never panicked or pointed fingers. Instead, he stayed patient and explained the technical trade-offs in a very simple, grounded way.

Working alongside him really took a lot of routine pressure off my plate. He took ownership of the most complex migration logic. That allowed the rest of us to focus on data validation without feeling overwhelmed. Thanks to his support, we delivered the project on time, and nobody felt burned out or left high and dry.

Overall, he was truly one of the most dependable people I’ve ever worked with."

### 💡 Quick Prep Bullet Points (1-Minute Planning Model)
* **Who**: *Minh, a senior data engineer brought on board last spring.*
* **Project**: *Migrating legacy SQL server pipelines to a modern cloud warehouse under a tight three-month deadline.*
* **Why a great match**: *Calm attitude, zero ego, fantastic communicator, always willing to explain technical trade-offs clearly.*
* **Impact**: *Took complex orchestration logic off our plate, preventing burnout and ensuring no one was left high and dry.*

## 🧠 Part 3: Two-Way Analytical Discussion (10 Mins)

Answer these analytical questions with a 3-part structure: **Direct Answer → Reason / Explanation → Real-life Example**.

### Question 1: What factors determine whether a new hire is a great match for a company's culture?
* **Model Outline**:
  * *Direct Answer*: Technical skills are important, but communication style and humility are what truly determine cultural alignment.
  * *Reason*: A brilliant coder who works in isolation or creates friction will drag team morale down, whereas someone open to feedback elevates everyone.
  * *Example*: In data engineering teams, when we **bring someone on board** who is willing to help unblock others, delivery speed improves naturally.

"In my view, while technical ability is certainly important, communication style and humility are what truly determine whether someone is a great match.

A brilliant specialist who works in complete isolation or resists feedback will quickly drag team morale down.

On the other hand, when a technical team brings someone on board who is open, humble, and willing to help unblock others, overall delivery speed improves naturally."

### Question 2: Why do some employees stay loyal to a company even when offered higher pay elsewhere?
* **Model Outline**:
  * *Direct Answer*: Compensation matters, but feeling valued and supported daily often outweighs marginal financial gains.
  * *Reason*: When engineers **have been treated well by** their leadership—with schedule flexibility and respect—they develop genuine loyalty.
  * *Example*: Professionals with strong work ethics refuse to **leave their teammates high and dry** during critical releases simply for a slight raise.

## Exercise 8: Sentence Transformation Practice

### **Transform these sentences as indicated:**

1. **Simple Past → Present Perfect:**
   "I completed the extraction last week."
   → "I have completed the extraction."

2. **Present → Present Perfect Continuous:**
   "I work on the transformation layer."
   → "I have worked on the transformation layer for two weeks."

3. **Future Planning:**
   "I deploy the pipeline next week."
   → "I will deploy the pipeline next week."

4. **Conditional:**
   "I will deploy next week."
   → "If everything goes smoothly, I will deploy next week."

5. **Passive Voice:**
   "DBT performs the transformations."
   → "The transformations is performed by DBT."

6. **Comparative:**
   "DBT is organized. Ad-hoc scripts are not organized."
   → "DBT is more organized than ad-hoc scripts."

7. **Business Language:**
   "I want to tell you about the project."
   → "I'd like to tell you about an update on the project."

8. **Office Conversation:**
   "I have completed the data extraction phase."
   → "I've completed the data extraction part."

### **Answers:**
1. have completed
2. have been working
3. will deploy
4. will deploy
5. are performed
6. more organized
7. provide
8. finished

## Exercise 9: Error Correction

## Practice Guidelines

### **How to Use These Exercises:**

1. **Complete all exercises** - work through each one systematically
2. **Check your answers** - review the correct answers provided
3. **Practice out loud** - say the completed sentences
4. **Record yourself** - listen for pronunciation and fluency
5. **Use in context** - apply patterns in real conversations

### **Common Mistakes to Avoid:**

1. **Present Perfect vs. Past Simple** - use present perfect for current relevance
2. **Future tense consistency** - don't mix "will" and "going to" unnecessarily
3. **Conditional structure** - remember "if" + present simple, "will" + base verb
4. **Passive voice** - use "be" + past participle
5. **Comparative forms** - use "more/less" with long adjectives

### **Progression Tips:**

1. **Start with basic patterns** - master present perfect and future tense
2. **Add complexity gradually** - practice conditionals and passive voice
3. **Focus on accuracy first** - get the grammar right before speed
4. **Build fluency** - practice speaking naturally and confidently
5. **Apply in real situations** - use patterns in actual conversations

Remember, the goal is to communicate clearly and professionally. These grammar patterns will help you sound more confident and effective in office conversations and project reporting!

# Week 05 - Speaking Scenarios & Practice (B1 Level)

## Scenario 1: Design Review Meeting

### Context
You're presenting your orchestration design to a team of senior engineers who are questioning the complexity and reliability of your approach.

### Your Role
Senior Data Engineer defending your Airflow/Dagster design choices

### Key Topics to Cover
1. **Idempotency**: How you ensure tasks are safe to retry
2. **Retry Strategy**: Your approach to handling failures
3. **Observability**: How you monitor and debug the system
4. **Maintainability**: How the team will maintain this complexity

### Sample Opening
"Thank you for the opportunity to present our orchestration design. Let me begin by discussing our core principles: idempotency, observability, and maintainability. I'd like to start with how we've addressed the reliability concerns you've raised."

### Key Phrases to Use
- "We chose this approach because..."
- "The trade-off we're making is..."
- "Our experience shows that..."
- "Let me explain how we mitigate that risk..."

### Practice Time: 6-8 minutes (B1 Level)

## Scenario 2: Stakeholder Update

### Context
You're updating business stakeholders on the progress of your orchestration implementation and its impact on data delivery.

### Your Role
Data Engineering Lead providing business impact updates

### Key Topics to Cover
1. **Progress Update**: Current implementation status
2. **Business Impact**: How this affects data delivery
3. **Risk Mitigation**: How you're handling potential issues
4. **Next Steps**: Timeline and upcoming milestones

### Sample Opening
"Let me begin by discussing our implementation progress. I'd like to start with the reliability improvements we've achieved. Moving on to business impact, we've seen significant improvements in data delivery times."

### Key Phrases to Use
- "The team reports that..."
- "Our metrics show that..."
- "What this means for the business is..."
- "To summarize, we've achieved..."

### Practice Time: 5-7 minutes (B1 Level)

## Scenario 3: Technical Deep Dive

### Context
You're explaining the technical details of your orchestration system to other engineers who need to understand and potentially maintain it.

### Your Role
Technical Lead providing detailed technical explanation

### Key Topics to Cover
1. **Architecture Overview**: High-level system design
2. **Implementation Details**: Specific technical choices
3. **Operational Procedures**: How to monitor and maintain
4. **Troubleshooting**: Common issues and solutions

### Sample Opening
"The first point I want to address is our overall architecture. Moving on to implementation details, I'll explain our key design decisions. Another important aspect is our operational procedures."

### Key Phrases to Use
- "The system is designed to..."
- "Our implementation ensures that..."
- "When issues occur, the procedure is..."
- "The key takeaway is that..."

### Practice Time: 10-12 minutes

---

## Scenario 4: Problem-Solution Presentation

### Context
You're presenting a problem you solved with your orchestration design, explaining the issue, your solution, and the results.

### Your Role
Problem-solver presenting your solution

### Key Topics to Cover
1. **Problem Description**: What was the issue?
2. **Solution Design**: How did you solve it?
3. **Implementation**: What did you build?
4. **Results**: What were the outcomes?

### Sample Opening
"Let me begin by discussing the problem we faced. Moving on to our solution, I'll explain our design approach. Another important aspect is the implementation details. To summarize, here are the results we achieved."

### Key Phrases to Use
- "The problem we faced was..."
- "Our solution addresses this by..."
- "The implementation includes..."
- "The results demonstrate that..."

### Practice Time: 7-9 minutes

---

## Scenario 5: Team Training Session

### Context
You're training new team members on how to work with your orchestration system.

### Your Role

### Key Phrases to Use
- "The system works by..."
- "When you need to..."
- "The best practice is to..."

## Practice Guidelines

### Preparation Tips
1. **Know Your Material**: Understand the technical concepts thoroughly
2. **Prepare Examples**: Have specific examples ready for each point
3. **Anticipate Questions**: Think about what questions might be asked
4. **Practice Timing**: Ensure you can cover all topics within the time limit

### Speaking Tips
1. **Start Strong**: Begin with a clear introduction
2. **Use Transitions**: Move smoothly between topics
3. **Provide Evidence**: Support your points with specific examples
4. **End Clearly**: Summarize your key points

### Grammar Integration
1. **Use Relative Clauses**: Define technical concepts clearly
2. **Apply Reported Speech**: Share team input and feedback
3. **Include Conditionals**: Discuss hypothetical scenarios
4. **Employ Cleft Sentences**: Emphasize important points

### Common Challenges
1. **Technical Complexity**: Break down complex concepts
2. **Time Management**: Stay within time limits
3. **Audience Engagement**: Keep listeners interested
4. **Question Handling**: Address questions confidently

## Recording Practice

### Setup
1. **Record Yourself**: Use your phone or computer to record
2. **Choose a Scenario**: Pick one of the scenarios above
3. **Time Yourself**: Stay within the specified time limit
4. **Review Recording**: Listen back and assess your performance

### Review Questions
1. **Clarity**: Could you understand what you were saying?
2. **Grammar**: Did you use advanced patterns correctly?
3. **Flow**: Did the presentation flow logically?
4. **Confidence**: Did you sound confident and knowledgeable?

### Improvement Areas
1. **Pronunciation**: Focus on technical terms
2. **Pace**: Adjust speed for clarity
3. **Structure**: Improve organization
4. **Examples**: Add more specific details

## Partner Practice

### Find a Study Partner
1. **Colleague**: Work with a colleague who's also learning
2. **Friend**: Practice with a friend who can provide feedback
3. **Online**: Use language exchange platforms
4. **AI**: Practice with AI conversation partners

### Practice Structure
1. **Role Assignment**: One person presents, the other listens
2. **Feedback**: Provide constructive feedback
3. **Switch Roles**: Take turns presenting
4. **Discussion**: Discuss what went well and what to improve

### Feedback Focus
1. **Content**: Was the information clear and accurate?
2. **Grammar**: Were advanced patterns used correctly?
3. **Delivery**: Was the presentation engaging?
4. **Timing**: Was the pace appropriate?

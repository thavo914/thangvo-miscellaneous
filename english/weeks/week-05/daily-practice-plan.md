# Week 05 - Daily Practice Plan

## Overview (B1 Level, Speaking-First)
This daily practice plan provides structured activities for each day of Week 05, focusing on orchestration design vocabulary, B1-appropriate grammar patterns, and extensive speaking practice (60%+ of time).

---

## Monday: Input + Shadowing; Chunks to SRS

### Duration: 45 minutes

#### Morning Session (20 minutes)
**Activity**: Vocabulary Input and Shadowing
- **Read**: Orchestration design articles or documentation
- **Shadow**: Listen to technical podcasts or videos
- **Focus**: Technical vocabulary and pronunciation
- **Target**: 8-10 new terms

#### Afternoon Session (25 minutes)
**Activity**: Chunks to SRS (Spaced Repetition System)
- **Review**: Previous week's vocabulary
- **Add**: New orchestration terms to SRS
- **Practice**: Pronunciation of technical terms
- **Target**: 15-20 vocabulary items

### Key Vocabulary to Focus On (B1 Level)
- **DAG** - workflow definition
- **Idempotent** - safe to retry
- **Retry mechanism** - automatic failure recovery
- **SLA monitoring** - performance tracking
- **Task dependencies** - execution order
- **Circuit breaker** - failure prevention
- **Observability** - system visibility
- **Resource utilization** - efficiency

### Success Criteria
- [ ] Added 8-10 new terms to vocabulary
- [ ] Practiced pronunciation of technical terms
- [ ] Reviewed previous week's material
- [ ] Set up SRS for new vocabulary

## Tuesday: PREP - Why This Orchestration Design

### Duration: 30 minutes

#### Preparation (10 minutes)
**Activity**: Structure Your Explanation
- **Point**: What is your main argument?
- **Reason**: Why did you choose this approach?
- **Example**: What specific example supports your choice?
- **Point**: Restate your main point

#### Practice (20 minutes)
**Activity**: Explain Your Design Choice
- **Topic**: Why you chose your orchestration approach
- **Format**: 3-4 minute explanation
- **Focus**: Use relative clauses and reported speech
- **Record**: Practice and record yourself

### Sample Structure
1. **Point**: "We chose Dagster for our orchestration needs."
2. **Reason**: "The reason is that Dagster provides better data lineage and observability."
3. **Example**: "For example, when a task fails, we can immediately see which data assets are affected."
4. **Point**: "Therefore, Dagster is the right choice for our team."

### Grammar Focus (B1 Level)
- **Relative clauses**: "Dagster, which provides data lineage, is ideal for our use case."
- **Reported speech**: "The team says that Dagster's abstractions are more intuitive."
- **Simple conditionals**: "If we use proper retry logic, we'll have fewer failures."

### Success Criteria
- [ ] Structured explanation using PREP method
- [ ] Used relative clauses correctly
- [ ] Applied reported speech appropriately
- [ ] Recorded and reviewed performance

## Wednesday: Role-Play - Defend Sensors vs Polling; Retries/Backoff

### Duration: 35 minutes

#### Preparation (10 minutes)
**Activity**: Review Role-Play Scenario
- **Read**: Role-play prompt from prompts/role-play.md
- **Prepare**: Key arguments for your position
- **Practice**: Opening and closing statements
- **Focus**: Technical justification

#### Practice (25 minutes)
**Activity**: Role-Play Defense
- **Partner**: Work with study partner or AI
- **Scenario**: Defend your orchestration design choices
- **Topics**: Sensors vs polling, retry strategies, backoff
- **Duration**: 6-8 minutes
- **Record**: Practice and record the conversation

### Key Arguments to Prepare
1. **Sensors vs Polling**: Why sensors are more efficient
2. **Retry Strategy**: Why exponential backoff is better

4. **Reliability**: How your design prevents failures

### Grammar Focus
- **Mixed conditionals**: "If we had implemented sensors earlier, we would have better efficiency now."
- **Unless/As long as**: "Unless we use proper backoff, we'll have retry storms."

## Thursday: Writing-to-Speaking - Runbook Excerpt → 2-Min Talk

### Duration: 40 minutes

#### Writing (15 minutes)
**Activity**: Write Runbook Excerpt
- **Topic**: Troubleshooting common orchestration issues
- **Format**: Step-by-step procedure
- **Length**: 200-300 words
- **Focus**: Clear, actionable instructions

#### Speaking (25 minutes)
**Activity**: Convert to 2-Minute Talk
- **Read**: Your written runbook excerpt
- **Convert**: Transform into spoken presentation
- **Practice**: Deliver as 2-minute talk
- **Record**: Practice and record yourself

### Sample Runbook Topic
**Troubleshooting Task Failures**
1. Check task logs for error messages
2. Verify task dependencies are met
3. Review resource availability
4. Check for data quality issues
5. Implement retry if appropriate

### Grammar Focus
- **Cleft sentences**: "It is the task logs that provide the most information."
- **Inversion**: "Only when we check the logs do we understand the failure."

### Success Criteria
- [ ] Wrote clear runbook excerpt
- [ ] Converted to spoken presentation
- [ ] Delivered 2-minute talk
- [ ] Used advanced grammar patterns

## Tips for Success

### Daily Practice
1. **Consistency**: Practice daily, even if briefly
2. **Quality**: Focus on accuracy over speed
3. **Recording**: Record yourself regularly
4. **Review**: Listen back and assess performance

### Grammar Integration
1. **Natural Use**: Integrate patterns naturally
2. **Context**: Use appropriate patterns for context
3. **Practice**: Repeat patterns until automatic
4. **Variety**: Use different patterns for variety

### Speaking Confidence
1. **Preparation**: Know your material well
2. **Practice**: Practice until comfortable
3. **Recording**: Record and review regularly
4. **Feedback**: Seek feedback from others

### Vocabulary Mastery
1. **Context**: Learn terms in context
2. **Usage**: Practice using terms in sentences
3. **Pronunciation**: Focus on correct pronunciation
4. **Retention**: Use SRS for long-term retention

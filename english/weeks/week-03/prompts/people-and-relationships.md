# Full practice conversation (B1) — People and Relationships at Work

Direction: Read aloud in a warm, welcoming tone. 

Speaker 2: Hi! How are you getting along with the new team members?
Speaker 1: Pretty well. Daniel from SAS is very helpful, and the business analysts are patient with my questions.
Speaker 2: Any conflicts or difficult situations?
Speaker 1: Not really. Sometimes people have different opinions about priorities, but we talk it out.
Speaker 2: How do you handle disagreements with stakeholders?
Speaker 1: I listen first, then explain my technical perspective. If we still disagree, I suggest a compromise or escalation.
Speaker 2: What about building trust with new team members?
Speaker 1: I show them I'm reliable by delivering on time and being honest about challenges. I also ask for their input.
Speaker 2: How do you manage expectations with different personality types?
Speaker 1: I adapt my communication style. Some people want details, others prefer high-level summaries. I ask what they prefer.
Speaker 2: Any tips for working with difficult people?
Speaker 1: Stay professional, focus on the work, and don't take things personally. Sometimes people are stressed about deadlines.
Speaker 2: How do you give feedback to colleagues?
Speaker 1: I'm specific about what went well and what could improve. I give examples and suggest solutions.
Speaker 2: What about receiving feedback?
Speaker 1: I listen without defending, ask questions to understand, and thank them for helping me improve.
Speaker 2: How do you build relationships across departments?
Speaker 1: I attend their meetings when invited, learn their language, and show how data can help their goals.
Speaker 2: Any advice for new team members joining?
Speaker 1: Ask questions, shadow experienced people, and don't be afraid to make mistakes while learning.

## Key vocabulary for people and relationships

### Communication
- **Stakeholder**: person with interest in project outcome
- **Escalation**: bringing issue to higher authority
- **Compromise**: finding middle ground between different views
- **Feedback**: constructive criticism to help improvement

### Team dynamics
- **Trust building**: developing confidence in others' reliability
- **Conflict resolution**: solving disagreements professionally
- **Personality types**: different ways people prefer to work
- **Cross-functional**: working across different departments

### Professional behavior
- **Reliable**: consistently delivering on promises
- **Transparent**: being open and honest about challenges
- **Professional**: maintaining appropriate workplace behavior
- **Constructive**: helpful and positive in feedback

## Monologue for Shadowing Practice

### Building Effective Workplace Relationships (2-3 minutes)

Building effective workplace relationships is crucial for any data engineer's success. When I first joined the team, I quickly learned that technical skills alone aren't enough. You need to understand how people work, what motivates them, and how to communicate effectively with different personality types.

The key is starting with trust. I show my colleagues I'm reliable by delivering on my promises and being honest about challenges. If I can't meet a deadline, I communicate early and explain why. This transparency builds credibility over time.

Another important aspect is adapting your communication style. Some stakeholders want detailed technical explanations, while others prefer high-level summaries focused on business impact. I always ask what format works best for them rather than assuming.

When conflicts arise, I focus on the work, not personalities. People often disagree because they have different priorities or constraints. By listening first and understanding their perspective, I can usually find a compromise that works for everyone.

Cross-department relationships take time to develop. I attend their meetings when invited, learn their terminology, and show how data can help achieve their goals. This helps bridge the gap between technical and business teams.

Finally, feedback is a two-way street. I give specific, constructive feedback with examples, and I receive feedback with an open mind. Every piece of feedback is an opportunity to improve, both personally and professionally.

## 📅 1. IELTS Practice Questions

### Part 1 (Introduction & Interview)
- *Do you make decisions quickly or do you take your time?*
1. Well, it depends on the situantion. For my work, like building a data pipeline, I usually take my time, but for daily things like what to eat, I decide quickly.
2. I definitely take my time when making decisions, because of my job, I need to make sure data is accurate and reliable. Sometimes, I even sleep on it before making a final decision.
3. I usually make decisions quickly, because I don't like thinking too much about details. I just go with my gut feeling.
- *What is the most difficult decision you have had to make recently?
1. Recently, I had to choose the best way to build a real-time data system for the company. I was looking at tools like Kafka and Spark Streaming, and it was a tough decision because I had to build it all by myself from scratch.
2. The hardest decision lately was committing to my marathon training. I was alway busy with work, so I wasn't sure if I could keep up with the training schedule. Also, I want to achieve sub 4 hours 30 mins for a full marathon, that requires a lot of time for training.
3. I recently had to choose between staying in dormitory or renting an apartment. If I stayed in dormitory, it would save me a lot of money, but I would have to share a room with 3 other people. On the other hand, renting an apartment would give me more privacy, but it would be much more expensive.
- *Do you think it's better to ask friends or family for advice when making a big decision?*
1. I think both friends and family can be helpful when making decisions. They all care about you, and they have different perspectives so you can consider different angles when making decisions. 
2. For me, I think it's important to listen to different perspectives, but ultimately, you should make the decision that you think is best for you.
3. I believe that friends have more similar experiences with you, so they can give you more relevant advice. On the other hand, family have more life experience so they can give you more general advice. 

### Part 2 (Cue Card)
```
Describe a time when you made a difficult decision that had a good result.
You should say:
- What the decision was
- When and why you made it
- What you did to make the choice
And explain how you felt about the good result.
```
1. I would like to talk about a difficult decision I had to make at work recently. It was about choosing the data streaming tech for our company's new real-time data system. 
 I made this decision 3 months ago. At that time, I was the only data engineer in the company, and I was building our data warehouse completely from scratch. We needed to stream data in real-time from various sources, including our casino management system and website, and I had to create a 4-week plan to research and build a POC(Proof of concept) for the data streaming platform. 
 At that time, I was overwhelmed with work, and I wasn't sure if I could handle this task on my own. The company didn't want to build a cloud based data pipeline and want to build an on-premise data pipeline instead. It was a bit of a challenge for me to be honest.
 To make the decision, I spent a lot of time testing different tools such as Kafka and Spark to see how well they handle our casino data. I looked at the documentation of the tools, compared their features, and finally decided to use Kafka.
 In the end, the decision turned out to be a good one, our data pipeline runs smoothly and we can now get real-time data from various sources. Building a complex system by myself without any help was a challenge, but I'm glad I made the right decision. I got promoted to Senior Data Engineer because of this project.
 
### Part 3 (Analytical Discussion)
- *Do you think young people take more risks than older people?*
1. Yes, I think so. Young people usually don't have families to feed or a mortgage to pay for yet. Because they have fewer responsibilities, they are more willing to take risks in their careers or personal lives.
2. Definitely. Young people are curious and adventurous, so they are more willing to try new things and take risks. Older people, on the other hand, already have a lot of life experience, so they are usually prefer stability and safety over taking a chance.
3. I agree with that. Young people have less experience and fewer responsibilities, and taking risks can help them learn and grow faster. However, they should also consider the potential consequences of their actions and make informed decisions. 

- *Should business managers rely more on facts or their own feelings when making choices?*
1. I believe that business managers should rely on facts or data more than their own feelings when making decisions. In business, feelings can be biased, subjective and lead to wrong decisions but data does not lie. For example, if a company wants to launch a new product, the should look at the market research data to make a decision, not just their own feelings.
2. I think the best managers need to combine both facts and feelings when making decisions. Facts and data can provide objective information while feelings can provide insights when data is not available.

## 🗣️ 2. ChatGPT Voice Mode Simulation Prompt

Copy and paste this prompt into ChatGPT Voice Mode for a natural, friendly practice chat:

```text
Act as Sarah, a friendly Marketing Manager at a casino resort. I am Alex, a Data Engineer on your team. 

We are having a quick chat in the office kitchen. I want to suggest that we start using a real-time data system instead of waiting for the daily batch reports. 

You are a bit worried because:
1. "The current daily reports work fine, why change it?"
2. "We don't have a big budget right now."
3. "I don't really understand how real-time data works. Can you explain it simply?"

CRITICAL INSTRUCTIONS FOR SARAH:
- Talk in a casual, warm, and conversational office tone. Do not make mid-chat corrections.
- Listen to see if I use these simple, real-life phrases and grammar:
  - Phrases: "make a choice", "deal with the risks", "plays a big part in", "weigh the options", "take a risk".
  - Grammar: Third Conditional ("If we had...", "we wouldn't have..."), Passive voice ("is sent", "are checked"), and Present Perfect Continuous ("have been working").
- After 3-4 quick exchanges, break character. Give me 2 friendly tips on how to make my speaking sound more natural and comfortable.

Start the chat by saying: "Hey Alex! Thanks for grabbing a coffee with me. I saw your message about updating our database reports. Can you tell me why you think we need this?"
```

## 📖 1. Grammar Focus

### 1. Relative Clauses (Defining and Non-Defining)
* **Quote**: *"The people **who are most joyful** understand that the journey is the destination."*
* **Rule Explanation**: 
  * **Defining relative clauses** (with *who*, *which*, *that*) give essential information about a noun. You cannot remove them without changing the meaning of the sentence. (No commas). 
  * **Non-defining relative clauses** give extra, non-essential information. (Use commas).
* **Common Pitfall**: 
  > [!WARNING]
  > Using *that* in a non-defining relative clause (with commas).
  > **Incorrect**: My manager, *that* is very experienced, told me to focus on the process.
  > **Correct**: My manager, *who* is very experienced, told me to focus on the process.

### 2. Reflexive Pronouns for Emphasis (yourself, myself, themselves)
* **Quote**: *"We just have to remind **ourselves** that the journey is the destination."*
* **Rule Explanation**: Reflexive pronouns (*myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves*) are used when the subject and the object of the verb are the same person. They can also be used for emphasis to show that someone did something independently.
* **Common Pitfall**: 
  > [!WARNING]
  > Using reflexive pronouns instead of object pronouns after prepositions of place.
  > **Incorrect**: She put the laptop next to *herself*.
  > **Correct**: She put the laptop next to *her*.

### 3. Double Comparatives (The more..., the more...)
* **Quote**: *"**The more** you step out of your comfort zone, **the easier** it gets."*
* **Rule Explanation**: We use double comparatives (`The + comparative ..., the + comparative ...`) to show cause and effect, meaning that one change causes another change to happen simultaneously.
* **Common Pitfall**: 
  > [!WARNING]
  > Forgetting the article "the" before the comparative adjectives.
  > **Incorrect**: *More* I practice, *better* I become.
  > **Correct**: *The more* I practice, *the better* I become.

## 🏗️ 2. Sentence Structure Templates

### Template 1: Clarifying True Desires
* **Communicative Function**: To clarify what you really want versus what you thought you wanted.
* **Formula**: `What I really wanted wasn't [Noun Phrase 1], but [Noun Phrase 2].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: What I really wanted wasn't a bigger house, but a closer community.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: What I really wanted wasn't a more complex pipeline, but a more reliable one.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: What I really wanted wasn't the promotion, but the feeling of being challenged.

### Template 2: Expressing Surprising Outcomes
* **Communicative Function**: To emphasize the positive, unexpected results of taking an action.
* **Formula**: `It's amazing what can happen when you [Verb Phrase].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: It's amazing what can happen when you let go of your fear of failure.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: It's amazing what can happen when you automate those repetitive daily tasks.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: It's amazing what can happen when you simply ask for help.

### Template 3: Contrasting Past and Present Beliefs
* **Communicative Function**: To explain how your mindset or perspective has changed over time.
* **Formula**: `I used to think that [Clause 1], but now I realize [Clause 2].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: I used to think that success meant being rich, but now I realize it's about peace of mind.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: I used to think that batch processing was always better, but now I realize streaming has its place.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: I used to think that working long hours meant you were dedicated, but now I realize it just leads to burnout.

### Template 4: Defining the True Value
* **Communicative Function**: To shift focus from the end result to the journey.
* **Formula**: `The joy isn't in [Verb-ing/Noun], it's the process of [Verb-ing].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: The joy isn't in reaching the top of the mountain, it's the process of climbing it.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: The joy isn't in deploying the final code, it's the process of solving the architecture puzzle.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: The joy isn't in getting the quarterly bonus, it's the process of building a great product.

### Template 5: Warning About Stagnation
* **Communicative Function**: To express the binary nature of personal or professional development.
* **Formula**: `If you're not [Verb-ing], you are [Verb-ing].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: If you're not growing and learning, you are essentially shrinking.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: If you're not updating your skills with new cloud tools, you are falling behind.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: If you're not moving forward, you are moving backward.

## 🕵️ 4. Spot the Mistake

Identify the error in each sentence and rewrite it correctly.

1. **Incorrect**: The more you step out of your comfort zone, easier it gets to handle stress.
   - **Correction**: ______________________________________________________________
2. **Incorrect**: My colleague, that sits next to me, taught me how to keep things in perspective.
   - **Correction**: ______________________________________________________________
3. **Incorrect**: We decided to review the entire data architecture by themself.
   - **Correction**: ______________________________________________________________

<details>
<summary><b>Answer Key & Explanations</b></summary>

1. **Correction**: The more you step out of your comfort zone, **the easier** it gets to handle stress. *(Explanation: Double comparatives always require the article "the" before both comparative adjectives).*
2. **Correction**: My colleague, **who** sits next to me, taught me how to keep things in perspective. *(Explanation: In a non-defining relative clause set off by commas, you must use 'who' for people, not 'that').*
3. **Correction**: We decided to review the entire data architecture by **ourselves**. *(Explanation: The subject is 'We', so the reflexive pronoun must be 'ourselves').*
</details>

# Day 4: Grammar & Structures (Restoring Stakeholder Trust)

---

## 📖 1. Grammar Focus

### 1. Passive Voice in Present Perfect (`has/have been + P.P.`)
* **Quote**: *"Rest assured, the missing records **have been backfilled** successfully."*
* **Rule Explanation**: Use the Present Perfect Passive to focus on the *result* of an action that was completed recently, without focusing on who did it. It's highly professional and reassuring in a business context.
* **Common Pitfall**: 
  > [!WARNING]
  > Forgetting the verb "been" in the passive structure.
  > **Incorrect**: The dashboard issue *has resolved* by our team.
  > **Correct**: The dashboard issue *has been resolved* by our team.

### 2. Cleft Sentences for Emphasis (`What... was...`)
* **Quote**: *"**What caused the discrepancy was** late-arriving data from the mobile app."*
* **Rule Explanation**: Cleft sentences allow you to emphasize a specific part of a sentence. By starting with "What" (meaning "The thing that"), you focus the listener's attention directly on the root cause or the solution.
* **Common Pitfall**: 
  > [!WARNING]
  > Omitting the main verb "is/was" after the "What" clause.
  > **Incorrect**: What we found late-arriving data.
  > **Correct**: What we found *was* late-arriving data.

### 3. Future Continuous for Plans (`will be + V-ing`)
* **Quote**: *"We **will be monitoring** the pipeline closely over the weekend."*
* **Rule Explanation**: Use the Future Continuous to talk about actions that will be in progress at a certain time in the future, or to politely state a planned, routine action without sounding like a direct order or promise.
* **Common Pitfall**: 
  > [!WARNING]
  > Using the present continuous instead of future continuous for a planned action that hasn't started yet.
  > **Incorrect**: We *are monitoring* it tomorrow. (Acceptable informally, but less professional).
  > **Correct**: We *will be monitoring* it tomorrow.

## 🏗️ 2. Sentence Structure Templates

### Template 1: Empathizing with Stakeholders
* **Communicative Function**: To validate a stakeholder's worry before explaining the issue.
* **Formula**: `I totally understand your concern about [Noun/Verb-ing].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: I totally understand your concern about the changing schedule.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: I totally understand your concern about the drop in active users on the dashboard.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: I totally understand your concern about missing the project deadline.

### Template 2: Explaining the Root Cause
* **Communicative Function**: To clearly state the technical reason for a problem using emphasis.
* **Formula**: `What caused the discrepancy was [Noun Phrase / Clause].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: What caused the delay was unexpected traffic on the highway.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: What caused the discrepancy was an upstream schema change.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: What caused the misunderstanding was a lack of clear communication.

### Template 3: Stating Current Actions
* **Communicative Function**: To reassure stakeholders that work is actively happening.
* **Formula**: `We are currently in the process of [Verb-ing] to ensure [Clause].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: We are currently in the process of reviewing the contract to ensure everything is correct.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: We are currently in the process of backfilling the data to ensure the metrics are accurate.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: We are currently in the process of hiring more staff to ensure we meet our goals.

### Template 4: Providing Reassurance and Timelines
* **Communicative Function**: To calm nerves by giving a firm deadline for resolution.
* **Formula**: `Rest assured, we will have [Noun Phrase] resolved by [Time].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: Rest assured, we will have the venue booked by tomorrow morning.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: Rest assured, we will have the pipeline issues resolved by 3 PM today.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: Rest assured, we will have the client's complaint resolved by the end of the week.

### Template 5: Outlining Preventive Measures
* **Communicative Function**: To explain how you will stop the problem from happening again.
* **Formula**: `Going forward, we'll implement [Noun Phrase] so that [Clause].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: Going forward, we'll implement a shared calendar so that everyone knows the schedule.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: Going forward, we'll implement automated data quality checks so that we catch anomalies earlier.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: Going forward, we'll implement a weekly sync meeting so that everyone stays aligned.

## 🕵️ 4. Spot the Mistake

Identify the error in each sentence and rewrite it correctly.

1. **Incorrect**: The missing data have recovered by the data engineering team.
   - **Correction**: ______________________________________________________________
2. **Incorrect**: What we found the root cause was a broken API.
   - **Correction**: ______________________________________________________________
3. **Incorrect**: We will monitoring the servers closely tonight to ensure stability.
   - **Correction**: ______________________________________________________________

<details>
<summary><b>Answer Key & Explanations</b></summary>

1. **Correction**: The missing data **has been** recovered by the data engineering team. *(Explanation: Data is generally treated as singular/uncountable in this context, so use "has". Also, it must be passive: "has been recovered").*
2. **Correction**: What we found **was that** the root cause was a broken API. (Or: **What caused the issue was** a broken API). *(Explanation: You need a linking verb "was" after the "What" clause to connect it to the noun phrase).*
3. **Correction**: We will **be** monitoring the servers closely tonight to ensure stability. *(Explanation: Future Continuous requires the auxiliary verb "be": will + be + V-ing).*
</details>

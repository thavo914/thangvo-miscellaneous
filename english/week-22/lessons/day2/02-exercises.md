# ✍️ Day 2 - Exercises: Contingencies & Policy Exceptions

> [!NOTE]
> **Output Engine Focus**: Train spoken reflex frameworks (**5W1H** and **O-R-E-O**) to negotiate workplace exceptions and handle unexpected schedule changes with diplomatic authority.

---

## 🎙️ Block 1: Warm-up Reflex & Shadowing (20–25 Mins)

### Part A: Speaking Reflex — The 60-Second Random Object Pitch (15 Mins)
* **Objective**: Pick an object near your desk (e.g., your badge, phone charger, water bottle). Pretend you must explain an exception regarding this item to a security guard or building manager. Speak continuously for 60 seconds without long pauses.
* **Target expressions to weave in**:
  * `extenuating circumstance`
  * `just this once`
  * `if anything comes up between now and then`
* **Sample Pitch**:
  > *"I realize I don't have my physical security badge on me today because I left my wallet in a taxi. I know you can't normally issue temporary passes without photo ID, but since this is an **extenuating circumstance**, could you allow me in **just this once**? I have an urgent client call at 9:00 AM..."*

### Part B: Interactive Shadowing & Pacing (10 Mins)
Shadow these key lines with breath pauses (`/`) and stress emphasis:
1. *"We can't NORMALLY do this on such short notice, / but since this is an EXTENUATING circumstance, / I can allow it JUST THIS ONCE."*
2. *"If ANYTHING comes up between now and then, / just ping me on Slack / and we will WORK AROUND it."*

## 🗣️ Block 2: Spoken Response Frameworks (30–35 Mins)

### 1. General Interview / IELTS Speaking Part 1 (5W1H Framework)
* **Question**: *"Have you ever had to ask for an exception to a company rule or deadline?"*
* **Scaffold (Direct Answer + 2 Details)**:
  - **Direct Answer**: Yes, I once had to ask our cloud operations team for an emergency deployment exception.
  - **Supporting Detail 1 (What happened & Why)**: It happened last quarter when an unexpected schema change broke our reporting pipeline right before month-end closing.
  - **Supporting Detail 2 (How it resolved)**: Since it was an extenuating circumstance, our lead approved the hotfix just that once, and the pipeline was back up within an hour.
* **Your Turn**: Record or speak your answer aloud following this 3-part expansion.

### 2. Cue Card Narrative / Part 2 (5W1H Checklist - 1.5 to 2 Mins)
* **Prompt**: Describe an occasion when a service or delivery was delayed and someone helped you resolve it.
* **1-Minute Scratchpad**:
  - **Who**: Customer support coordinator named Alex.
  - **What**: Urgent delivery of replacement server cables.
  - **Where & When**: At our downtown tech office last month.
  - **Why**: Upstream warehouse was out of stock.
  - **How**: Alex put a special note on the ticket and expedited courier dispatch just this once.
* **Delivery Practice**: Speak aloud continuously for 90 seconds using your notes.

### 3. Analytical Discussion / Part 3 (O-R-E-O Framework)
* **Question**: *"Do you think organizations should be strictly rule-bound or should managers have the freedom to make exceptions?"*
* **Scaffold**:
  - **O (Opinion)**: In my view, while clear rules are necessary, managers must be given the flexibility to make occasional exceptions.
  - **R (Reason)**: This is because unexpected bottlenecks and extenuating circumstances are simply a reality in modern business.
  - **E (Example)**: For instance, if an engineering team strictly adheres to a 48-hour approval policy during a critical production outage, the company could lose thousands of dollars.
  - **O (Outcome)**: Therefore, allowing calculated exceptions with proper documentation produces much better business results.
* **Your Turn**: Deliver this argument aloud cleanly using the O-R-E-O structure.

### Drill 2: Spot the Pitfall (Error Correction)
Find and correct the error in each sentence:
1. *"My manager told me that was a very good news for our project."*
2. *"If any problem happens between now and that time, give me a call."*
3. *"Since this is an extenuating situation, we can allow it just one time."*

<details>
<summary>💡 Click to View Explanations & Corrections</summary>

1. **Correction**: *"My manager told me that was **very good news** for our project."*  
   *Explanation*: `News` is uncountable, so never use the indefinite article `a`.
2. **Correction**: *"If anything comes up **between now and then**, give me a call."*  
   *Explanation*: Native speakers use the set phrase `between now and then` rather than `between now and that time`.
3. **Correction**: *"Since this is an **extenuating circumstance**, we can allow it **just this once**."*  
   *Explanation*: The standard native collocations are `extenuating circumstance` and `just this once`.
</details>

# 📘 Day 1 - Knowledge: Delivery Tracking & Availability Commitments

> [!NOTE]
> **Input Engine Focus**: Learn how to track deliveries, confirm order details over the phone, and communicate your availability boundaries clearly using Plain English.

---

## 🏢 1. Context & Setting

- **Scenario**: Minh, a data engineer working remotely, ordered a replacement dual-monitor arm and server cables for his home office. The delivery was marked as "pending" for two days. Minh calls customer support to track down the parcel because he has an off-site team lunch at noon.
- **Characters**: 
  - **Minh**: Remote Data Engineer.
  - **Sarah**: Logistics & Dispatch Coordinator.

---

## 🗣️ 2. Realistic Dialogue Transcript

**Minh**: Hi Sarah, good morning. **I was calling to check on an order** I placed earlier this week. We’re **expecting a delivery** of some computer equipment today, but the tracking status hasn't updated.  
**Sarah**: Good morning! I can certainly help you look into that. **What is the name the order was placed under**, and do you have the **reference number** handy?  
**Minh**: Yes, it was **placed under Minh Vo**, and the **reference number** is Ref-8842.  
**Sarah**: Thanks, let me pull that up in our system... Okay, I see it here. The courier picked it up from the hub at 8:30 AM. It’s scheduled to arrive **sometime tomorrow afternoon**, but there’s a note saying it might arrive earlier today.  
**Minh**: Ah, I see. The reason I'm asking is that I have a team lunch at 12:30 PM, but **I should be back by 2:00 PM**. Do you think it will come before then?  
**Sarah**: Looking at the courier’s route, he is currently three stops away from your district. He will likely drop it off between 10:30 AM and 11:30 AM.  
**Minh**: That would be great! Earlier this week, the vendor **had trouble finding the monitor arm in stock**, so I was worried the shipment would be held up.  
**Sarah**: Everything looks completely clear now. I’ll **keep an eye out for** any dispatch alerts and text you if anything changes.  
**Minh**: Thanks a lot for checking that so quickly, Sarah. Have a good one!  
**Sarah**: You too, Minh! Bye.

## 🧩 3. Sentence Structure & Formula Deconstruction

### Pattern 1: Polite Inquiry Opening
* **Quote**: *"I was calling to check on an order I placed earlier this week."*
* **Formula**: `I was calling to + [check on / ask about / inquire about] + [Topic / Noun]...`
* **Functional Meaning**: Using the past continuous (*was calling*) softens the opening of a call, making you sound polite, calm, and professional rather than demanding.
* **Everyday Example**: *"I was calling to check on my dinner reservation for tonight."*
* **Tech / Workplace Example**: *"I was calling to ask about the delivery timeline for our server racks."*

### Pattern 2: System Identification Preposition
* **Quote**: *"What is the name the order was placed under?"*
* **Formula**: `The [order / ticket / account] was placed under + [Name / Department]...`
* **Functional Meaning**: In English, reservations, orders, and system records exist *under* someone's name, not *with* or *by*.
* **Everyday Example**: *"I have a table reserved under the name Johnson."*
* **Tech / Workplace Example**: *"The Jira ticket was filed under the Data Platform team."*

### Pattern 3: Availability Boundary with Modal `Should` and `By`
* **Quote**: *"I have a team lunch at 12:30 PM, but I should be back by 2:00 PM."*
* **Formula**: `I [have a commitment], but I should be back / available by + [Specific Time]...`
* **Functional Meaning**: Combines `should` (reasonable expectation) with `by` (no later than) to set a clear, reliable availability boundary.
* **Everyday Example**: *"I'm stepping out for groceries, but I should be back by 5:00 PM."*
* **Tech / Workplace Example**: *"I'm in a client sync right now, but I should be free by 3:30 PM to review your PR."*

### Pattern 4: Explaining Past Roadblocks with Gerunds
* **Quote**: *"The vendor had trouble finding the monitor arm in stock..."*
* **Formula**: `[Subject] + had trouble + [Verb-ing] + [Object] + because + [Reason]...`
* **Functional Meaning**: A natural Plain English way to explain delays without using stiff academic words like *encountered severe difficulties*.
* **Everyday Example**: *"I had trouble finding parking near the clinic this morning."*
* **Tech / Workplace Example**: *"We had trouble connecting to the replica database because the port was blocked."*

## 📖 4. High-Yield Collocations & Phrasing

| Phrase / Collocation | POS | Vietnamese Meaning | Plain English Usage Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `expecting a delivery` | Verb Phrase | đang chờ nhận hàng | **Do not say**: *"I'm expecting for an order"*. `Expect` is transitive: say *"expecting an order"*. |
| `reference number` | Noun Phrase | mã tra cứu / số tham chiếu | Native standard abbreviation is **Ref #**. Avoid the literal Vietlish *"order code"*. |
| `sometime tomorrow afternoon` | Adverbial | chiều mai (thời điểm ước lượng) | `sometime` (một thời điểm nào đó) $\neq$ `sometimes` (thỉnh thoảng) $\neq$ `some time` (một khoảng thời gian dài). |
| `track down [something]` | Phrasal Verb | lần theo dấu vết / tìm ra | Active spoken idiom: *"Let me track down that tracking number for you."* |
| `keep an eye out for` | Idiom | để mắt canh chừng / chờ đón | Friendly, everyday reassurance: *"Keep an eye out for the courier around 11:00 AM."* |
| `in stock` | Prep Phrase | còn hàng trong kho | The opposite is `out of stock` or `on backorder`. |

## 💡 5. Communicative Nuance & Delivery Guide

### 1. `By` vs. `Until` (Deadline Mechanics)
- **`by [Time]`**: Means *at or before* that moment (a deadline or point of completion).
  - *"I will be back by 2:00 PM."* (Tôi sẽ về trước hoặc đúng 2:00).
- **`until [Time]`**: Means an action *continues continuously* up to that point.
  - *"I will be in the meeting until 2:00 PM."* (Tôi họp liên tục đến 2:00).

### 2. Breath-Friendly Rhythm & Connected Speech
When speaking on the phone, link prepositions smoothly:
- *"I was **calling to** check on an **order**..."* $\rightarrow$ `/aɪ wəz ˈkɔːlɪŋ tə tʃɛk ɒn ən ˈɔːdər/`
- Avoid pausing after every word. Group words into natural breath units:  
  `[I was calling to check on an order] / [I placed earlier this week].`

# 🎧 1-Minute Gist-Catching & Verbal Summary: Master Reference & Scoring Guide

> **Target Skill**: Real-Time Listening Comprehension, 3W Gist Extraction & 15-Second Verbal Summaries  
> **Source Material**: 4 Authentic Workplace Dialogue Transcripts (TOEIC Part 3)  
> **Scoring Scale**: 10 Points per Clip (Total: 40 Points)  
> **Passing Benchmark**: 32/40 (80% — Proficient B1–B2 Workplace Reflex)

---

## 🎯 How to Use This Reference for Self-Evaluation

When practicing **1-Minute Gist-Catching & Verbal Summary**:
1. **Listen Once**: Play the audio clip without looking at the transcript.
2. **Instant Verbal Output (The 15-Second Rule)**: The second the audio stops, pause the recording and **speak your summary out loud in English** within 15–20 seconds using the **3W Framework**:
   * **Who**: Who is talking to whom?
   * **What**: What is the root problem, dilemma, or core update?
   * **Action/Next**: What action, compromise, or next step was agreed upon?
3. **Compare & Score**: Review the transcript, 3W Key, and Model Summary below to calculate your score out of 10 points.

### 📊 Universal 10-Point Scoring Rubric

| Component | Max Points | Evaluation Criteria |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1. Core Fact Accuracy (3W)** | **4 Pts** | Captured the core situation, conflict, and final resolution without missing key facts. |
| **2. Speed & Reflex** | **3 Pts** | Began speaking within 3 seconds of the audio ending; finished summary in under 20 seconds. |
| **3. Natural Phrasing & Chunks** | **3 Pts** | Used native phrasal verbs/collocations; avoided word-by-word Vietnamese translation. |

## 📑 Clip 1: The Copier Meltdown & Last-Minute Workaround

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Woman**: *"Excuse me, David. I've been trying to print these reports for the last twenty minutes, but the copier keeps jamming. Do you know what's going on with it?"*
* **Man (David)**: *"Oh, I'm sorry about that. A technician was actually scheduled to come and fix it yesterday, but she called to cancel at the last minute. Something about an emergency at another office."*
* **Woman**: *"That's frustrating. I really need these documents for my two o'clock meeting with the sales team."*
* **Man**: *"Well, there's a working printer on the fourth floor, near the break room. Or, if you'd prefer, I can show you how to send the files directly to their laptops. That way they can view everything on screen."*
* **Woman**: *"The fourth floor sounds easier. I'll head up there now. Thanks for letting me know."*

### 🔑 3W Key Elements
* **Who**: A female employee and her coworker David.
* **What**: The printer keeps jamming right before her 2:00 PM sales meeting because the repair technician cancelled at the last minute.
* **Action/Next**: David gave her two workarounds (a 4th-floor printer or digital file sharing); she chose to head up to the 4th floor.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"A woman was dealing with a jammed copier right before her two o'clock sales meeting because the technician cancelled yesterday at the last minute. Her coworker David suggested either using the working printer on the fourth floor or sharing the files digitally, and she decided to head up to the fourth floor."*

### 📋 Clip 1 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Mentioned the copier malfunction (`keeps jamming` / `broken printer`).
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Mentioned the time pressure (`2 o'clock meeting with sales team`).
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Mentioned the technician cancelled last minute.
* [ ] **Fact 4 (1 pt)**: Stated the resolution (`heading up to the 4th floor`).
* [ ] **Reflex & Flow (3 pts)**: Started speaking immediately without pauses longer than 2 seconds.
* [ ] **Native Chunks (3 pts)**: Used natural phrases: `at the last minute`, `keeps jamming`, `head up there`.

## 📑 Clip 2: The Accounting Candidate & Software Skill Gap

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Woman**: *"I've just finished reviewing the applications for the accounting position. I think Jennifer Marsh is our strongest candidate. She has eight years of experience and excellent references from her previous employer."*
* **Man**: *"I saw her resume too. She's certainly qualified, but I noticed she hasn't worked with our financial reporting software before. That's going to be a problem since we use it for everything here."*
* **Woman**: *"We can offer training. The basics only take a couple of weeks to learn, and someone with her background would pick it up quickly."*
* **Man**: *"That's true. And honestly, finding someone with both the experience and the exact software skills we need has been difficult."*
* **Woman**: *"Exactly. Should we schedule her for an interview next week?"*
* **Man**: *"Yes, let's do that. Can you check her availability for Tuesday or Wednesday?"*

### 🔑 3W Key Elements
* **Who**: Two hiring managers reviewing accounting applicants.
* **What**: Top applicant Jennifer Marsh has 8 years of experience and great references, but lacks experience with their internal financial reporting software.
* **Action/Next**: They agreed she will pick up the software quickly with short training, so they decided to check her availability for an interview next Tuesday or Wednesday.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"Two managers are reviewing candidates for an accounting position and agreed that Jennifer Marsh is their strongest applicant. Although she lacks experience with their internal reporting software, they're confident she can pick it up quickly through training, so they decided to schedule an interview with her for next Tuesday or Wednesday."*

### 📋 Clip 2 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Identified Jennifer Marsh as the strongest applicant for accounting.
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Mentioned her strength (`8 years experience` / `great references`).
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Noted the concern (`lacks experience with their specific software`).
* [ ] **Fact 4 (1 pt)**: Stated the outcome (`offering training` + `scheduling interview for Tuesday/Wednesday`).
* [ ] **Reflex & Flow (3 pts)**: Summary delivered in 15–20 seconds with steady momentum.
* [ ] **Native Chunks (3 pts)**: Used idiomatic chunks: `pick it up quickly`, `check her availability`, `qualified`.

## 📑 Clip 3: Client Reception Budget Cut & Negotiation

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Man A**: *"Alright, let's talk about the client reception next month. We've just received word from management that we need to cut the overall budget by fifteen percent. Any suggestions?"*
* **Woman**: *"Well, the biggest expense right now is the venue. We're paying quite a lot for that downtown hotel. What if we moved it to our own conference center instead? It's free to use and still looks professional."*
* **Man B**: *"I'm not sure about that. The hotel location is more convenient for most of our clients. They're expecting something upscale. I think we should look at the catering instead. We could switch from the sit-down dinner to a cocktail reception with lighter refreshments."*
* **Man A**: *"That's a fair point about client expectations. Let me pull up the catering quotes and see where we can make some adjustments. We might be able to keep the venue and still hit our target."*
* **Woman**: *"That works for me. Just let us know what you find."*

### 🔑 3W Key Elements
* **Who**: Three colleagues (Man A, Woman, Man B) organizing a client reception.
* **What**: Management ordered a 15% budget cut; the woman proposed moving from an expensive downtown hotel to their free conference center, while Man B argued to preserve client impressions by trimming catering instead.
* **Action/Next**: Man A decided to inspect catering quotes first to see if they can reduce food costs while keeping the hotel venue.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"A team needs to cut next month's client reception budget by 15%. While one coworker suggested switching the venue to their in-house conference center, another pushed to keep the upscale hotel and downgrade catering to light refreshments. The lead agreed to review catering quotes first so they can try to hit their budget target without changing venues."*

### 📋 Clip 3 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Identified the 15% budget cut mandate for the client reception.
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Mentioned the venue-swap proposal (`moving to internal conference center`).
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Mentioned the catering-swap counterproposal (`cocktail reception instead of sit-down dinner`).
* [ ] **Fact 4 (1 pt)**: Stated the resolution (`pulling up catering quotes to hit target`).
* [ ] **Reflex & Flow (3 pts)**: Cohesive multi-speaker synthesis delivered smoothly in under 20 seconds.
* [ ] **Native Chunks (3 pts)**: Used phrases: `fair point`, `pull up quotes`, `hit our target`, `upscale`.

## 📑 Clip 4: Client Meeting Travel Coordination & Logistics

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Man**: *"Have you figured out how we're getting to the client meeting on Thursday? It starts at ten, so we can't afford to be late."*
* **Woman**: *"I've been looking at the train schedule. There are a few options, but since travel expenses have been tight lately, I'd rather not take the express service if we don't have to."*
* **Man**: *"That makes sense. As long as we arrive with enough time to spare, I'm fine with the regular train. We should probably get there at least half an hour early to set up the presentation."*
* **Woman**: *"Right. So we need to arrive before nine-thirty at the latest. Let me see... yes, there's one that works perfectly. I'll book the tickets online right now."*
* **Man**: *"Great. And could you arrange for a taxi to pick us up from the station? I don't want to waste time looking for the office building."*

### 🔑 3W Key Elements
* **Who**: Two coworkers planning travel for an off-site client meeting.
* **What**: They have a crucial 10:00 AM meeting and tight travel expenses, so they need to take a cheaper regular train that still gets them there at least 30 minutes early (by 9:30 AM).
* **Action/Next**: The woman booked tickets for a train arriving before 9:30 AM, and the man requested arranging a taxi from the station to avoid getting lost.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"Two coworkers are planning their trip for a 10 AM client meeting on Thursday. Because travel budgets are tight, they opted for a regular train over the express service, aiming to arrive before 9:30 so they have enough time to set up their presentation. The woman booked the train tickets, and the man asked her to arrange a taxi from the station."*

### 📋 Clip 4 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Identified the 10:00 AM client meeting on Thursday.
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Noted the budget constraint (`regular train instead of express`).
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Stated arrival requirement (`before 9:30 at the latest` / `30 mins early to set up`).
* [ ] **Fact 4 (1 pt)**: Stated final tasks (`booking tickets` + `arranging taxi from station`).
* [ ] **Reflex & Flow (3 pts)**: Spoken under 20 seconds with crisp rhythm.
* [ ] **Native Chunks (3 pts)**: Used chunks: `with time to spare`, `can't afford to be late`, `at the latest`, `arrange a taxi`.

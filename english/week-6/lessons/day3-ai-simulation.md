# Day 3 — AI Simulation (Episode Theme)
## Week 6 | Is this the era of distrust?
*Technique: AI Conversation Practice | ~60 minutes total*

> **Rules for all rounds:**
> - Aim for **B1–B2 level** English — clear, structured, not too formal.
> - The AI should **not correct you mid-conversation** — ask for feedback only after each round.
> - Use at least **2–3 vocabulary words** from this week in each round: *trustworthiness, gullible, trust to luck, indiscriminately, competence, reliability.*
> - After each round, ask the AI: *"Please give me feedback on my grammar, vocabulary use, and fluency."*

## Round 1 — Discussion with a Curious Peer (15 min)

**Setup:** The AI plays a curious colleague who has not heard the episode. You summarise the episode and discuss the topic of trust.

### Prompt (paste this into your AI chat):

> "Let's have a 15-minute conversation in English. You are my curious colleague who hasn't heard a BBC podcast episode I just listened to. The episode was called 'Is this the era of distrust?' and it discussed why we are living through a crisis in trust, what trustworthiness means, and how to identify trustworthy people. Ask me questions about the episode, and I'll try to explain the ideas. Use B1–B2 level English. Don't correct me during the conversation — give feedback only at the end."

### Conversation Prompts (if the AI pauses, use one of these):

- "The philosopher Onora O'Neill said that trust and trustworthiness are very different things. Do you find that interesting?"
- "Would you say you're a trustworthy person? Why?"

## Round 2 — Expert Role-play (15 min)

**Setup:** The AI plays philosopher **Onora O'Neill**. You are David Edmonds, the BBC presenter. You have a structured interview about trust in the modern world.

### Prompt (paste this into your AI chat):

> "Let's do a role-play in English. You are philosopher Onora O'Neill, author of a work on trust and trustworthiness. I am David Edmonds, a BBC journalist interviewing you for a programme called 'The Big Idea'. I will ask you questions about your ideas on trust, trustworthiness, gullibility, and how people can identify who to trust. Stay in character and give detailed, thoughtful answers. Use B1–B2 level English. Don't correct me during the conversation — give feedback only at the end."

### Interview Questions to Ask:

1. "Professor O'Neill, many people say we're in a crisis of trust. Do you agree?"
2. "You distinguish between trust and trustworthiness. Can you explain the difference simply?"
3. "You mentioned three ingredients for trustworthiness — honesty, competence, and reliability. Which one do you think is most important and why?"
4. "In the age of social media, do you think people have become more gullible? Are they trusting things too indiscriminately?"

## Round 3 — Devil's Advocate: Is Trust Overrated? (20 min)

**Setup:** The AI argues that people are *right* to be suspicious and distrustful. You argue the opposite — that trust is essential for society to function. Debate the following position:

> **"In the modern world, distrust is a rational and healthy response. Trusting people is naïve."**

### Prompt (paste this into your AI chat):

> "Let's have a structured debate in English. You will argue that distrust is a rational and healthy response in the modern world, and that trusting people is naïve. I will argue the opposite — that trust is essential and worthwhile even if it sometimes means being deceived. We each get 2–3 turns to make our case. Use B1–B2 level English and feel free to use examples from politics, technology, or social media. Don't correct me during the debate — give feedback at the end."

### Key Arguments to Prepare (use these if you get stuck):

- *"Without trust, society would grind to a halt — we'd have to verify every interaction."*
- *"Being lied to once doesn't mean everyone is untrustworthy — it means we should judge more carefully, not stop trusting entirely."*
- *"Philosopher Onora O'Neill's three criteria give us a method — trustworthiness can be assessed, not just assumed."*

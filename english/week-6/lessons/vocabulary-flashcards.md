## Card 1

**FRONT**
> *"The quality of being able to be trusted as honest and reliable. It has three ingredients: honesty, competence, and consistency over time."*
>
> What word is this?

---

**BACK**

**trustworthiness** /ˌtrʌstˈwɜːðinəs/ | *noun*

**Definition:** The quality of being able to be trusted as honest and reliable.

**Episode quote:** *"We'll be hearing from a philosopher who believes the problem is not about trust itself but about trustworthiness."*

**Personal sentence:** *"A data pipeline's trustworthiness is measured not just by one successful run, but by its consistent, accurate delivery over months."*

---

## Card 2

**FRONT**
> *"This adjective describes a person who is easy to deceive because they believe what others say without questioning or judging whether it's true."*
>
> What word is this?

---

**BACK**

**gullible** /ˈɡʌlɪbl/ | *adjective*

**Definition:** Easy to deceive because you trust and believe people too quickly.

**Episode quote:** *"If you simply place trust indiscriminately without making a judgement about whether the other person or institution is trustworthy… just trusting to luck… is probably not a virtue."*

**Personal sentence:** *"Stakeholders who accept dashboard numbers without questioning the source system are being gullible — even if the data looks clean."*

---

## Card 3

**FRONT**
> *"This phrase describes the behaviour of hoping everything will work out without making a careful judgement. It's the opposite of a strategic, reasoned approach."*
>
> What phrase is this?

---

**BACK**

**trust to luck** /trʌst tə lʌk/ | *verb phrase*

**Definition:** Believe or simply hope that things will happen for the best without making a careful judgement.

**Episode quote:** *"Just trusting to luck as we say, is probably not a virtue."*

**Personal sentence:** *"Deploying a pipeline to production without running integration tests is little more than trusting to luck."*

---

## Card 4

**FRONT**
> *"This adverb describes action taken in a random, unsystematic way that does not show care or judgment — and usually leads to harmful results."*
>
> What word is this?

---

**BACK**

**indiscriminately** /ˌɪndɪˈskrɪmɪnətli/ | *adverb*

**Definition:** Done in a random way that does not show care or judgement, usually with harmful results.

**Episode quote:** *"Their biggest mistake was to trust him indiscriminately — in a way that does not show care or judgement, usually with harmful results."*

**Personal sentence:** *"If you ingest raw files indiscriminately without validating schemas, corrupted data will propagate through every downstream model."*

---

## Card 5

**FRONT**
> *"This noun refers to the ability to do something well, in a satisfactory or effective way. It is one of three conditions needed to be trustworthy."*
>
> What word is this?

---

**BACK**

**competence** /ˈkɒmpɪtəns/ | *noun*

**Definition:** The ability to do something well, in a satisfactory or effective way.

**Episode quote:** *"Second, competence. Beyond honesty and competence there's a third element to trustworthiness: reliability."*

**Personal sentence:** *"Technical competence in dbt means not just writing SQL that runs, but building models that are accurate, maintainable, and perform at scale."*

---

## Card 6

**FRONT**
> *"This noun describes the quality of behaving well and keeping your promises all the time — not just occasionally or when it's convenient. It is the third and 'boring' ingredient of trustworthiness."*
>
> What word is this?

---

**BACK**

**reliability** /rɪˌlaɪəˈbɪlɪti/ | *noun*

**Definition:** The quality of being trusted to do what you say you will, all the time.

**Episode quote:** *"That's the boring one. That's just being honest and competent each time so that it's not enough to be episodically honest and competent."*

**Personal sentence:** *"An Airflow DAG's reliability is not about running perfectly once — it's about handling retries, dead letter queues, and failure alerts consistently, every single day."*

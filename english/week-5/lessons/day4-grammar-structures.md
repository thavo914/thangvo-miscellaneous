## Pattern 1 — Present Perfect for Recent Change
**Transcript quote:**
> *"Companies who've got rid of managers say it has helped them do a better job."*

**Communicative function:** Describes a completed action with a present relevance — the change happened and its effects are still felt now.

**Template:**
> `[Subject] + have/has + [past participle] + [result/benefit]`

**Examples:**
- "We've migrated the pipeline to Airflow and it has reduced our manual errors significantly."
- "The team has adopted a new code review policy and it has improved delivery speed."
- "The casino has installed a new surveillance system and it has helped reduce shrinkage."

**Your turn:**
> Describe one recent change in your team or project using this structure. What happened, and what has it helped you achieve?

## Pattern 2 — Passive Voice for Describing Systems
**Transcript quote:**
> *"Decisions are made by all employees equally without coercion."*

**Communicative function:** Emphasises the action or process rather than who is personally doing it — common in professional and formal contexts.

**Template:**
> `[Subject] + am/is/are + [past participle] + by [agent]`

**Examples:**
- "Data quality checks are run automatically by the pipeline on every load."
- "Player ratings are calculated by the analytics engine every 24 hours."
- "Incident reports are reviewed by the oncall engineer before escalation."

**Your turn:**
> Describe one process in your work that happens without a specific person directing it each time.

## Pattern 3 — Modal Verb of Obligation (must / have to)
**Transcript quote:**
> *"Employees must keep their commitments — promises or firm decisions to do something when requested."*

**Communicative function:** Expresses a strong obligation — something non-negotiable in a rule or expectation.

**Template:**
> `[Subject] + must/have to + [base verb] + [object/complement]`

**Examples:**
- "Every engineer must document their changes before merging to main."
- "Data pipelines have to complete within the SLA window or the on-call is paged."
- "A casino dealer must follow the house rules without exception, even under pressure."

**Your turn:**
> State one non-negotiable rule or standard in your team. Use "must" or "have to."

## Pattern 4 — Condition Clause with Result (even if / no matter whether)
**Transcript quote:**
> *"No matter whether employees are good self-managers or not, ultimately they are held accountable."*

**Communicative function:** Introduces a concession — the result stays the same regardless of the condition.

**Template:**
> `No matter whether [condition A] or [condition B], [result]`
> `Even if [condition], [result]`

**Examples:**
- "No matter whether the source data is delayed or not, the pipeline must complete by 6am."
- "Even if the model runs in full-refresh mode, the output should match the incremental results."
- "No matter whether the table is a fact or dimension, primary keys must always be unique."

**Your turn:**
> Describe a quality rule or delivery constraint in your work that applies in all conditions.

## Pattern 5 — Phrasal Verb with Emphasis (take full advantage of)
**Transcript quote:**
> *"Other workers enjoy managing themselves and take full advantage of the system."*

**Communicative function:** "Take advantage of" with "full" adds emphasis — meaning to use something to its maximum benefit.

**Template:**
> `[Subject] + take(s) full advantage of + [opportunity/system/tool]`

**Examples:**
- "Our team has taken full advantage of dbt's incremental models to cut run times by 60%."
- "The casino marketing team took full advantage of the new CRM data to personalise promotions."
- "I'm trying to take full advantage of Airflow's dynamic task mapping for our batch jobs."

**Your turn:**
> Describe one tool, system, or situation at work where you have (or haven't yet) taken full advantage.

## Pattern 6 — Relative Clause to Define a Term
**Transcript quote:**
> *"Most companies are hierarchies — systems of organising people according to their level of importance."*

**Communicative function:** A relative clause (or dash + noun phrase) adds a formal, spoken definition — very common in explanation and professional communication.

**Template:**
> `[Term] — [noun] of [verb+ing] [object] according to / based on [criteria]`

**Examples:**
- "We use a data lakehouse — a storage architecture that combines the flexibility of a data lake with the structure of a warehouse."
- "Airflow is a workflow orchestrator — a tool for scheduling and monitoring jobs according to a defined DAG."
- "A VIP tier is a loyalty classification — a system of ranking players according to their lifetime spend."

**Your turn:**
> Give a spoken definition of one technical term from your job using this pattern.

## Pattern 7 — Concession with "Although / However"
**Transcript quote:**
> *"Although having no boss sounds good, if things go wrong, there's no one to blame but yourself."*

**Communicative function:** Introduces a contrast between what seems true on the surface and a less obvious reality — essential for balanced arguments.

**Template:**
> `Although [positive/expected statement], [unexpected/contrasting result].`
> `However, [contrasting point].`

**Examples:**
- "Although real-time streaming gives us low latency, it significantly increases infrastructure complexity."
- "Although self-service BI empowers business users, it can lead to inconsistent metric definitions."
- "However, giving casino floor staff direct access to dashboards without training can cause misinterpretation of data."

**Your turn:**
> State one trade-off or risk in your current technical approach using "although" or "however."

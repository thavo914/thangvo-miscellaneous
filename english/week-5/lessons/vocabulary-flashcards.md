# Vocabulary Flashcards: No More Bosses
**Episode**: BBC 6 Minute English | B1-B2 Intermediate | Week 5

---

> **Study method**: Cover the back. Read the front question. Answer aloud. Then reveal the back.
> **Spaced repetition**: Review cards you got wrong every day. Review correct cards every 2–3 days.

---

## Card 1

**FRONT:**
> What word describes a system where people are organised according to their level of importance — with managers at the top and workers below?

**BACK:**
> **hierarchy** /ˈhaɪərɑːki/ | *noun*
> A system of organising people according to their level of importance.
> **Episode quote**: *"Most companies are hierarchies — systems of organising people according to their level of importance."*
> **Personal sentence**: _In most casino companies, the IT hierarchy means data engineers rarely speak directly to executive stakeholders._

---

## Card 2

**FRONT:**
> What is the noun for the act of using force or pressure to make someone do something they don't want to do?

**BACK:**
> **coercion** /kəʊˈɜːʃən/ | *noun*
> The use of force to persuade someone to do something they do not want to do.
> **Episode quote**: *"Decisions are made by all employees equally without coercion."*
> **Personal sentence**: _Good team leads get results through trust and clarity, not through coercion or micro-management._

---

## Card 3

**FRONT:**
> What word describes a serious promise or firm decision to do something — the thing Morning Star employees are required to keep with each other?

**BACK:**
> **commitment** /kəˈmɪtmənt/ | *noun*
> A promise or firm decision to do something.
> **Episode quote**: *"Employees must keep their commitments — promises or firm decisions to do something when requested."*
> **Personal sentence**: _When I commit to a pipeline delivery date, I make sure the infrastructure and testing plan are already in place._

---

## Card 4

**FRONT:**
> What phrasal verb means to remain at a lower level than others because you are not as quick to improve, develop, or progress?

**BACK:**
> **left behind** /left bɪˈhaɪnd/ | *phrasal verb*
> To remain at a lower level than others because you are not as quick to improve or progress.
> **Episode quote**: *"Some employees may be left behind — remain at a lower level than others because they are not as quick to develop."*
> **Personal sentence**: _Engineers who don't adopt cloud-native tools risk getting left behind as the industry moves toward real-time architectures._

---

## Card 5

**FRONT:**
> What phrase means to make good use of an opportunity in order to progress or achieve a goal?

**BACK:**
> **take full advantage (of)** /teɪk fʊl ədˈvɑːntɪdʒ/ | *phrase*
> To make good use of an opportunity to progress or achieve a goal.
> **Episode quote**: *"Other workers enjoy managing themselves and take full advantage of the system."*
> **Personal sentence**: _Our team has taken full advantage of dbt's incremental models to cut pipeline runtimes by over 40%._

---

## Card 6

**FRONT:**
> What phrase means to accept full responsibility for the consequences of your actions — especially when things go wrong?

**BACK:**
> **be held accountable** /biː held əˈkaʊntəbl/ | *phrase*
> To accept responsibility for the consequences of your actions.
> **Episode quote**: *"If they don't act according to their commitments, they can be held accountable by anyone."*
> **Personal sentence**: _In an on-call rotation, each engineer is held accountable for incidents in the systems they own._

---

## Review Quiz

Answer without looking at the cards. Write your answers first, then check.

**Q1.** What is the word for a company structure with managers on top and workers below?

**Q2.** Fill in the blank: *"In a self-managed company, everything is based on requesting — not _______ — people to act."*

**Q3.** What is the difference between a **commitment** and a **promise** in everyday usage? (Tip: think about formality and weight.)

**Q4.** Use "take full advantage of" in a sentence about your work.

**Q5.** Rephrase this sentence using the passive: *"The team holds every engineer accountable for their deliverables."*

---

> **Answers:**
> Q1: hierarchy
> Q2: coercion
> Q3: Both are pledges, but "commitment" has a stronger, more formal weight — often tied to professional obligation.

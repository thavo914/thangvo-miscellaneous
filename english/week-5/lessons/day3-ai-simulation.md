# Day 3 — AI Simulation (Episode Theme)
**Episode**: No More Bosses | BBC 6 Minute English | B1-B2 Intermediate

---

## Rules for All Rounds
- Aim for **B1-B2 level** natural English — not overly formal, not too casual.
- Do **not** stop during a round for corrections. Finish first.
- After each round: review AI feedback and **re-read improved sentences aloud x3**.
- Use at least **2 vocabulary words** from this episode in each round.

**Target Vocabulary Reminder:**
> hierarchy | coercion | commitment | left behind | take full advantage (of) | be held accountable

---

## Round 1 — Peer Discussion (15 min)
> **AI Role**: A curious colleague who has just listened to the same BBC episode and wants to discuss it with you.

**Opening prompt (paste this to your AI):**

> "You are my curious colleague and we just listened to a BBC 6 Minute English episode called 'No More Bosses'. It discusses self-managing companies like Morning Star where there are no bosses, and employees must keep commitments and be held accountable by each other. Please have a natural B1-B2 level conversation with me about this. Start by asking me what I thought about the idea of removing all managers. Do not correct my language during the conversation — save all corrections for after I say 'end of round'."

**Discussion starters (for yourself):**
- What's your honest reaction to the idea of "no bosses"?
- Would this kind of system work in your current company? Why / why not?
- Do you think some workers benefit more than others in a self-managed system?

**Post-round feedback prompt:**
> "End of round. Now show me: 3 phrases I used that sounded unnatural, the corrected version, and why it sounds better."

---

## Round 2 — Expert Role-Play (15 min)
> **AI Role**: Doug Kirkpatrick, the Morning Star employee from the episode, explaining his company's philosophy in detail.

**Opening prompt (paste this to your AI):**

> "You are Doug Kirkpatrick, a senior employee at Morning Star — the Californian self-managed tomato company featured in a BBC World Service programme. I am a journalist interviewing you about how self-management works in practice. Answer my questions confidently and in detail, drawing on the two principles from the episode: no coercion, and keeping commitments. My English is B1-B2. Do not correct me during the conversation — save feedback for when I say 'end of round'."

**Questions to ask Doug:**
1. What happens when someone doesn't keep their commitment — and there's no manager to enforce it?
2. Have you ever seen anyone get truly "left behind" in this system? How was it handled?
3. How do you hold someone accountable without using authority or coercion?

**Post-round feedback prompt:**
> "End of round. Now show me: 3 phrases I used that sounded unnatural, the corrected version, and why it sounds better."

---

## Round 3 — Devil's Advocate / Core Debate (20 min)
> **AI Role**: A sceptical HR director who believes traditional management hierarchies are necessary and efficient.

**Opening prompt (paste this to your AI):**

> "You are a sceptical HR director with 20 years of experience building management hierarchies in large companies. I believe self-management can work and want to convince you. Push back on my arguments firmly but respectfully. Use real-world examples or logic. My English is B1-B2. Do not correct me during the debate — save all feedback for when I say 'end of round'."

**Core debate positions:**
- **You argue**: Self-management leads to more motivated employees and saves money.
- **HR director argues**: Hierarchies exist for a reason — accountability, direction, and efficiency at scale.

**Vocabulary challenge**: Try to use ALL 6 target words at least once during this round.

**Post-round feedback prompt:**
> "End of round. Now: 1) Show me 3 corrections. 2) Tell me which vocabulary words I used correctly, which I used awkwardly, and which I forgot to use."

---

2. Say each sentence aloud **x3** — no reading, just speaking.
3. Write one new sentence of your own using one of today's vocabulary words.

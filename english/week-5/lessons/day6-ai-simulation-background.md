# Day 6 — AI Simulation (Background Topic: Data Engineering / Casino)
**Episode**: No More Bosses | BBC 6 Minute English | B1-B2 Intermediate

---

## Purpose
Practice professional English in real-world conversations from your Data Engineering and Casino background. Connect this week's vocabulary — hierarchy, coercion, commitment, accountability — to your actual work context.

## Rules for All Rounds
- B1-B2 level — natural, professional, not overly formal.
- No mid-conversation corrections. Finish each round first.
- After each round: review AI feedback and re-read improved sentences aloud x3.
- Challenge: Use at least **3 episode vocabulary words** naturally across all rounds.

---

## Background Prompt Bank
> These 3 customised prompts connect this episode's theme (self-management, accountability, flat structures) to your professional domain. Use one per round.

**Prompt A — No bosses in data teams:**
> "You are a senior engineering manager at a casino tech company. I am a data engineer pitching the idea of moving our data team to a more self-managed, flat structure — inspired by the Morning Star model. Push back on my proposal with real concerns about accountability, commitments, and coordination. My English is B1-B2. Do not correct me mid-conversation."

**Prompt B — Accountability in data pipelines:**
> "You are a hiring manager for a Senior Data Engineer role at a resort and casino group. The company is transitioning from a traditional IT hierarchy to a more agile, self-organized team model. Ask me about: (1) how I handle accountability when there is no assigned owner for a data issue, (2) a time I committed to a deadline and had to manage it without direct supervision, and (3) how I avoid 'getting left behind' as technologies evolve. My English is B1-B2."

**Prompt C — Hierarchy vs. flat team in analytics:**
> "You are a CTO at a mid-sized casino group who is evaluating whether to keep a traditional data hierarchy (data engineer → senior DE → team lead → manager) or flatten it. I am arguing for a flatter model with stronger individual accountability. Have a strategic discussion with me. Use real-world engineering examples to challenge or support my points. My English is B1-B2. Do not correct me during the conversation."

---

## Round 1 — Non-Technical Stakeholder (15 min)
> **AI Role**: A casino floor manager or marketing director with no data background.
> **Use prompt A above.**

**Your goals:**
- Explain the concept of a self-managed data team **without technical jargon**.
- Use an analogy (e.g., compare the data pipeline to a restaurant kitchen where each cook owns their station).
- Address the manager's concern: *"If there's no team lead, who do I call when data is wrong?"*

**Post-round feedback prompt:**
> "End of round. Show me: 3 phrases I used that sounded unnatural or unclear, the corrected version, and explain why the correction is better for a non-technical audience."

---

## Round 2 — Peer Expert Discussion (15 min)
> **AI Role**: A senior data engineer or solution architect at another company.
> **Use prompt C above.**

**Your goals:**
- Discuss the architecture of accountability in a flat data team.
- Explain how you handle incidents, ownership, and SLA commitments without a management layer.
- Mention a real trade-off you have seen between moving fast (no hierarchy) and maintaining data quality.

**Post-round feedback prompt:**
> "End of round. Show me: 3 corrections, one vocabulary word from the episode I used well, and one I could have used but didn't."

## Phase 2 — Recorded Monologue (30 min)

### Choose ONE Topic:

**Option A — Data Engineering Focus:**
> *"Describe how accountability works (or should work) in your data engineering team when there is no clear owner for a pipeline failure. Do you think a flatter structure would help or hurt your team's performance?"*

**Option B — Casino Operations Focus:**
> *"How would you explain the idea of a 'self-managed data team' to a non-technical casino operations director? What analogy would you use, and how would you address their concern about who to call when something goes wrong?"*

**Option C — Personal Reflection Focus:**
> *"Think of a time in your career when you had to be fully accountable for a technical decision — without a manager to back you up. What happened, what did you do, and what did you learn?"*

---

### Timing Table

| Time | Section | What to Say |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0:00 – 0:30 | **Hook** | Start with a question, a surprising fact from the episode, or a short story from your work. |
| 0:30 – 1:30 | **Main Point** | State your central idea clearly. Use 1–2 episode vocabulary words here. |
| 1:30 – 2:30 | **Example** | Give a specific, real example from your own experience. Use STAR format loosely. |
| 2:30 – 3:00 | **Conclusion** | Wrap up with a personal opinion or lesson learned. Use "although," "however," or "ultimately." |

---

### Example Hook Options (pick one or write your own):

- *"Morning Star has no managers and still processes millions of tons of tomatoes every year. So why does my company still have three layers of approval for a Jira ticket?"*
- *"In data engineering, hierarchies are everywhere — team leads, architects, VPs of Data. But when the pipeline breaks at 3am, it's not the hierarchy that fixes it. It's the person who owns the table."*
- *"The episode asked: what happens when workers are left behind in a self-managed team? I've seen that happen — not to a person, but to an entire data stack."*

## 🎯 How to Use This Reference for Self-Evaluation

When practicing **1-Minute Gist-Catching & Verbal Summary** (Drill 2 in [daily-listening-and-speaking-reflex-guide.md](file:///c:/Users/adm.thangvm/repos/thangvo-miscellaneous/english/daily-listening-and-speaking-reflex-guide.md)):

1. **Pass 1 (Gist Pass)**: Listen to the audio once through without reading the transcript. Focus only on the macro picture: **WHO** is speaking, **WHAT** is the obstacle, and what **ACTION** is taken.
2. **Pass 2 (Precision Pass)**: Listen a second time to catch constraints (names, times, policy rules, location details).
3. **Instant Verbal Output (The 15-Second Rule)**: Stop the audio and immediately speak your summary out loud in English within 15–20 seconds using the **3W Framework**.
4. **Compare & Score**: Read the transcript, 3W Key Elements, and Benchmark Model Summary below to score your performance out of 10 points.

### 📊 Universal 10-Point Scoring Rubric

| Component | Max Points | Evaluation Criteria |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **1. Core Fact Accuracy (3W)** | **4 Pts** | Captured the core situation, conflict, and final resolution without missing key facts (1 pt per fact). |
| **2. Speed & Reflex** | **3 Pts** | Began speaking within 3 seconds of the audio ending; finished summary in under 20 seconds without long hesitations. |
| **3. Natural Phrasing & Chunks** | **3 Pts** | Used native phrasal verbs, idioms, and collocations; completely avoided word-by-word translation. |

## 📑 Clip 1: Hotel Check-In & First-Name Reservation Mix-up (Questions 1–3)

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Man**: *"Hi, I have a reservation for tonight. The name is Peterson, Michael Peterson."*
* **Woman**: *"Good evening, sir. Let me check that for you. Hmm, I'm not seeing a reservation under Peterson. Could you spell your last name for me?"*
* **Man**: *"P-E-T-E-R-S-O-N. I booked it online last week. Actually, I have the confirmation email right here on my phone if that helps."*
* **Woman**: *"Oh, that would be very helpful. Let me see... Ah, I see what happened. The booking was entered under your first name, Michael, rather than your last name. I do apologize for the confusion."*
* **Man**: *"No problem, as long as I have a room!"*
* **Woman**: *"Absolutely. You're all set in Room 412. Here's your key card. The elevators are just around the corner to your left."*

### 🔑 3W Key Elements
* **Who**: A hotel guest (Michael Peterson) and the front desk receptionist.
* **What**: The receptionist couldn't find his booking under his last name Peterson, but after checking his confirmation email, discovered the reservation was accidentally recorded under his first name Michael.
* **Action/Next**: She apologized for the confusion and checked him into Room 412, handing him his key card and giving directions to the elevators.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"A guest named Michael Peterson arrived at a hotel, but the receptionist couldn't locate his reservation under his last name. After he showed his confirmation email, she realized the booking was mistakenly filed under his first name, apologized, and successfully checked him into Room 412."*

### 📋 Clip 1 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Noted that the receptionist couldn't find the reservation under his last name.
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Mentioned the guest offered his confirmation email on his phone.
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Caught the mix-up: entered under first name Michael instead of last name.
* [ ] **Fact 4 (1 pt)**: Stated the resolution (`checked into Room 412` + `handed key card`).
* [ ] **Reflex & Flow (3 pts)**: Spoke within 3 seconds, delivered cohesive narrative under 20 seconds.
* [ ] **Native Chunks (3 pts)**: Used phrases: `reservation under [Name]`, `entered under`, `as long as`, `you're all set`, `just around the corner`.

## 📑 Clip 2: Retail Return & Store Credit Policy (Questions 4–6)

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Woman**: *"Excuse me, I'd like to return this shirt. I bought it last weekend, but when I got home I realized the color doesn't match what I needed it for."*
* **Man**: *"Of course, I can help you with that. Do you have your receipt?"*
* **Woman**: *"Unfortunately, no. I think I threw it away by accident. But I paid with my credit card, would that work instead?"*
* **Man**: *"I understand. The thing is, our store policy requires a receipt for cash refunds. However, since you have the card, I can verify the purchase in our system and offer you store credit for the full amount."*
* **Woman**: *"That's fine with me. I'll probably just pick out a different color anyway."*
* **Man**: *"Perfect. Let me just scan the item and pull up the transaction. This will only take a moment."*

### 🔑 3W Key Elements
* **Who**: A female customer and a retail store associate.
* **What**: The customer wanted to return a shirt because the color didn't match, but had accidentally thrown away her paper receipt.
* **Action/Next**: The associate explained that store policy requires a receipt for cash refunds, but offered to look up her credit card transaction to issue full store credit, which she happily accepted.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"A customer wanted to return a shirt because the color didn't suit her needs, but had lost her receipt. The sales associate explained that cash refunds require a receipt, but offered to verify her credit card and provide store credit for the full amount, which she accepted to choose another color."*

### 📋 Clip 2 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Customer wanted to return a shirt because the color didn't match.
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Customer threw away her receipt by accident.
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Store policy prohibits cash refunds without a receipt.
* [ ] **Fact 4 (1 pt)**: Resolution: Verified card and issued store credit for the full amount.
* [ ] **Reflex & Flow (3 pts)**: Clear narrative transitions delivered within 15–20 seconds.
* [ ] **Native Chunks (3 pts)**: Used chunks: `store policy requires`, `cash refunds`, `store credit`, `pull up the transaction`.

## 📑 Clip 3: Dental Appointment Rescheduling & Work Conflict (Questions 7–9)

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Woman**: *"Good morning, Riverside Dental Clinic. How may I help you?"*
* **Man**: *"Hello, this is Thomas Wright. I have an appointment scheduled for this Thursday at two o'clock, but I'm afraid I need to reschedule. Something's come up at work."*
* **Woman**: *"I see. Let me check what we have available. Is there a particular day that works better for you?"*
* **Man**: *"Ideally sometime next week. My schedule should be much clearer by then. My manager just informed me about an important client meeting on Thursday that I can't miss."*
* **Woman**: *"Alright, I have an opening next Tuesday at nine in the morning. Would that suit you?"*
* **Man**: *"Tuesday morning works perfectly. Thank you for fitting me in."*
* **Woman**: *"You're welcome, Mr. Wright. We'll see you next Tuesday at nine. Have a good day."*

* **What**: Thomas needed to reschedule his dental checkup on Thursday at 2:00 PM due to an unavoidable client meeting called by his manager.
* **Action/Next**: The receptionist checked clinic availability and rebooked his appointment for next Tuesday at 9:00 AM, which fitted his schedule perfectly.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"Thomas Wright called Riverside Dental Clinic to reschedule his Thursday two o'clock appointment due to an urgent client meeting at work. The receptionist checked their schedule and rebooked him for an opening next Tuesday at nine in the morning, which suited him perfectly."*

### 📋 Clip 3 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Identified caller Thomas Wright and Riverside Dental Clinic.
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Original appointment was Thursday at 2:00 PM.
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Reason: Urgent client meeting at work that he cannot miss (`something's come up`).

* **Action/Next**: The agent reassured him that windows are fitted with soundproof double-glazed glass, and confirmed the lease includes 5 underground parking spaces with options for more.

The leasing agent reassured him that the windows are double-glazed and soundproof, and confirmed the lease includes five reserved parking spaces in the garage."*

## 📑 Clip 6: Scheduled System Maintenance & Offline Workaround (Questions 4–6)

### 📜 Full Audio Transcript
* **Man**: *"Hi, I'm having trouble logging into the system this morning. It keeps saying my credentials are invalid, but I'm certain I'm typing the right password."*
* **Woman**: *"Yes, we've had a few calls about this already. The system is currently down for scheduled maintenance. We're installing some security upgrades, and everything should be back online by ten o'clock."*
* **Man**: *"Ah, that explains it. I wish someone had sent out a reminder. Is there anything I can do in the meantime? I have a report due at noon."*
* **Woman**: *"Well, if you have any documents saved locally on your computer, you could work on those. And once the system is back up, you shouldn't have any trouble uploading your files."*
* **Man**: *"Good idea. I think I have an older draft on my desktop. I'll work on that for now."*

### 🔑 3W Key Elements
* **Who**: An employee facing login issues and an IT support representative.
* **What**: The employee couldn't log into the company portal due to an "invalid credentials" error, which was actually caused by scheduled system maintenance for security upgrades until 10:00 AM.
* **Action/Next**: Facing a noon deadline for a report, the employee followed IT's advice to work offline on a local draft on his computer until the system is restored.

### 🎙️ Benchmark Model Verbal Summary (15 Seconds)
> *"An employee couldn't log into the system and was told by IT support that it was temporarily down for scheduled security maintenance until 10 AM. Since his report was due at noon, he decided to work offline on an older draft saved on his desktop in the meantime."*

### 📋 Clip 6 Scoring Checklist (10 Points)
* [ ] **Fact 1 (1 pt)**: Problem: Login failure due to "invalid credentials" error prompt.
* [ ] **Fact 2 (1 pt)**: Cause & Timeline: System down for scheduled maintenance / security upgrades until 10:00 AM.
* [ ] **Fact 3 (1 pt)**: Urgency/Deadline: Employee has a report due at noon (`due at noon`).
* [ ] **Fact 4 (1 pt)**: Workaround: Editing local drafts saved on desktop until servers come back online.
* [ ] **Reflex & Flow (3 pts)**: Fast start (< 3s); continuous verbal delivery without hesitation in under 18 seconds.

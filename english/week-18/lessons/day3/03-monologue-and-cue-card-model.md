# Day 3 - Exercise 3: Monologue & Cue Card Model Answer

> [!NOTE]
> **Block 2 (Part B)**: Workplace monologue on professional boundary setting, key vocabulary breakdown, and Band 6.5–7.0 Cue Card model answer.

## 🗣️ Part 1: Workplace Monologue (Why Engineers Must Learn to Say No)

Listen to or read this short professional monologue aloud:

> *"Early in my career as a data engineer, I used to believe that being a great teammate meant saying yes to every single request. Whenever someone asked for a custom SQL extract or a quick dashboard change, **I tended to jump right on it**.  
> 
> However, I quickly realized that saying yes to everything means **spreading yourself too thin**. When your attention is scattered across ten minor tasks, the quality of your core pipelines inevitably drops. That's why learning to **set clear boundaries** is actually an act of professional responsibility.  
> 
> When unexpected requests come in, **instead of reacting immediately, I'd rather pause and evaluate the real priority**. If a request compromises our sprint stability, **I won't hesitate to push back on the deadline politely**. By communicating trade-offs openly, I can **keep up with the core workload**, protect code quality, and ensure I can **unplug after work** without constant anxiety."*

### Key Vocabulary & Concepts
| Term / Phrase | Part of Speech | Vietnamese Translation | Contextual Spoken Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **scattered across** | Adjective phrase | Bị phân tán / rải rác | *"My attention was scattered across too many tickets."* |
| **act of responsibility** | Noun phrase | Hành động có trách nhiệm | *"Setting boundaries is an act of responsibility."* |
| **compromise stability** | Verb phrase | Làm tổn hại đến sự ổn định | *"Rushing the deploy will compromise stability."* |
| **hesitate to** | Verb phrase | Do dự / ngần ngại | *"I won't hesitate to push back when needed."* |

## 🏆 Part 2: Band 6.5–7.0 Cue Card Model Answer

Model answer for the Day 2 Cue Card (*Describe a time when you had to manage a very heavy workload*):

> *"I'd like to talk about a challenging period last quarter when our data team was migrating our core payment reporting pipeline.  
> 
> At the time, we were facing a very heavy workload because two senior engineers were out on planned leave right when an upstream third-party service changed their event schema without notice. Suddenly, dozens of downstream financial reports broke, and our Slack channels were flooded with urgent pings.  
> 
> Initially, **I tended to feel overwhelmed**, and I started **spreading myself too thin** trying to answer every individual message while writing migration scripts simultaneously. I quickly realized that working sixteen hours a day wasn't sustainable and would only lead to careless errors.  
> 
> So, I stepped back and **set clear boundaries**. I posted a concise update in our central announcement channel, letting stakeholders know that **my top priority was fixing the upstream ingestion layer**, and that **I wouldn't be able to handle custom queries until Friday**. For non-critical requests, **I pushed back on the delivery timeline**.  
> 
> **Instead of panicking and pushing hotfixes to production**, I spent four focused hours isolating the schema mismatch and writing clean transformation logic. That disciplined approach allowed us to restore data accuracy by Thursday afternoon.  
> 
> From that experience, I learned that managing workload stress isn't about working more hours—it's about communicating transparently, prioritizing ruthlessly, and having the courage to say no so you can **unplug after work** with peace of mind."*

### 🌟 3 High-Yield Expressions from the Model:
1. **flooded with urgent pings** (*ngập trong các tin nhắn khẩn cấp*)
2. **ruthlessly prioritize** (*ưu tiên một cách dứt khoát và chọn lọc*)
3. **with peace of mind** (*với sự an tâm / thanh thản*)

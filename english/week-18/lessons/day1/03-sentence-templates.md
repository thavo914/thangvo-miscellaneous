## 📐 Template 1: Communicating Work Priorities & Polite Boundary

* **Function**: To state your current top focus and defer new requests without friction.
* **Slot-Filling Formula**:
  $$\text{Right now, my top priority is } [\text{Task/Project}], \text{ so I won't be able to take on } [\text{New Request}] \text{ until } [\text{Time}].$$
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: Right now, my top priority is preparing dinner for my family, so I won't be able to run that errand until tomorrow morning.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: Right now, my top priority is debugging the payment streaming consumer, so I won't be able to take on the marketing ad-hoc query until Thursday afternoon.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: Right now, my top priority is completing the quarterly budget report, so I won't be able to join the brainstorming session until next week.

### Immediate Practice Drill
* **Mini-Drill**: A PM asks you to build a new dashboard export today while you are fixing a broken warehouse ingestion pipeline. Use Template 1 to set a boundary.
* **Your Turn**: `Right now, my top priority is ` ____________________ `, so I won't be able to take on ` ____________________ ` until ` ____________________.

<details>

* *Right now, my top priority is fixing the broken warehouse ingestion pipeline, so I won't be able to take on the dashboard export until tomorrow at 10 AM.*
* *Right now, my top priority is stabilizing the customer event table, so I won't be able to take on that new extract until Friday.*
</details>

## 📐 Template 2: Identifying Recurring Personal or Team Tendencies

* **Function**: To reflect objectively on habits or patterns that cause friction or stress.
* **Slot-Filling Formula**:
  $$\text{I've noticed that whenever I } [\text{Action/Trigger}], \text{ I tend to } [\text{Result/Habit}].$$
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: I've noticed that whenever I drink coffee after 4 PM, I tend to have trouble sleeping through the night.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: I've noticed that whenever I skip writing mock tests for a new DAG, I tend to spend twice as much time troubleshooting staging errors.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: I've noticed that whenever I keep my messaging notifications on during deep work, I tend to lose track of my main objective.

### Immediate Practice Drill
* **Mini-Drill**: Explain what happens when you don't write down your daily priorities in the morning.
* **Your Turn**: `I've noticed that whenever I ` ____________________ `, I tend to ` ____________________.

<details>
<summary>💡 Click to View Suggested Completions</summary>

* *I've noticed that whenever I start my workday without a clear task list, I tend to get pulled into minor ad-hoc requests.*
* *I've noticed that whenever I deploy changes on Friday afternoons, I tend to spend the weekend checking server alerts.*
</details>

## 📐 Template 3: Explaining Scope Trade-offs

* **Function**: To explain that accepting new tasks requires pushing back existing commitments.
* **Slot-Filling Formula**:
  $$\text{If we agree to take on } [\text{Project A}], \text{ we'll have to push back on } [\text{Project B}] \text{ because of } [\text{Constraint}].$$
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: If we agree to go out for dinner tonight, we'll have to push back on our movie plans because of traffic.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: If we agree to take on the real-time analytics feature, we'll have to push back on the schema cleanup because of limited engineering hours.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: If we agree to host the client visit this Wednesday, we'll have to push back on the internal sprint review because of meeting room availability.

### Immediate Practice Drill
* **Mini-Drill**: A manager asks to expedite a customer cohort report. Explain that doing so means delaying the daily ETL optimization.
* **Your Turn**: `If we agree to take on ` ____________________ `, we'll have to push back on ` ____________________ ` because of ` ____________________.

<details>
<summary>💡 Click to View Suggested Completions</summary>

* *If we agree to take on the urgent customer cohort report, we'll have to push back on the daily ETL optimization because of our current compute constraints.*
* *If we agree to take on the audit data extraction, we'll have to push back on the table partitioning because of team bandwidth.*
</details>

## 📐 Template 4: Emphasizing Quality Over Last-Minute Rushing

* **Function**: To advocate for thoughtful, robust solutions instead of messy shortcuts.
* **Slot-Filling Formula**:
  $$\text{Instead of } [\text{Verb-ing}] \text{ at the last minute, I'd rather } [\text{Verb Phrase}] \text{ upfront.}$$
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: Instead of packing our bags at the last minute, I'd rather get everything organized the night before.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: Instead of patching failed partitions at the last minute, I'd rather build automated validation checks upfront.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: Instead of scrambling to prepare presentation slides at the last minute, I'd rather align with the stakeholders on an outline early in the week.

### Immediate Practice Drill
* **Mini-Drill**: Explain why you prefer building automated unit tests early rather than fixing bugs right before deployment.
* **Your Turn**: `Instead of ` ____________________ ` at the last minute, I'd rather ` ____________________ ` upfront.`

<details>
<summary>💡 Click to View Suggested Completions</summary>

* *Instead of debugging broken schema dependencies at the last minute, I'd rather write clear schema contract tests upfront.*
* *Instead of fixing data mismatch errors right before the business review, I'd rather set up automated reconciliation alerts upfront.*
</details>

## 📐 Template 5: Protecting Bandwidth & Preventing Burnout

* **Function**: To describe proactive habits for workload management and recovery.
* **Slot-Filling Formula**:
  $$\text{To avoid spreading myself too thin, I make sure to } [\text{Action}] \text{ every } [\text{Time Period}].$$
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: To avoid spreading myself too thin, I make sure to schedule at least one quiet evening every week.
  2. *Data Engineering Context*: To avoid spreading myself too thin, I make sure to block out two hours of uninterrupted focus time every morning.
  3. *General Workplace Context*: To avoid spreading myself too thin, I make sure to review and reprioritize my task board every Monday morning.

### Immediate Practice Drill
* **Mini-Drill**: Describe a practical boundary you use to maintain your energy and productivity.
* **Your Turn**: `To avoid spreading myself too thin, I make sure to ` ____________________ ` every ` ____________________.

<details>
<summary>💡 Click to View Suggested Completions</summary>

* *To avoid spreading myself too thin, I make sure to mute non-critical Slack channels for two hours every afternoon.*
* *To avoid spreading myself too thin, I make sure to review incoming tickets with my team lead every morning.*
</details>

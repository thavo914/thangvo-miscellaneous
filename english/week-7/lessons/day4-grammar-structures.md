# Day 4: Grammar Structures

## Pattern 1: Present Perfect + Passive/Active (Describing historical effects up to now)
**Transcript Quote:** "From the bubonic plague to cholera and tuberculosis, disease and pandemics have changed the way cities have been built."
**Communicative Function:** Used to talk about something from the past that has an enduring result or ongoing effect on something else.
**Template:** `[Subject] has changed the way [Object] has been [verb].` OR `[Subject] has changed the way [Subject] [Verb].`
**Examples:**
- The shift to remote work has changed the way teams communicate.
- **Data Engineering Context:** Cloud computing has changed the way data pipelines have been architected.

## Pattern 2: Passive Voice + Infinitive of Purpose
**Transcript Quote:** "Buildings in 19th century Paris were designed with large, high-up windows to allow plenty of sunlight."
**Communicative Function:** Explaining the intended purpose behind a structural or design choice when it doesn't matter who built it.
**Template:** `[Subject] was/were designed to [verb phrase].` OR `[Subject] was designed with [Noun phrase] to [verb].`
**Examples:**
- The new highway was designed to reduce traffic in the city centre.
- **Casino Context:** The new loyalty program was designed with tiered rewards to encourage longer playtime.

## Pattern 3: "Be Supposed To" for Intended Outcomes
**Transcript Quote:** "They were supposed to stop the spread of tuberculosis."
**Communicative Function:** Stating what was planned, expected, or intended to happen (often implying it might not have worked perfectly).
**Template:** `[Subject] is/were supposed to [verb phrase].`
**Examples:**
- We were supposed to launch the product next week.
- **Data Engineering Context:** The new caching layer was supposed to speed up the analytical queries.

## Pattern 4: Passive Voice Question (Estimations and Effects)
**Transcript Quote:** "But approximately how many people were affected?"
**Communicative Function:** Asking for numbers or statistics about an event that happened to a group.
**Template:** `How many [noun] were [past participle]?`
**Examples:**
- How many passengers were delayed by the storm?
- **Data Engineering Context:** How many records were dropped during the ETL transformation phase?

## Pattern 5: "Unlike..." for Contrasting Attributes
**Transcript Quote:** "Unlike cholera, which was eliminated in London by re-designing the sewage system in the 1850s, TB was airborne."
**Communicative Function:** Drawing a direct contrast between two things by emphasizing the specific difference.
**Template:** `Unlike [Noun A], which [relative clause], [Noun B] was [adjective/noun phrase].`
**Examples:**
- Unlike the old software, which was very slow, the new app is lightning fast.
- **Data Engineering Context:** Unlike batch processing, which runs on a schedule, streaming data is processed continuously.

## Pattern 6: Fronting / Inversion ("Adding to the problem was...")
**Transcript Quote:** "Adding to the problem was the fact that antibiotics... was not discovered until 1928"
**Communicative Function:** Highlighting a compounding factor. Inverting the sentence structure makes "the fact that" the impactful ending.
**Template:** `Adding to the [noun/problem/challenge] was the fact that [Clause].`
**Examples:**
- Adding to the delay was the fact that the supplier delivered the wrong materials.
- **Data Engineering Context:** Adding to the data loss was the fact that the backup server had been offline for two days.

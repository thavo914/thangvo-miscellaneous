## 📘 Section 1: Block 2 - Workplace Practice: Tech Negotiation Grammar (60 Mins)

### Rule 1: Polite Assertive Modals for Making High-Stakes Asks
* **Quote**: *"Based on my expanded scope of ownership and current market benchmarks, **I'd like to make a strong case for a 25% salary adjustment**."*
* **Rule Explanation**: In salary negotiations, direct commands (*"Give me a 25% raise"*) sound aggressive, while overly timid language (*"Maybe if possible can you consider..."*) weakens your bargaining power. Use assertive modals with conditional softening: `I would like to propose / I'd like to make a case for + [Noun Phrase]`.
* **Common Pitfall**:
  > ❌ **Incorrect**: *I want you increase my salary 25% because market pays that.* (Sounds blunt, combative).  
  >  **Correct**: *Based on my contributions and market benchmarks, **I'd like to propose a 25% compensation adjustment**.*

---

### Rule 2: Causative & Business Impact Formulations (`By + V-ing, I [Verb] + [Measurable Metric]`)
* **Quote**: *"**By optimizing the storage tier and warehouse queries, I was able to deliver significant bottom-line impact**, cutting our monthly cloud spend by over thirty percent."*
* **Rule Explanation**: Connect the engineering action directly to business value. Don't just say *what* you configured; explicitly show *how* it impacted cost, speed, or revenue using causative prepositions (`By + V-ing... enabled / allowed / resulted in`).
* **Common Pitfall**:
  > ❌ **Incorrect**: *I wrote code to clean database and it was good for company.* (Vague, sounds amateur).  
  >  **Correct**: *By repartitioning the analytical warehouse tables, **I was able to deliver immediate bottom-line impact**, reducing query execution costs by 35%.*

---

### Rule 3: Concession & Pivot Linkers (`While I understand + [Constraint], given that + [Value]...`)
* **Quote**: *"**While I understand standard company bands**, my responsibilities have evolved from routine pipeline maintenance to leading architectural overhauls..."*
* **Rule Explanation**: Acknowledge your manager's budget constraints with a concession clause (*While / Although I understand...*), then immediately pivot to your exceptional value (*given that / in light of...*). This prevents defensiveness.
* **Common Pitfall**:
  > ❌ **Incorrect**: *I don't care about the company band, I need 25%.* (Unprofessional).  
  >  **Correct**: *While I recognize that standard merit increases are between 5% and 10%, **given the expanded scope of my responsibilities**, I believe a 25% adjustment accurately reflects my contributions.*

## 🧩 Section 2: Sentence Structure Templates (5 Tech Negotiation Patterns)

### Template 1: The Direct Salary Proposal Pattern
* **Communicative Function**: To state your requested percentage clearly, backed by evidence.
* **Slot Formula**: `Based on my + [expanded scope / proven track record] + in + [Area] + and + [market benchmarks], I'd like to make a strong case for a + [Percentage/Amount] + salary adjustment.`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: "Based on my years of loyal service and current rental rates, I'd like to make a strong case for renewing our contract at the current price."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context*: "Based on my expanded scope of ownership across our real-time gaming pipelines and current market benchmarks, I'd like to make a strong case for a 25% salary adjustment."
  3. *General Workplace Context*: "Based on my proven track record in driving client retention and market benchmarks, I'd like to make a strong case for a 20% compensation increase."

### Template 2: Quantifying Bottom-Line Financial Impact
* **Communicative Function**: To show that your technical work directly saved money or increased revenue.
* **Slot Formula**: `By + [Verb-ing] + [Technical System/Tool], I was able to deliver significant bottom-line impact, specifically + [Measurable Financial Metric / Savings].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: "By meal prepping every Sunday, I was able to deliver significant savings, reducing our monthly grocery bill by 25%."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context*: "By refactoring our heavy data aggregation queries, I was able to deliver significant bottom-line impact, specifically saving $4,000 in monthly Snowflake credits."
  3. *General Workplace Context*: "By automating client invoicing, I was able to deliver significant bottom-line impact, cutting billing errors by over 90%."

### Template 3: The Diplomatic Pushback / Concession Pivot
* **Communicative Function**: To counter a manager's standard corporate pay-band restriction.
* **Slot Formula**: `While I understand + [Corporate Constraint/Standard Band], my contributions have + [Grown/Evolved] + into + [Higher Level of Ownership], which I believe justifies + [Adjustment/Raise].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: "While I understand the store's strict return policy, this item was defective out of the box, which I believe justifies a full replacement."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context*: "While I understand annual budget bands are typically capped at 7%, my contributions have evolved into end-to-end pipeline ownership, which I believe justifies a 25% raise."
  3. *General Workplace Context*: "While I understand the company's hiring freeze, my workload has doubled across three accounts, which I believe justifies a tier promotion."

### Template 4: The Collaborative Fallback Option
* **Communicative Function**: To keep the door open if the manager cannot approve the full amount immediately.
* **Slot Formula**: `If a full + [Percentage] + adjustment isn't feasible right now, would it be possible to + [Alternative: Agree on a 6-month milestone / Add performance incentives]?`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: "If paying the full amount today isn't feasible, would it be possible to split it into two monthly installments?"
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context*: "If a full 25% adjustment isn't feasible right now, would it be possible to implement a 15% increase now and revisit the remaining 10% in six months based on pipeline milestones?"
  3. *General Workplace Context*: "If a base salary bump isn't feasible this quarter, would it be possible to tie the difference to our project completion bonuses?"

### Template 5: Demonstrating Expanded Technical Ownership
* **Communicative Function**: To show you are already performing at the next seniority tier.
* **Slot Formula**: `Taking on additional ownership in + [Technical Domain/Project] + has allowed our team to + [Business/Operational Benefit].`
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday Context*: "Taking on additional ownership of family finances has allowed us to invest in our future."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context*: "Taking on additional ownership in data pipeline alerting and CDC syncs has allowed our analytics team to eliminate morning report lag completely."
  3. *General Workplace Context*: "Taking on additional ownership in weekly stakeholder communications has allowed our engineering leads to stay focused on deep technical work."

## 🔍 Section 4: Spot the Mistake (Error Correction Drill)

Identify the mistake in each statement:
1. *By optimize the warehouse queries, I made the company save 30% cost.*
2. *I want to justify a raise of 25% because other company benchmark pay higher.*
3. *While I am understanding standard company bands, I deserve more salary.*

<details>
<summary>💡 Click to View Explanations & Corrections</summary>

1. **Error**: *By optimize* and *save 30% cost* $\rightarrow$ **Correction**: *By **optimizing** the warehouse queries, I was able to **deliver significant bottom-line impact, cutting compute costs by 30%**.* (Gerund after preposition 'by', and use executive vocabulary).
2. **Error**: *other company benchmark pay higher* $\rightarrow$ **Correction**: *I want to justify a 25% raise because **market benchmarks for this role are significantly higher**.*
3. **Error**: *While I am understanding* $\rightarrow$ **Correction**: *While I **understand** standard company bands...* ('Understand' is a stative verb; do not use in continuous form).
</details>

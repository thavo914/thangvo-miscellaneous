## Why Dictation?

When you listen passively, your brain guesses words from context. Dictation forces your brain to identify **every single sound** — revealing where your ears are actually fooling you.

Today you will discover which connected speech phenomena are blocking your comprehension.

### Step 2: Classify your errors

For each mistake or `[???]`, identify the cause:

| Error Category           | Description                        | Example from this episode                         |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Elision**              | A sound was dropped                | *"initially"* → the /l/ in "initially" is reduced |
| **Weak form / Schwa**    | A vowel was shrunk to /ə/          | *"an internet giant"* → "an" is /ən/ not /æn/     |
| **Catenation (linking)** | A consonant grabbed the next vowel | *"bring out"* → sounds like "brin-gout"           |
| **Unknown vocabulary**   | You didn't know the word at all    | "ameliorate", "kudos"                             |
| **Speed**                | Simply too fast                    | Natural if first time hearing                     |

## Phase 3: Connected Speech Drill (10 min)

Practice these specific phrases from the clip, focusing on the linked sounds:

### Phrase 1: "an internet giant"
- Natural speech: "ə**n**_**in**ternet giant" — the /n/ links to "internet"
- Drill: Say it 5 times, linking the /n/ smoothly to "in"

### Phrase 2: "bring out the very nastiest"
- Natural speech: "brin**g**_**ou**t the very nastiest" — /g/ links to "out"
- Drill: Say it 5 times

### Phrase 3: "once connected by technology"
- Natural speech: "once con**nect**ed by tech**nol**ogy" — "connected" is /kəˈnek.tɪd/
- Drill: Reduce the unstressed syllables: say it fast 5 times

### Phrase 4: "humanity will do wonderful things"
- Natural speech: "human**i**ty [ə]l do wonderful things" — "will" becomes /əl/
- Drill: Say it 3 times at natural speed

## 💡 Key Insight for Today

> In natural spoken English, the phrase *"an internet giant"* does not sound like three separate words.  
> It sounds like: **"ə nin-tər-nət jai-ənt"** — a smooth, linked stream of sound.  
> Dictation is the only exercise that forces your brain to untangle this.

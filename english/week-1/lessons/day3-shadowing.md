# 🗣️ Day 3 — Shadowing: Articulatory Synchronization

**Technique**: Shadowing (4-stage protocol)  
**Episode**: BBC 6 Minute English — "Is the Internet Good or Bad?"  
**Total time**: ~60 minutes  
**Transcript**: [lesson-transcript.md](./lesson-transcript.md) — open and ready

---

## What Is Shadowing?

Shadowing means speaking **simultaneously** with the native speaker — echoing them with a tiny delay (0.5–1 second). You are not translating, not thinking about meaning — you are **copying sounds, rhythm, and intonation** like a vocal mirror.

> **Warning**: Your mouth will feel uncomfortable. That discomfort is your articulatory muscles learning new positions. That is exactly the goal.

---

## 🎯 Primary Target: Tim Berners-Lee's Clip (Part 4)

> *"Initially I felt the main thing an internet giant had to do was just to be neutral — just be a platform — and humanity, once connected by technology, will do wonderful things. And clearly it doesn't work like that. If you connect humanity via Wikipedia then they do produce, in general, wonderful things. If you connect people by social network where they have anonymity, then it can bring out the very nastiest of people."*

This clip is approximately 45 seconds. You will work through **4 stages**. Spend most of your time on Stages 3 and 4.

---

## Stage 1: Selective Listening — Auditory Priming (5 min)

**No transcript. No speaking. Eyes closed if possible.**

Play the Tim Berners-Lee clip 3 times continuously.

Listen for:
- The **overall melody** — does his voice rise or fall at the end of sentences?
- The **rhythm** — where does he speed up? Where does he pause?
- The **emotional tone** — does he sound hopeful, worried, analytical?
- Which words get the most **stress** and seem to "punch out"?

*Do not try to understand every word — feel the music of his speech.*

---

## Stage 2: Transcribed Echoing — Pause & Repeat (15 min)

**Open the transcript (Part 4). Read along as you listen.**

Play the clip sentence by sentence. After each sentence:
1. **Pause** the audio
2. **Read the sentence aloud** from the transcript, copying the exact rhythm and intonation you just heard
3. Repeat each sentence **3–5 times** before moving to the next

**Focus sentences** (start with these if short on time):

| Sentence                                             | Key Feature to Copy                   |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| *"Initially I felt the main thing..."*               | Stress on "main" and "just"           |
| *"...just be a platform..."*                         | Very fast weak form — "be a" → /bi ə/ |
| *"...humanity, once connected by technology..."*     | The rhythm of the long phrase         |
| *"And clearly it doesn't work like that."*           | Falling intonation at the end ↘       |
| *"...it can bring out the very nastiest of people."* | Final stress on "nastiest"            |

---

## Stage 3: Synchronous Shadowing with Text (20 min)

**Read the transcript aloud while the audio plays — simultaneously.**

You are reading AND speaking at the same time as Tim Berners-Lee speaks. Trail him by about 0.5 seconds.

**Tips:**
- If you fall behind, skip a word and catch up — don't stop
- Do not over-enunciate — match his **speed**, not dictionary pronunciation
- Notice when he swallows/reduces words (e.g., "humanity" → stress is on "MAN")
- Do this **10+ times** until it starts to feel natural

**Common mistakes to avoid:**

| Problem                         | Fix                                                |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Speaking too slowly             | Trust the rhythm — go faster, not slower           |
| Over-enunciating every syllable | Reduce unstressed syllables to schwa /ə/           |
| Stopping when lost              | Skip ahead and rejoin — native speakers don't stop |
| Translating in your head        | Focus on SOUND, not meaning                        |

---

## Stage 4: Blind Shadowing — No Transcript (15 min)

**Close / hide the transcript.**

Play the clip and shadow it from memory and pure listening.

This is the hardest stage. Your brain will panic. That panic is the exercise.

- Play the clip 5–8 times in Stage 4
- Record yourself on at least 2 of those attempts (voice memo on your phone)

**After recording**, listen back once. Ask yourself:
1. Did I match the **rhythm**? (fast/slow in the right places?)
2. Did I match the **intonation**? (rising/falling in the right places?)
3. Which word did I consistently mispronounce or miss?

Practice this section if you have extra time. Rob speaks more slowly and clearly than Tim — this builds confidence after the harder shadowing work.

## 🎤 Bonus: Shadow + Personalize

After completing all 4 stages, try this creative exercise:

1. Shadow Tim's first sentence: *"Initially I felt the main thing an internet giant had to do was just to be neutral."*
2. Then immediately **swap in your own content**:  
   *"Initially I felt the main thing a **data pipeline** had to do was just to be **reliable**."*

This bridges the gap from mimicry to real communication.

## 💡 Key Insight for Today

> Shadowing is not about understanding. It is about **training your mouth, jaw, tongue, and breath** to move in the patterns of English. Even a professional opera singer must physically train the muscles before they can perform. Speech is no different.  
> If it feels uncomfortable, you are doing it correctly.

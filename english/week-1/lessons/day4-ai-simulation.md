# 🤖 Day 4 — AI Simulation: Debate & Discussion

**Technique**: AI Simulation (Generative Practice)  
**Episode**: BBC 6 Minute English — "Is the Internet Good or Bad?"  
**Total time**: ~60 minutes  
**Tools**: ChatGPT (voice mode or text) / Gemini / Claude

---

## Why AI Simulation Today?

You have absorbed the vocabulary (Day 1), decoded the sounds (Day 2), and trained your mouth (Day 3). Now it's time to **push that knowledge into active, unpredictable output**.

An AI conversation partner creates real pressure: it asks follow-up questions, challenges your ideas, and forces you to respond on the spot — without a script.

---

## 🔴 Ground Rules for Today

1. **Speak out loud** — use voice mode if possible. Do not just type.
2. **Try to use at least 3 target words** (initially, neutral, anonymity, ameliorate, tweak, kudos) during each round.
3. **Do not write a script first** — the whole point is spontaneous output.
4. **After each round, ask for feedback** using the specific prompt below.

---

## Round 1: The Basic Debate (15 min)

Copy and paste this prompt into your AI tool:

```
Act as an intelligent, curious university student named Sam.
I want to practice discussing a BBC English topic with you.

Our topic is: "Is the internet good or bad for the world?"

Please ask me one open question to start the discussion.
After I respond, push back gently on my ideas or ask a follow-up question.

Rules:
- Keep the conversation natural and at a B1-B2 level.
- Do not correct my grammar during the conversation. 
- After 3 exchanges, stop and tell me which of these words I used correctly: 
  initially, neutral, anonymity, ameliorate, tweak, kudos.
- Also tell me one grammar pattern I used incorrectly.
```

**Your goal**: Talk for at least 3 full exchanges. Use the vocabulary.

---

## Round 2: Tim Berners-Lee's Perspective (15 min)

```
Now let's do a role-play. You will play Tim Berners-Lee, the inventor of the World Wide Web.

I will interview you about your concerns regarding the internet and social media,
specifically about anonymity, internet giants, and the concept of kudos.

Stay in character. Speak at an intermediate English level (B1-B2).
After 3-4 questions from me, break character and give me feedback on:
1. How naturally I asked questions in English
2. Any grammar mistakes I made
3. Whether I used the vocabulary from the BBC episode correctly
```

**Your goal**: Ask 3–4 good questions. You are the interviewer.

Example questions to get you started (don't just read these — adapt them):
- *"Mr. Berners-Lee, when you first created the web, what did you expect would happen?"*
- *"Do you feel that internet companies have the responsibility to stop anonymity?"*
- *"What specific changes — what 'tweaks' — would you recommend?"*

---

## Round 3: Devil's Advocate — Defend a Position (20 min)

```
I want to practice defending an argument in English. 

I will argue that the internet has been MORE GOOD than bad for the world.
You must argue the opposite — that the internet has caused serious harm.

Push back hard on my arguments. Use real-world examples.
After 4 full exchanges, grade my performance on:
- Use of linking words (however, although, on the other hand, therefore)
- Vocabulary range
- Grammatical accuracy
- How convincingly I made my case

Also suggest 2 phrases I could have used that would sound more natural or persuasive.
```

---

## Phase 2: Review & Best Sentences (10 min)

After your 3 rounds, ask the AI:

```
Looking back at everything I said in our 3 conversations today,
write the 3 best sentences I produced — ones that were natural, grammatically correct, 
and showed good vocabulary. Then write 3 alternative versions of my weakest sentences,
corrected and improved, that I can practice saying aloud.
```

**Read the improved sentences aloud 3 times each.** These are your sentences to own.

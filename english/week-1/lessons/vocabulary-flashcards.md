# 🃏 Vocabulary Flashcards — Week 1

**Episode**: BBC 6 Minute English — "Is the Internet Good or Bad?"  
**Target words**: 6 cards  
**Format**: Anki-compatible (Front / Back / Example / Context)

> **How to use**: Create these as Anki cards. Set the card type to "Basic (and reversed)" so you can be tested both ways — word → meaning AND meaning → word.

## Card 1 — initially

**Front (English prompt):**
> What word means "at first" or "in the beginning" — and implies that things changed later?

**Back (Answer):**
> **initially** /ɪˈnɪʃ.ə.li/  
> *adverb*  
> At first; in the beginning (often implies a later change)

**Episode sentence:**
> *"Initially I felt the main thing an internet giant had to do was just to be neutral."*

**Your sentence (fill in):**
> When I started using data pipelines, I initially thought the hardest part would be ____________.

**Pronunciation drill:**
> in-**NISH**-uh-lee → Say it 5 times fast.

---

## Card 2 — neutral

**Front (English prompt):**
> What adjective describes a person, company, or platform that does NOT take sides and does NOT try to control things?

**Back (Answer):**
> **neutral** /ˈnjuː.trəl/  
> *adjective*  
> Not taking sides; not exerting control or influence

**Episode sentence:**
> *"...just be a platform — and humanity, once connected by technology, will do wonderful things."*  
> *(he wanted internet giants to be neutral — just a platform)*

**Your sentence (fill in):**
> When a conflict happens in the team, I try to stay ____________ and listen to both sides.

**Collocations to remember:**
> stay neutral | remain neutral | politically neutral | a neutral platform | neutral ground

## Card 3 — anonymity

**Front (English prompt):**
> What noun describes the state of being unknown — when no one knows your real identity?

**Back (Answer):**
> **anonymity** /ˌæn.əˈnɪm.ɪ.ti/  
> *noun*  
> The condition of having a hidden or unknown identity

**Episode sentence:**
> *"If you connect people by social network where they have anonymity, then it can bring out the very nastiest of people."*

**Your sentence (fill in):**
> Some whistleblowers rely on ____________ to safely report corruption without risking their jobs.

**Related words:**
> anonymous (adjective) | anonymously (adverb) | to remain anonymous

**Pronunciation trap:**
> a-**NON**-uh-mi-tee — 5 syllables. The stress is on the second syllable: **NIM**.

## Card 4 — ameliorate

**Front (English prompt):**
> What formal verb means "to make a bad situation better"? It is more sophisticated than simply saying "improve."

**Back (Answer):**
> **ameliorate** /əˈmiː.li.ə.reɪt/  
> *verb (formal)*  
> To make a bad or unsatisfactory situation better; to improve

**Episode sentence:**
> *"...the issue could be hugely ameliorated by tweaking the way the thing works."*

**Your sentence (fill in):**
> Adding data validation steps could greatly ____________ the quality issues we see in the pipeline.

**Register note:**
> This is a **formal/academic** word. In casual conversation, you would say "improve" or "make better." Use "ameliorate" in professional writing, presentations, or to impress.

**Pronunciation:**
> uh-**MEE**-lee-uh-rayt → 5 syllables.

## Card 5 — tweak

**Front (English prompt):**
> What casual verb means "to make a small adjustment to how something works — not a major redesign, just a minor fix"?

**Back (Answer):**
> **tweak** /twiːk/  
> *verb / noun*  
> To make a small change or adjustment to something

**Episode sentence:**
> *"...the issue could be hugely ameliorated by tweaking the way the thing works — by changing the way retweets are propagated."*

**Your sentence (fill in):**
> I just need to ____________ the SQL query by adding one more filter — it shouldn't take long.

**Collocations:**
> tweak the algorithm | tweak the settings | a minor tweak | make a few tweaks

**Context note:**
> Very common in tech, data, and engineering contexts. Perfect for your daily work vocabulary.

## Card 6 — kudos

**Front (English prompt):**
> What noun (borrowed from Greek) means "praise, recognition, or appreciation given to someone for something they have achieved"?

**Back (Answer):**
> **kudos** /ˈkjuː.dɒs/  
> *noun (uncountable)*  
> Praise and recognition for an achievement; public appreciation

**Episode sentence:**
> *"Give them more kudos for being constructive, for example."*

**Your sentence (fill in):**
> Big kudos to the team for delivering the dashboard two days ahead of schedule.

**Grammar warning:**
> "Kudos" looks plural but is **uncountable** — do NOT say "a kudos" or "kudos are."  
> ✅ "Kudos to you." / "She received a lot of kudos."  
> ❌ "She received a kudos."

**Usage context:**
> Common in professional settings (Slack messages, team announcements). Also used sarcastically.

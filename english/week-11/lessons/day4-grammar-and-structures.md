## 🔍 1. Grammar Focus (3 Target Rules)

### 1. Gerunds as Subjects (Discussing complex concepts)
- **Quote:** *"Designing large-scale systems requires a lot of planning."*
- **Rule Explanation:** When we want to talk about an action or a process as the main subject of a sentence, we use the `-ing` form of the verb (a gerund). This sounds much more natural and professional than using "To [verb]".
  - **Formula:** `[Verb-ing] + [Noun/Object] + [Singular Verb] + ...`
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Using plural verbs after a gerund subject, or using the base verb.
  > - *Incorrect:* "**Design** systems **are** hard."
  > - *Correct:* "**Designing** systems **is** hard."

---

### 2. Comparative Adjectives for Role Differences
- **Quote:** *"An architect's role is much more strategic and less hands-on than an engineer's role."*
- **Rule Explanation:** When comparing two roles, tools, or concepts, we use comparatives. 
  - For long adjectives (2+ syllables): use `more / less + Adjective + than`.
  - For short adjectives (1 syllable): use `Adjective-er + than`.
  - Add intensifiers like `much` or `a lot` to emphasize the difference.
  - **Formula:** `[Subject A] is much more [Adjective] than [Subject B].`
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Using both "more" and the "-er" suffix.
  > - *Incorrect:* "An architect is **more higher** than an engineer."
  > - *Correct:* "An architect is **higher** than an engineer." or "An architect's role is **more strategic**."

---

### 3. "Need to" vs. "Have to" vs. "Must" (Career Requirements)
- **Quote:** *"You need to be less hands-on with coding."* / *"You must learn to bridge the gap."*
- **Rule Explanation:** 
  - `Need to` expresses a requirement for success or a good idea (soft necessity).
  - `Have to` expresses an external rule or policy set by the company.
  - `Must` is stronger and is often used for internal conviction or an absolute prerequisite in a mentoring context.
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Confusing the negative forms. "Don't have to" means it is optional. "Must not" means it is forbidden.
  > - *Incorrect:* "As an architect, you **must not** code every day." (It is forbidden to code).
  > - *Correct:* "As an architect, you **don't have to** code every day." (It is optional/not required).

## 🧱 2. Sentence Structure Templates (5 Patterns)

### Template 1: Expressing a career goal
- **Communicative Function:** Stating your intention to move into a new role.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `I'm looking to transition into a [Role Name] role over the next [Time Period].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "I'm looking to transition into a management role over the next year."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "I'm looking to transition into a Data Architect role over the next year."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "I'm looking to transition into a product owner role over the next few months."

---

### Template 2: Contrasting two responsibilities
- **Communicative Function:** Explaining the difference between two jobs using comparatives.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `A [Role A] focuses on [Verb-ing], but a [Role B] is much more [Adjective].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "A cook focuses on following recipes, but a head chef is much more creative."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "An engineer focuses on building pipelines, but an architect is much more strategic."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "A salesperson focuses on pitching, but a manager is much more analytical."

---

### Template 3: Identifying the core challenge (Gerunds)
- **Communicative Function:** Stating what action is the most difficult or important.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `[Verb-ing] the gap between [Group A] and [Group B] requires [Skill/Noun].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "Bridging the gap between parents and teenagers requires patience."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "Bridging the gap between business needs and technical systems requires clear communication."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "Bridging the gap between marketing and sales requires regular meetings."

---

### Template 4: Defining the scope of work
- **Communicative Function:** Explaining what you need to focus on to succeed.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `Instead of just [Verb-ing], you need to look at the big picture and [Base Verb].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "Instead of just complaining, you need to look at the big picture and find a solution."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "Instead of just writing code, you need to look at the big picture and make architectural decisions."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "Instead of just fixing bugs, you need to look at the big picture and improve the software design."

---

### Template 5: Asking for mentorship/advice
- **Communicative Function:** Politely asking a senior colleague for guidance.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `How can I start preparing for that? Do you have any advice on [Verb-ing]...?`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "How can I start preparing for that? Do you have any advice on managing personal finances?"
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "How can I start preparing for that? Do you have any advice on designing scalable systems?"
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "How can I start preparing for that? Do you have any advice on leading a team meeting?"

## 🛠️ 4. Spot the Mistake (Error Correction Drill)
*Identify the grammatical or vocabulary error in each sentence, and rewrite it correctly.*

1. "Design the cloud architecture are very difficult."
   - *Your Correction*: ____________________________________________________________________
2. "As an architect, you are much more higher level than an engineer."
   - *Your Correction*: ____________________________________________________________________
3. "Instead to write code, you need to look at the big picture."
   - *Your Correction*: ____________________________________________________________________

<details>
<summary>🔑 Click to reveal Answer Key & Explanations</summary>

1. **Incorrect**: "**Design** the cloud architecture **are** very difficult."
   - **Correct**: "**Designing** the cloud architecture **is** very difficult."
   - **Why**: Use the gerund (-ing) as the subject, and it takes a singular verb ("is").
2. **Incorrect**: "...much **more higher** level..."
   - **Correct**: "As an architect, you are at a **much higher** level than an engineer." OR "...you are **much more strategic**."
   - **Why**: Do not use "more" with "-er" adjectives. "High" becomes "higher". 
3. **Incorrect**: "Instead **to write** code..."
   - **Correct**: "Instead **of writing** code, you need to look at the big picture."
   - **Why**: The preposition "of" must be followed by a gerund ("writing").
</details>

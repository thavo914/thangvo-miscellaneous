# Vocabulary Definitions: Batch 5 (28-33)

**28. it's still being written**
*(phrase)*: a metaphorical way to say that a situation, legacy, or outcome is not yet finalized and can still change or develop. *(Vietnamese: mọi thứ vẫn đang tiếp diễn / câu chuyện vẫn chưa kết thúc)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. People are calling the new pipeline a failure, but we just launched phase one—its story is still being written.
2. My marathon block started terribly with an injury, but race day is two months away, so the outcome is still being written.
3. The history of the new manager's time at Liverpool is still being written, so it's too early to judge his tactics.
4. Don't worry if your career as a data engineer feels slow right now; your professional journey is still being written.
5. We are currently trailing by a goal at halftime, but the story of this match is still being written.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **The story isn't over yet** — very natural and common.
- **The jury is still out** — idiom meaning a final decision or outcome hasn't been reached.
- **It's a work in progress** — focuses more on the ongoing effort.
- **It remains to be seen** — slightly more formal, meaning the future is unknown.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **The book is closed on that** — means the situation is completely finalized and unchangeable.
- **It's a done deal** — completed and finalized.
- **The dust has settled** — the situation is stable and the outcome is clear.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> Our start to the season has been poor, but the final league table ______________________.

> The architecture is full of bugs right now, but the final version of this data platform ______________________.

**29. I have not caught any of the matches**
*(phrase)*: a natural conversational way to say you haven't watched any of the sports games recently. *(Vietnamese: tôi chưa xem được trận đấu nào cả)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. I've been so busy migrating the data warehouse to the cloud that I have not caught any of the matches this weekend.
2. With my marathon training peaking, I'm out running every Saturday afternoon, so I have not caught any of the matches recently.
3. I used to watch Liverpool every week, but with the new time zones, I have not caught any of the matches this month.
4. To be honest, I have not caught any of the matches lately because I've been spending my evenings studying for a certification.
5. The guys at the office were talking about the Champions League, but I had to admit I have not caught any of the matches.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **I haven't watched any of the games** — slightly more literal but just as common.
- **I've missed all the fixtures** — a bit more British/football-specific.
- **I haven't been following the games** — implies not even checking the scores.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **I've tuned in to every single game** — actively watching everything.
- **I've caught all the highlights** — even if not watching live, keeping fully up to date.
- **I haven't missed a minute** — watching obsessively.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**

> I've been on call for server emergencies all week, so ______________________.

**30. high stakes game with the heavy hitters**
*(phrase)*: a highly important, risky, or significant situation involving the most powerful, influential, or skilled people in a particular field. *(Vietnamese: cuộc chơi lớn/rủi ro cao với những nhân vật tầm cỡ)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Presenting the new data strategy to the board of directors is a high stakes game with the heavy hitters of the company.
2. The Boston Marathon isn't just a race; for elite athletes, it's a high stakes game with the heavy hitters of the running world.
3. The Champions League semi-final against Real Madrid is always a high stakes game with the heavy hitters of European football.
4. Negotiating the enterprise cloud contract is a high stakes game with the heavy hitters from AWS and Microsoft.
5. In the transfer window, bidding for a £100m midfielder is a high stakes game with the heavy hitters where one mistake costs a fortune.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Playing in the big leagues** — implies operating at the highest level.
- **A critical situation with the top brass** — more corporate/military.
- **A high-pressure scenario with top-tier players** — descriptive and clear.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **A low stakes environment** — a situation with very little risk or pressure.
- **Playing with the amateurs** — dealing with inexperienced or less powerful people.
- **A friendly knockabout** — British idiom for a casual, pressure-free game or situation.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> Defending our database architecture in front of the global CTO feels like a ______________________.

> Competing in the Olympic trials is a ______________________ of track and field.

**31. click when it counts**
*(phrase)*: to suddenly work perfectly, harmonize, or become effective precisely at the most important or critical moment. *(Vietnamese: tỏa sáng đúng lúc / kết nối/hiệu quả đúng thời điểm quan trọng)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. The new ETL pipeline had bugs all week, but thankfully it managed to click when it counts during the live Black Friday launch.
2. My training block was a mess, but my pacing and nutrition managed to click when it counts on race day.
3. The Liverpool forwards looked disjointed in the first half, but they managed to click when it counts to score the winning goal in the 90th minute.
4. Your presentation might feel rough during practice, but I know you'll make it click when it counts in front of the stakeholders.
5. You can have all the talent in the world, but a great team has to click when it counts in the knockout stages.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Come together at the right time** — very natural and common.
- **Deliver when the pressure is on** — focuses on performance under stress.
- **Hit top gear when it matters most** — a more dynamic, sports-oriented idiom.
- **Step up to the plate** — an idiom meaning to take responsibility and perform when needed.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Choke under pressure** — failing exactly when it is most important.
- **Fall apart when it matters** — systems or people breaking down at the critical moment.
- **Fail to show up** — figuratively meaning not performing well in a big event.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> We had a terrible pre-season, but the manager trusts the squad will ______________________.

> The data migration scripts were failing in staging, but they absolutely have to ______________________ in production.

**32. talent on paper**
*(noun phrase)*: the perceived ability or quality of a person or team based purely on their resume, statistics, or reputation, which may not reflect their actual real-world performance. *(Vietnamese: tài năng trên giấy tờ / giỏi theo lý thuyết)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. The new software engineer had an incredible resume and great talent on paper, but he struggled to write efficient SQL queries in reality.
2. A runner might have amazing talent on paper based on their VO2 max, but marathon racing also requires mental toughness.
3. That rival team has incredible talent on paper, but they lack the chemistry and work rate of a Klopp side.
4. The architecture design looked like pure talent on paper, but when we built it, it couldn't handle the data volume.
5. You can buy all the talent on paper you want, but you still have to build a team that fights for each other.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Great in theory** — applies more to ideas or plans rather than people.
- **Hypothetical ability** — a very literal and formal phrase.
- **Good credentials** — focuses on the resume aspect.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Proven track record** — actual evidence of success in the real world.
- **Practical experience** — hands-on ability rather than theoretical knowledge.
- **Street smarts** — practical, real-world intelligence rather than book learning.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> Having a master's degree shows you have ______________________, but can you debug a broken pipeline at 3 AM?

> Manchester United always seems to buy an incredible amount of ______________________, but they fail to perform as a cohesive unit.

**33. have a roster full of superstars**
*(phrase)*: to have a team or group comprised entirely of highly talented, famous, or elite individuals. *(Vietnamese: sở hữu một đội hình toàn siêu sao)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. You don't need to have a roster full of superstars to build a great data engineering team; you just need people who collaborate well.
2. Real Madrid might have a roster full of superstars, but Liverpool's high-pressing system can still beat them.
3. The Kenyan national team essentially has a roster full of superstars when it comes to long-distance running.
4. The big tech companies have a roster full of superstars, but our small startup ships features twice as fast.
5. Even if you have a roster full of superstars, a bad manager can still ruin the team's morale.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **A star-studded lineup** — very common in sports and entertainment.
- **A team of galácticos** — football-specific slang for a team of expensive superstars.
- **An all-star team** — implies everyone is the best in their position.
- **An elite squad** — slightly more formal or military-sounding.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **A team of underdogs** — a group not expected to win or full of unknown people.
- **A squad of average players** — a mediocre group.
- **A ragtag group** — a disorganized group of varied, often lower-tier individuals.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> I prefer working at a startup where everyone grinds together, rather than a giant corporation that claims to ______________________.

> It's difficult to manage egos when you ______________________ in the dressing room.

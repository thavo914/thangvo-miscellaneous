## 🗣️ 1. IELTS Speaking Practice Questions

### Part 1: Interview (Skills & Learning)
1. *Do you prefer working on hands-on coding tasks, or planning the overall design of a project?*
- Currently, I spend most of my time on hands-on coding. However, as I gain more experience, I am starting to prefer planning the overall design. Looking at the big picture is much more challenging but also more rewarding.
- I enjoy both. Writing code gives me immediate satisfaction, but making architectural decisions allows me to have a larger impact on the company's data strategy.
**Your Turn:**
- I prefer working on hands-on coding tasks. I love turning a raw idea into a working program.
- At the beginning of my career, I preferred hands-on coding tasks, it made me feel excited and confident when finishing a ticket. But now, making architectural decisions for my projects is more interesting for me.
- I prefer planning the overall design of a project, it pushes me to think more strategically and consider the big picture.
2. *How do you usually learn new technical skills for your job?*
- Mostly through self-study and reading documentation. But when I want to learn about scalability or system design, I often ask a senior architect to mentor me. They help me bridge the gap between theory and real-world application.
- I usually try to build small projects on my own. But recently, I've started shadowing our Lead Data Architect because I am looking to transition into that role in the future.
**Your Turn:**
- I usually learn new technical skills by building small projects on my own, it is a good way for me to understand the concepts deeply.
- I usually learn new technical skills from reading e-books and documentation, and also through online courses. But to deeply understand the concepts, I usually try to build small projects on my own.
- Mostly, I learn new technical skills from handling more complex and challenging tasks at work. It makes me think out of the box and find the best solutions for the problems.
3. *Do you think communication skills are important for people working in IT?*
- Absolutely. Technical skills are not enough. You need to be able to explain complex ideas to non-technical stakeholders. If you can't bridge the gap between engineering and business, you won't be able to lead big projects.
**Your Turn:**
- Absolutely. Many engineers think communication skills are not important, but they are wrong. When you want to be a senior engineer, you have to communicate with different stakeholders and explain complex ideas to non-technical stakeholders. 
- Obviously, if you can't communicate well, you can't work effectively in a team. Technical skills are not enough if you can't convey your ideas to others.
- It depends on the roles. If you work as an engineer, technical skills are the most important. If you have good communication skills, it's good, but if you are not good at it, I think it's not a big deal because you can improve it over time.

### Part 2: Cue Card (Long Turn)
> **Describe a time when you asked a senior colleague for career advice or help with a difficult concept.**
> 
> You should say:
> - Who you asked and what their job was
> - What you wanted to know
> - What advice they gave you
> - And explain how this advice helped you in your career.

**Model Idea for Data Engineers:**
I'd like to talk about a conversation I had with our Lead Data Architect, Michael, about three months ago. At that time, I was feeling a bit stuck in my Data Engineer role and wanted to understand how to transition into a Data Architecture position. We grabbed a coffee, and I asked him how I could start preparing for that career jump. 

He gave me some excellent advice. He explained that instead of just focusing on writing clean code, I needed to look at the big picture. He emphasized that designing systems requires a strong understanding of scalability and security. His most memorable tip was that an architect must bridge the gap between what the business wants and what the engineering team can actually build. This advice completely shifted my perspective. Now, instead of just taking tickets from the backlog, I try to participate in system design meetings, which has made my daily work much more strategic.
**Your Turn:**
- I'd like to talk about a time when I asked a Solution Architect for career advice. At that time, I was a database developer feeling stuck in my role and I wanted to transition into a data engineer role to adapt to the new trend of data. The project I was working on only used the legacy technology like SQL Server, SSIS, Stored Procedure on premise. These tech stacks were not trending anymore and I was afraid that I would be outdated to the market. So I asked him what should I do to overcome this situation. He advised me that I should spend time to learn about big data technology like hadoop, spark, kafka, hive, and so on. He said that I have excellent SQL skill, it's the foundation for learning big data technology. He also emphasized that I should look at the big picture, understand the business requirements, and be able to bridge the gap between business and engineering. That advice absolutely changed my mindset, from then on, I started to build small data engineering projects on my own to practice and improve my skills. I also joined a POC project to validate the big data technology to prove that it was a good direction for the company. It was a great experience for me. Now I am a data engineer and I am happy with my job.



### Part 3: Analytical Discussion (Deeper Questions)
1. *In your opinion, what makes a technology leader successful?*
 - A successful technology leader needs to be much more strategic than a regular developer. They don't have to write code every day, but they must understand the architecture deeply. More importantly, they need excellent communication skills to bridge the gap between different departments.
 **Your turn:**
 - In my opinion, a successful technology leader needs to be a good mentor and coach for their team members. They should have deep understanding about technology and business knowledge to effectively coach their team members and make strategic decisions. Besides, they also need to be able to build trust and motivate their team members to achieve common goals.
 - I think the technology leader should have deep understanding about technology and business knowledge to make strategic decisions and effectively coach their team members. They should also be good at problem solving and communication skills to bridge the gap between business and engineering.
2. *How do you think the roles in data engineering will change in the next 5 to 10 years?*
 - With the rise of AI and automated coding tools, I believe standard coding tasks will become much easier. Therefore, data professionals will need to focus more on high-level architecture, scalability, and data governance. The ability to make solid architectural decisions will be more valuable than just knowing a specific programming language.
**Your turn**
- With the rise of AI and automated coding tools, I believe standard coding tasks will become much easier. The data engineers should focus more on high-level architecture, scalability, and data governance instead of just writing code.
- AI nowadays is really powerful and it can write effective code. So the data engineers who only focuses on writing code will be replaced by AI in the future. They should take on more responsibilities like data analysis, data modeling, and so on. I think Data AI Engineer will be the trend in the future.

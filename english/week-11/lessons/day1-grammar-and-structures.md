# Day 1: Grammar & Structures (Week 11 - Topic 1 Focus)

This file houses the grammar rules, sentence templates, and drills for the first half of Week 11, focusing on **Topic 1: Getting a Promotion to Senior Data Engineer (Level 3, Private Apartment, 25% Salary Increase)**.

---

## 🔍 1. Grammar Focus (3 Target Rules)

### 1. First Conditional (Negotiating and Discussing Future Benefits)
- **Quote:** *"If you move to Level 3, you will get a private apartment."*
- **Rule Explanation:** We use the First Conditional to talk about real and possible situations in the future. It is highly effective in negotiations or performance reviews.
  - **Formula:** `If + [Subject] + [Present Simple], [Subject] + will + [Base Verb]`
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Avoid using "will" in the "if" clause.
  > - *Incorrect:* "If I **will be** promoted to Level 3, I will take on more responsibility."
  > - *Correct:* "If I **am** promoted to Level 3, I **will take** on more responsibility."

---

### 2. Prepositions of Percentage and Change
- **Quote:** *"Your base pay will increase by 25%."* / *"We are planning to promote you to Level 3."*
- **Rule Explanation:** 
  - Use **by** to indicate the amount of change (e.g., increase/decrease *by* 25%).
  - Use **to** to indicate the final destination or level (e.g., promote *to* Level 3, transition *to* Senior).
  - Use **at** to indicate the current state (e.g., My salary is currently *at* market rate).
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Confusing "by" and "to" when discussing salary changes.
  > - *Incorrect:* "My salary increased **to** 25%." (Means your total salary is now 25% of what it used to be)
  > - *Correct:* "My salary increased **by** 25%."

---

### 3. Modals of Obligation and Expectation ("be expected to")
- **Quote:** *"As a Senior Data Engineer, you are expected to take on more responsibility."*
- **Rule Explanation:** Instead of saying "you must" (which sounds too bossy/aggressive), in professional settings, we use "be expected to" to describe the duties and requirements of a role.
  - **Formula:** `[Subject] + am/is/are + expected to + [Base Verb]`
- **Common Pitfall Box:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Mistake:** Forgetting the verb "be" or the "d" in expected.
  > - *Incorrect:* "I **expect to** leading the team."
  > - *Correct:* "I **am expected to lead** the team."

## 🧱 2. Sentence Structure Templates (5 Patterns)

### Template 1: Expressing readiness for a new challenge
- **Communicative Function:** Professionally stating you are prepared for a promotion.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `I feel ready to + [Verb Phrase] + and + [Verb Phrase].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "I feel ready to buy a house and settle down."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "I feel ready to step up and take on more responsibility."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "I feel ready to manage a team and handle bigger projects."

---

### Template 2: Discussing conditions of a promotion (First Conditional)
- **Communicative Function:** Confirming what happens if a career goal is met.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `If I + [Present Simple], will I + [Base Verb] + ?`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "If I finish this early, will I be able to leave?"
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "If I am promoted to Level 3, will I receive a housing allowance?"
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "If I take on this new project, will I get a salary bump?"

---

### Template 3: Highlighting numerical growth
- **Communicative Function:** Discussing salary or performance metrics clearly.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `[Metric] + will increase by + [Percentage/Amount].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "My rent will increase by 10% next year."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "My base salary will increase by 25%."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "Our quarterly target will increase by 50 units."

---

### Template 4: Describing new role expectations
- **Communicative Function:** Outlining what a new job title requires.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `In this role, [Subject] + is/are expected to + [Base Verb].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "In this role, you are expected to be available on weekends."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "In this role, you are expected to make architectural decisions."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "In this role, managers are expected to mentor junior staff."

---

### Template 5: Showing excitement for specific benefits
- **Communicative Function:** Reacting positively to a job offer or promotion package.
- **Slot-Filling Formula:** `A [Benefit] and a [Benefit]? That's amazing! I'm completely ready to + [Base Verb].`
- **Examples:**
  1. *Everyday Context:* "A free gym membership and flexible hours? That's amazing! I'm completely ready to start."
  2. *Data Engineering / Casino Context:* "A 25% salary bump and a private apartment? That's amazing! I'm completely ready to lead the team."
  3. *General Workplace Context:* "A bonus scheme and extra vacation days? That's amazing! I'm completely ready to sign the contract."

<details>
<summary>🔑 Click to reveal Answer Key & Explanations</summary>

1. **Incorrect**: "If I **will get** promoted..."
   - **Correct**: "If I **get** promoted to Level 3, I will get a private apartment."
   - **Why**: The First Conditional requires Present Simple in the "if" clause.
2. **Incorrect**: "...increased **to** 25%..."
   - **Correct**: "My salary has increased **by** 25% this year."
   - **Why**: Use "by" to show the amount of change. Using "to" means your salary dropped to only 25% of its total.
3. **Incorrect**: "...I **expect to reviewing**..."
   - **Correct**: "As a Senior, I **am expected to review** the team's code."
   - **Why**: Passive voice (`be expected to`) is needed to describe responsibilities, and it must be followed by the base verb.

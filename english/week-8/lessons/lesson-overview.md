## Learning Objectives

| Category | Objective |
| --- | --- |
| Listening | Understand the main ideas and specific details in discussions about conspiracy theories. |
| Vocabulary | Master 6 target words/idioms related to conspiracy theories and evaluation. |
| Pronunciation | Practice connected speech, stress, and intonation using the shadowing technique. |

| Thinking | Formulate structured arguments, and explain technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders using analogies. |

## Target Vocabulary

| Word | POS | Meaning | Episode Sentence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **down the rabbit hole** | idiom | enter a situation which seems interesting and uncomplicated at first but ends up becoming strange, confusing and hard to escape from | It looks like reading online conspiracies has sent Neil down the rabbit hole. |
| **pretext** | noun | pretend reason for doing something that is used to hide the real reason | Some conspiracies claim that Bill Gates wants to implant microchips in people and that he’s using the coronavirus as a pretext. |
| **bonkers** | adjective | silly, stupid or crazy (informal) | Some of the claims are bonkers. |
| **disdain** | noun | disliking something because you feel it does not deserve your attention or respect | We should treat them with the disdain they deserve. |
| **get to the bottom of** | idiom | discover the real but sometimes hidden reason why something happens | So we need to get to the bottom of these claims. |
| **sweeping** | adjective | something spoken or written about that is too general and does not carefully consider all the relevant fact | A good way to engage people in discussion is to avoid sweeping claims or statements. |

## Ground Rules
1. **One artifact, many angles** - apply every technique to this same episode.
2. **Record your output** - voice memos count. You are your own evaluator.
3. **Resist translation** - describe unknown words in English first.
4. **Quantity beats quality** - 3 imperfect recordings beat 1 over-rehearsed one.

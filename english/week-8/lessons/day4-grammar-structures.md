# Day 4: Grammar Structures

## 1. Modal Verb for Deduction
- **Quote:** "I mean, that must be fake news!"
- **Function:** To express strong belief or certainty that something is true based on evidence.
- **Template:** [Subject] + must be + [noun/adjective]
- **Examples:**
  - The servers are completely down; that must be a network issue.
  - He's been working on that data pipeline for 12 hours; he must be exhausted.

## 2. Conditionals with 'unless'
- **Quote:** "Unless it was the giant lizards controlling the government!"
- **Function:** To introduce an exception to a statement (meaning 'if not').
- **Template:** [Statement] + unless + [subject] + [verb]
- **Examples:**
  - The job will fail unless we increase the memory allocation.
  - I won't believe it unless I see the raw data myself.

## 3. Speculation with 'It looks like'
- **Quote:** "It looks like reading online conspiracies has sent Neil down the rabbit hole."
- **Function:** To make a deduction based on what you can see or understand from a situation.
- **Template:** It looks like + [subject] + [verb]
- **Examples:**
  - It looks like the new marketing campaign is driving more traffic to the site.
  - It looks like we'll need to refactor this code before the next release.

## 4. Phrasal verb 'ends up' + gerund
- **Quote:** "...ends up becoming strange, confusing and hard to escape from."
- **Function:** To describe the final result of a situation or series of events, often unexpected.
- **Template:** [Subject] + ends up / ended up + [verb-ing]
- **Examples:**
  - We started trying to fix a small bug, but we ended up rewriting the whole module.
  - He didn't want to go to the casino, but he ended up staying there all night.

## 5. Relative Clauses as Subject
- **Quote:** "Somebody who’s the target of many conspiracy theories is Microsoft’s Bill Gates."
- **Function:** To highlight or focus on a specific person or thing before revealing who/what it is.
- **Template:** [Noun/Pronoun] + who/which/that + [verb phrase] + is + [subject complement]
- **Examples:**
  - Something that causes a lot of performance issues is poorly optimized SQL queries.
  - A tool which has been incredibly useful for our team is Apache Kafka.

## 6. Introductory 'It' for Observation
- **Quote:** "...it’s not hard to see how people looking for answers can get sucked down online rabbit holes."
- **Function:** To introduce a general observation or opinion impersonally.
- **Template:** It is + [adjective] + to see how / to understand why + [clause]
- **Examples:**
  - It is easy to see how a small data error can compound over time.
  - It's not hard to understand why players get frustrated with slow payouts.

## 7. Preposition 'by' + Gerund for Method/Result
- **Quote:** "And by doing so calmly and neutrally you might persuade them to reconsider."
- **Function:** To explain the method by which a result is achieved.
- **Template:** By + [verb-ing] + [adverb/object], [subject] + [verb]
- **Examples:**
  - By automating the ETL process, we saved the team 10 hours a week.
  - By speaking to stakeholders early, you can avoid sweeping misunderstandings later.

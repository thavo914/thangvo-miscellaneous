# Vocabulary Flashcards

*Instructions: Test yourself. Look at the definition on the front, try to remember the word, then check the back.*

---

**Front:**
What is an idiom that means to enter a situation which seems interesting and uncomplicated at first but ends up becoming strange, confusing and hard to escape from?
<!-- slide -->
**Back:**
**down the rabbit hole** /daʊn ðə ˈræbɪt həʊl/ | idiom | enter a situation which seems interesting at first but ends up confusing
*Quote:* "It looks like reading online conspiracies has sent Neil down the rabbit hole."
*My Sentence:* I started reading about database optimization and went down a rabbit hole for three hours.

---

**Front:**
What is a noun that means a pretend reason for doing something that is used to hide the real reason?
<!-- slide -->
**Back:**
**pretext** /ˈpriːtekst/ | noun | pretend reason used to hide the real reason
*Quote:* "...that he wants to use the virus as a pretext to microchip everyone in the world."
*My Sentence:* They used the server upgrade as a pretext to cut the project budget.

---

**Front:**
What is an informal adjective that means silly, stupid or crazy?
<!-- slide -->
**Back:**
**bonkers** /ˈbɒŋkəz/ | adjective | silly, stupid or crazy
*Quote:* "Some of the claims are bonkers."
*My Sentence:* It's bonkers that we are still using this legacy system in 2024.

---

**Front:**
What is a noun that means disliking something because you feel it does not deserve your attention or respect?
<!-- slide -->
**Back:**
**disdain** /dɪsˈdeɪn/ | noun | dislike because unworthy of attention/respect
*Quote:* "We should treat them with the disdain they deserve."
*My Sentence:* The senior architect looked at my proposed architecture with disdain.

---

**Front:**
What is an idiom that means to discover the real but sometimes hidden reason why something happens?
<!-- slide -->
**Back:**
**get to the bottom of** /get tə ðə ˈbɒtəm əv/ | idiom | discover the real reason behind something
*Quote:* "So we need to get to the bottom of these claims."
*My Sentence:* We need to get to the bottom of why the ETL job is failing every Tuesday.

---

**Front:**
What is an adjective used to describe statements that are too general and do not carefully consider all the relevant facts?
<!-- slide -->
**Back:**
**sweeping** /ˈswiːpɪŋ/ | adjective | too general, not considering all facts
*Quote:* "A good way to engage people in discussion is to avoid sweeping claims or statements."
*My Sentence:* The new manager made some sweeping changes without understanding the team's workflow.

# Vocabulary Definitions: Batch 2 (6-12)

**6. I'll head out after 5:30 pm to avoid the heat**
*(phrase)*: a casual statement expressing the intention to leave a place (usually home or work) later in the afternoon or early evening when temperatures drop, often used to plan outdoor activities. *(Vietnamese: tôi sẽ ra ngoài sau 5:30 chiều để tránh nóng)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. I know I have a 15k tempo run today, but I'll head out after 5:30 pm to avoid the heat and humidity.
2. I'm working from the coffee shop today, but the sun is glaring through the window, so I'll head out after 5:30 pm to avoid the heat on my walk home.
3. We're meeting at the pub to watch the pre-season friendly, but I'll head out after 5:30 pm to avoid the heat on the train.
4. The summer training block is brutal; I always tell my running group I'll head out after 5:30 pm to avoid the heat.
5. Since the database migration is stable, I'm logging off early, though I'll head out after 5:30 pm to avoid the heat during my commute.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **I'll leave later when it cools down** — very natural and conversational.
- **I'll wait until the evening to go out** — slightly more formal.
- **I'm heading out once the sun goes down** — a bit more descriptive.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **I'll brave the heat and go now** — indicates a willingness to face the hot weather.
- **I'm heading out in the midday sun** — exact opposite action.

**7. reset button for my mind**
*(noun phrase)*: an activity or experience that completely clears one's head, relieving stress and allowing one to start fresh with renewed focus. *(Vietnamese: nút khởi động lại cho tâm trí/cách giải tỏa đầu óc)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Going for a slow, easy 10k run on a Sunday morning is the ultimate reset button for my mind.
2. After staring at complex SQL joins for eight hours, taking a walk without my phone acts as a reset button for my mind.
3. Watching a classic Steven Gerrard highlight reel on YouTube is a great reset button for my mind after a stressful week.
4. I was so anxious about the upcoming marathon, but that final taper run was a real reset button for my mind.
5. When the data pipeline fails at 3 AM, sometimes a quick shower is the only reset button for my mind before I start debugging.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **A mental palate cleanser** — a bit more metaphorical and sophisticated.
- **A way to clear my head** — very common and natural.
- **A mental break** — simpler and more direct.
- **A way to decompress** — focuses on relieving pressure.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **A source of stress** — something that adds mental burden.
- **Mental clutter** — things that confuse or overwhelm the mind.
- **Brain drain** — an activity that exhausts mental energy.

**8. we're on the same page**
*(idiom)*: to have a shared understanding of a situation, agreement on a plan, or to be thinking in a similar way. *(Vietnamese: chúng ta có cùng quan điểm/hiểu ý nhau)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Before we start building this data model, let's review the schema to make sure we're on the same page.
2. My coach and I had a long chat about my target marathon pace, and I'm glad we're on the same page now.
3. Some fans want a new striker, but I think most of us are on the same page that we need a defensive midfielder first.
4. I just sent out the meeting minutes to the stakeholders to ensure we're on the same page regarding the project timeline.
5. If we're going to do this interval session together, let's make sure we're on the same page about the recovery times.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **In agreement** — more formal and direct.
- **On the same wavelength** — slightly more informal, implies a deep mental connection or shared vibe.
- **Seeing eye to eye** — emphasizes agreeing on a specific topic or dispute.
- **In sync** — focuses on coordinated timing or understanding.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Talking at cross purposes** — misunderstanding each other completely.
- **On completely different wavelengths** — having totally different perspectives.
- **At odds** — in conflict or disagreement.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> I want to discuss the new dashboard requirements with the marketing team just to make sure ______________________.

> Before we commit to running the 50k ultra, let's make sure ______________________ about the training commitment.

**10. intuitive**
*(adjective)*: easy to understand or operate without explicit instruction; acting on instinct or natural feeling rather than conscious reasoning. *(Vietnamese: trực quan, dễ hiểu / thuộc về trực giác)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. The new BI tool we adopted is incredibly intuitive; even the non-technical stakeholders can build their own reports now.
2. Pacing a race by feel rather than staring at your watch requires a very intuitive understanding of your own body.
3. Firmino was such an intuitive player; he always knew exactly where Salah and Mané were going to run without even looking.
4. Writing dbt models feels much more intuitive to me than writing raw, nested SQL queries.
5. Sometimes you have to ignore the training plan and make an intuitive decision to rest if your legs feel unusually heavy.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Instinctive** — driven by natural instinct rather than thought.
- **User-friendly** — specific to software/tools that are easy to use.
- **Straightforward** — easy to grasp, uncomplicated.
- **Natural** — feels normal and not forced.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Counterintuitive** — goes against what you would naturally expect or guess.
- **Clunky / Complicated** — for software, hard to figure out.
- **Calculated** — carefully planned and reasoned out rather than felt.

**11. our call almost up**
*(phrase)*: a conversational way to indicate that the scheduled time for a meeting or phone call is nearly finished. *(Vietnamese: cuộc gọi/họp của chúng ta sắp hết giờ rồi)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. I see our call almost up, so let's quickly summarize the action items for the data migration.
2. Our call almost up, but before we drop, did you see that ridiculous goal Mo Salah scored last night?
3. With our call almost up, let's finalize what time we're meeting for the track session tomorrow.
4. I know our call almost up, but I really need two minutes to show you this bug in the Airflow DAG.
5. Since our call almost up, we can table the discussion on cloud costs until next week's sync.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **We're almost out of time** — very common and natural.
- **We're approaching the top of the hour** — corporate/professional slang.
- **Our time is almost up** — slightly more formal but similar.
- **We have to wrap up soon** — focuses on finishing the current task.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **We have plenty of time** — indicating no rush.
- **We've just started** — beginning of the call.
- **Let's extend the meeting** — adding more time.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> I know ______________________, but can we quickly discuss the failed pipeline run?

> With ______________________, let's quickly confirm who is buying the tickets for the Anfield tour.

**12. hit the spot**
*(idiom)*: to be exactly what is needed or wanted, especially regarding food or drink that is very satisfying. *(Vietnamese: đúng thứ đang thèm / ngon đúng điệu)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. After running 25 kilometers in the blazing heat, that ice-cold coconut water really hit the spot.
2. We were debugging until midnight, and the late-night pizza delivery completely hit the spot.
3. Celebrating a 90th-minute winner at the pub with a cold pint of beer just absolutely hits the spot.
4. A hot cup of coffee hits the spot perfectly when you're reviewing a massive pull request at 7 AM.
5. I was so hungry after the track workout; that Shredded Chicken Rice hit the spot like nothing else.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Was exactly what I needed** — a direct, literal translation.
- **Went down a treat** — British idiom, often used for food/drinks.
- **Was perfect** — simple and common.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Missed the mark** — failed to achieve the desired result or satisfy.
- **Was underwhelming** — disappointing, not as good as expected.
- **Left a bad taste in my mouth** — can be literal or metaphorical for a bad experience.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> When you finish a freezing winter run, a hot bowl of pho really ______________________.

> That cold beer after deploying the code to production ______________________.

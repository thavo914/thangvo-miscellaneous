# Vocabulary Definitions: Batch 1 (1-5)

**1. crash and do nothing**
*(verb phrase)*: to completely relax and rest, usually by doing absolutely no activities, after a period of intense work or physical exertion. *(Vietnamese: nằm ườn ra và không làm gì cả)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. After spending the whole week debugging that messy Spark job, I plan to just crash and do nothing this Saturday.
2. When I finish my 30km long run on Sunday mornings, my only goal for the afternoon is to crash and do nothing.
3. Watching Liverpool drop points in the last minute completely drained me; I just wanted to crash and do nothing for the rest of the day.
4. We finally deployed the new data warehouse architecture, so the whole team is ready to crash and do nothing over the long weekend.
5. Sometimes you need to listen to your body and just crash and do nothing instead of pushing through a recovery run.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Vegetate** — informal; implies sitting around without mental or physical effort.
- **Chill out entirely** — casual and common.
- **Unwind completely** — slightly more formal but still natural.
- **Do absolutely nothing** — emphasizes the total lack of activity.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Keep busy** — actively doing tasks.
- **Be on the go** — constantly moving or working.
- **Stay active** — maintaining physical or mental engagement.

**2. keep an eye out and jump in**
*(phrase)*: to remain vigilant or observant for an opportunity or problem, and then take immediate action or offer help when needed. *(Vietnamese: để mắt tới và can thiệp/nhảy vào giúp đỡ)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. I told the junior engineers to run the pipeline, but I'll keep an eye out and jump in if any tasks fail.
2. When Trent goes forward, Fabinho used to always keep an eye out and jump in to cover the space he left behind.
3. I'm pacing my friend for his first half marathon, so I'll keep an eye out and jump in with encouragement when he starts to struggle.
4. We are monitoring the live dashboard during the Black Friday sale; we need to keep an eye out and jump in if the latency spikes.
5. The transfer window is closing soon, so we need to keep an eye out and jump in if a good midfielder becomes available.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Watch closely and intervene** — more formal.
- **Monitor the situation and step in** — professional, good for work context.
- **Look out for and assist** — slightly less dynamic but means the same.
- **Stay alert and take action** — emphasizes readiness.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Turn a blind eye** — ignoring a problem intentionally.
- **Stay out of it** — deciding not to get involved.
- **Let things run their course** — observing without interfering.

**3. it's a tough cycle**
*(phrase)*: a repeating series of difficult or challenging events that are hard to break away from or resolve. *(Vietnamese: đó là một vòng lặp khó khăn/mệt mỏi)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. You fix one bug, and two more appear in the downstream models—it's a tough cycle to break without a full refactor.
2. Getting injured, losing fitness, and then trying to rebuild it from scratch—it's a tough cycle for any runner.
3. Playing three games a week with a squad full of injuries—it's a tough cycle for the players to maintain their intensity.
4. We have tight deadlines and messy source data; it's a tough cycle that leads to a lot of late nights for the team.
5. Pushing hard on workouts but not sleeping enough to recover—it's a tough cycle that eventually leads to burnout.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **A vicious circle** — implies a situation where an attempt to resolve a problem creates new problems.
- **A difficult loop** — a slightly less common but clear alternative.
- **A challenging pattern** — more formal, used to describe recurring difficulties.
- **A hard routine to break** — focuses on the difficulty of changing the situation.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **A virtuous cycle** — a repeating situation where good results lead to more good results.
- **A smooth process** — a situation that flows easily without repeating issues.
- **An easy routine** — comfortable and not challenging.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**

**4. lost your momentum for the weekend**
*(phrase)*: to lose the energy, enthusiasm, or forward progress you had, resulting in a lack of motivation to do things during your days off. *(Vietnamese: mất đi động lực/đà làm việc vào cuối tuần)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. After dealing with a severe production outage on Friday afternoon, I completely lost my momentum for the weekend.
2. I missed my Thursday tempo run due to rain, and somehow I just lost my momentum for the weekend long run.
3. Liverpool conceded a late equalizer on Saturday, and honestly, I completely lost my momentum for the weekend after that.
4. The stakeholder meeting went terribly, which meant I lost my momentum for the weekend and couldn't even enjoy my time off.
5. When you skip the first few training sessions of the week, it's easy to feel like you've lost your momentum for the weekend.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Lost your drive** — similar, focusing on internal motivation.
- **Ran out of steam** — informal, implies running out of energy.
- **Lost your motivation** — a more direct way of saying it.
- **Hit a wall** — implies a sudden stop in progress or energy.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Kept the ball rolling** — maintaining momentum and continuing progress.
- **Stayed fired up** — keeping a high level of enthusiasm.
- **Carried the energy into the weekend** — successfully maintaining momentum.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**

**5. shake it off**
*(verb phrase)*: to recover quickly from a setback, mistake, or negative feeling and move on without letting it affect you. *(Vietnamese: giũ bỏ nó đi/vượt qua nó)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Even though they went 1-0 down in the first ten minutes, the team managed to shake it off and win the game.
2. I accidentally dropped a development table, but I had to just shake it off, restore the backup, and keep working.
3. I had a terrible split on my third interval, but I told myself to shake it off and focus on the next one.
4. When a stakeholder heavily criticizes your dashboard, you just have to shake it off and ask for constructive feedback.
5. It's raining and cold, but if you want to stick to your marathon plan, you have to shake it off and get out the door.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Brush it off** — very similar; to ignore criticism or a minor issue.
- **Move on** — simple and direct.
- **Bounce back** — focuses on recovering your previous positive state.
- **Get over it** — a bit more direct and sometimes harsh.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Dwell on it** — to think about a negative event for too long.
- **Let it get to you** — allowing something to affect your emotions negatively.
- **Take it to heart** — to be deeply affected by criticism or failure.

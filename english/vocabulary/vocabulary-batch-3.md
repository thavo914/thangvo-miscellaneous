# Vocabulary Definitions: Batch 3 (13-20)

**13. humility**
*(noun)*: a modest or low view of one's own importance; the quality of not being proud or arrogant. *(Vietnamese: sự khiêm tốn)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Even after winning the Premier League, the manager showed true humility in his press conference, praising the backroom staff.
2. It takes a lot of humility to admit your data model design was flawed and start over from scratch.
3. No matter how fast you get, running a marathon will always teach you humility when you hit the wall at mile 20.
4. He accepted the criticism of his code review with complete humility, acknowledging he had much to learn.
5. Elite runners often possess a quiet humility because they know the sport is unpredictable.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Modesty** — the most direct synonym, focuses on not showing off.
- **Meekness** — slightly negative connotation, implies being overly submissive.
- **Unpretentiousness** — not trying to impress others.
- **Lack of ego** — very clear and casual description.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Arrogance** — having an exaggerated sense of one's own importance.
- **Hubris** — extreme pride or dangerous overconfidence.
- **Conceit** — excessive pride in oneself.

> You can see his ______________________ when he celebrates team goals more than his own.

**14. spinning your wheels in silence**
*(idiom)*: putting in a lot of effort without making any real progress, while not asking for help or communicating the struggle. *(Vietnamese: cố gắng trong vô vọng mà không lên tiếng / loay hoay trong im lặng)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. I spent three hours trying to fix the broken pipeline before I realized I was just spinning my wheels in silence instead of asking a senior engineer.
2. If you are injured and not getting faster, don't keep spinning your wheels in silence—talk to a coach.
3. The new player looked totally lost in the midfield system, just spinning his wheels in silence while the game bypassed him.
4. It's easy for remote workers to end up spinning their wheels in silence when they get stuck on a complex task.
5. Stop spinning your wheels in silence and post the error log in the team channel so we can help you out.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Struggling alone without progress** — descriptive and literal.
- **Going nowhere fast on your own** — highlights the lack of progress.
- **Suffering in silence** — focuses more on the pain/difficulty than the lack of progress.
- **Working hard but getting nowhere** — very clear and direct.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Making fast progress** — moving forward quickly.
- **Asking for help early** — resolving the "in silence" part.
- **Being highly productive** — getting things done efficiently.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> Instead of ______________________, you should just jump on a quick call with the architecture team.

> I realized my marathon training wasn't working, so I hired a coach rather than ______________________.

**15. keep yourself occupied or motivated while waiting on other people to finish their part**
*(phrase)*: to find productive or engaging things to do so you don't lose momentum when your work depends on someone else's completion. *(Vietnamese: giữ cho bản thân bận rộn/có động lực trong khi chờ người khác làm xong phần việc của họ)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. The hardest part of the data migration project is finding ways to keep yourself occupied or motivated while waiting on other people to finish their part.
2. In a relay race, you have to keep yourself occupied or motivated while waiting on other people to finish their part of the course.
3. As a manager, you need strategies to keep yourself occupied or motivated while waiting on other people to finish their part, rather than micromanaging them.
4. When the infrastructure team is provisioning the new servers, I try to keep myself occupied or motivated while waiting on other people to finish their part by writing documentation.
5. In the offseason, a manager must keep himself occupied or motivated while waiting on other people to finish their part of the transfer negotiations.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Stay busy while waiting for dependencies** — more tech-focused.
- **Maintain momentum during delays** — professional and clear.
- **Find things to do while blocked** — very common in agile/tech settings.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Sit around doing nothing** — wasting the waiting time.
- **Lose your drive while waiting** — the exact opposite of staying motivated.
- **Micromanage the other person** — trying to control them instead of focusing on your own tasks.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> When the upstream data isn't ready yet, how do you ______________________?

> It's a long gap between the morning heats and the evening finals, so athletes have to ______________________.

**16. it's a tough spot**
*(phrase)*: a difficult, awkward, or challenging situation to be in, often with no easy solution. *(Vietnamese: đó là một tình thế khó khăn/khó xử)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. With two key defenders injured right before the final, the manager admitted it's a tough spot to be in.
2. The stakeholders want the new dashboard tomorrow, but the source data is corrupted—it's a tough spot for the engineering team.
3. Trying to train for a marathon while working 60-hour weeks is possible, but it's a tough spot mentally and physically.
4. Having to tell your junior colleague that their code isn't good enough to deploy—it's a tough spot for any mentor.
5. When you realize halfway through a race that your pacing strategy was completely wrong, it's a tough spot to recover from.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **A difficult position** — slightly more formal.
- **A tricky situation** — similar meaning, implies complexity.
- **Between a rock and a hard place** — an idiom meaning caught between two bad choices.
- **A catch-22** — a paradoxical situation where you cannot escape because of contradictory rules.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **An easy win** — a situation that is simple to resolve successfully.
- **A comfortable position** — feeling secure and unchallenged.
- **A walk in the park** — idiom for something very easy.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> We have to cut the budget but still deliver the same features; ______________________.

> Losing your fastest runner right before the relay championship means ______________________.

**17. play the bad guy**
*(idiom)*: to take on the role of the person who enforces rules, delivers bad news, or makes unpopular decisions. *(Vietnamese: đóng vai ác / làm người xấu)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Sometimes as a tech lead, you have to play the bad guy and reject pull requests that don't meet our standards.
2. Klopp never wanted to play the bad guy, but occasionally he had to drop fan-favorite players for the good of the team.
3. I hate having to play the bad guy, but I had to tell my running partner we needed to skip our rest break if we wanted to hit our time goal.
4. If the stakeholders keep expanding the project scope, the project manager will have to play the bad guy and say no.
5. It's exhausting having to constantly play the bad guy when enforcing data governance rules across different departments.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Be the enforcer** — focuses on making people follow rules.
- **Be the bearer of bad news** — focuses on delivering negative information.
- **Put your foot down** — idiom for being strict and firm.
- **Take a hard line** — to be uncompromising on an issue.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Be the good cop** — the person who is friendly and accommodating.
- **Let things slide** — allowing rules to be broken without consequence.
- **Be a pushover** — someone who is easily manipulated and can't say no.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> I didn't want to ______________________, but I had to tell them the infrastructure wasn't ready.

> When the team loses focus during practice, the coach has to step in and ______________________.

**18. pushing your colleagues to get across the finish line**
*(phrase)*: actively encouraging, supporting, or pressuring coworkers to complete a difficult project or task. *(Vietnamese: thúc đẩy đồng nghiệp hoàn thành mục tiêu/dự án)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. The deployment was delayed, so the lead engineer spent the whole weekend pushing his colleagues to get across the finish line.
2. Just like pacing a teammate in a race, part of teamwork is pushing your colleagues to get across the finish line when they are exhausted.
3. A good captain is always pushing his colleagues to get across the finish line, even when the team is down a goal in the 89th minute.
4. We had a massive data backlog, and the manager was great at gently pushing your colleagues to get across the finish line without causing burnout.
5. There is a fine line between motivating your team and aggressively pushing your colleagues to get across the finish line.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Driving the team to deliver** — more corporate/management speak.
- **Encouraging the team to finish strong** — emphasizes positive motivation.
- **Getting the team over the hurdle** — focuses on overcoming a specific obstacle.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Dragging the team down** — negatively affecting team performance.
- **Letting the project stall** — allowing momentum to die.
- **Leaving them to figure it out alone** — withdrawing support.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> With only two days until the product launch, we are completely focused on ______________________.

> It takes strong leadership skills to succeed in ______________________ without destroying team morale.

**19. tricky**
*(adjective)*: requiring care, skill, or tact because it is difficult, complicated, or awkward. *(Vietnamese: phức tạp, rắc rối, khó xử)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. Writing a SQL query to handle overlapping timestamps is always a tricky problem to solve.
2. Pacing a hilly half marathon is tricky because you have to adjust your speed constantly without burning out.
3. Replacing a club legend like Steven Gerrard was a very tricky situation for the manager to navigate.
4. It's a tricky conversation to have when you need to tell a senior developer their architecture design is flawed.
5. Balancing marathon training with a full-time data engineering job can be incredibly tricky.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Complicated** — involving many different parts or factors.
- **Delicate** — requiring sensitive handling (especially for social situations).
- **Complex** — intellectually difficult.
- **Awkward** — causing embarrassment or difficulty.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- **Straightforward** — uncomplicated and easy to do or understand.
- **Simple** — having few parts, easily solved.
- **Clear-cut** — unambiguous and easy to define.

**"Your Turn" Prompts:**
> Navigating the office politics during a major reorganization is always a very ______________________ process.

> Handling the late-game substitutions when you only have a one-goal lead is extremely ______________________.

**20. give people some space when they're off, or try to cheer them up**
*(phrase)*: the dilemma of choosing whether to leave someone alone when they are upset or underperforming, or to actively try to improve their mood. *(Vietnamese: để cho mọi người không gian riêng khi họ buồn/không tốt, hay cố gắng động viên họ)*

**Sample Sentences:**
1. When a teammate misses a crucial penalty, you have to decide whether to give people some space when they're off, or try to cheer them up immediately.
2. Managing a stressed engineering team is hard because you don't always know if you should give people some space when they're off, or try to cheer them up.
3. After my friend had a terrible race and missed his PR, I wasn't sure whether to give him some space when he was off, or try to cheer him up with a beer.
4. A good leader learns the emotional intelligence required to know when to give people some space when they're off, or try to cheer them up.
5. When a coworker is frustrated by endless pipeline failures, I usually give people some space when they're off, or try to cheer them up by offering to take over a ticket.

**Synonyms / Alternatives:**
- **Leave them alone vs. comfort them** — a simpler way to express the same dilemma.
- **Back off vs. offer support** — a more casual phrasing.
- **Give them distance vs. lift their spirits** — focuses on the emotional aspect.

**Antonyms / Contrasts:**
- *(Note: This is a complex situational phrase, so it doesn't have a direct antonym. However, the opposite of the entire thoughtful approach would be "ignoring their feelings completely.")*

> After a tough defeat, the manager has to decide if it's better to ______________________.

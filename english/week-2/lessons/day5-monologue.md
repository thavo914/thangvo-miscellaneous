# Day 5: Recorded Monologue (60 min)

## Phase 1: Warm-up (15 min)

### Exercise A: Lexical Labeling (5 min)
*Goal: Associate objects directly with English sounds.*
- Look at the objects in your room. Name them aloud.
- If you see a book, say: *"This is a hardcover book. It has many pages of literature."*
- If you see your computer, say: *"I use this for scanning emails, but not for reading literature."*

### Exercise B: Syntactic Narration (10 min)
*Goal: Building momentum in English thought.*
- Narrate your current actions aloud:
- *"I am opening my notebook now. I'm going to record a monologue about the soothing power of books. I need to remember the word 'hemisphere' and 'stimulus'. My goal is to speak for two minutes without stopping."*

---

## Phase 2: Recorded Monologue (30 min)
**Topic**: My Personal View on Bibliotherapy and the Power of Books.

**Structure Table**:
| Section | Time | Content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Opening** | 15s | State if you think books are a good "remedy" for modern stress. |
| **First point** | 30s | Explain your own reading habits (Do you prefer scanning or deep reading?). |
| **Counterpoint** | 30s | Acknowledge that books take effort, but mention the brain "coming to life". |
| **Expert idea** | 20s | Mention Professor Davis's idea about the left and right hemispheres. |
| **Your verdict** | 20s | Agree/disagree that bibliotherapy should be prescribed by doctors. |
| **Closing** | 5s | One final sentence on your favorite book. |

---

## Recording Protocol
1. **Attempt 1**: Record for 2 minutes. **DO NOT STOP**, even if you make a mistake.
   - *Use Recovery Phrases*: "What I mean is...", "Let me put it another way...", "How should I say this..."
2. **Review**: Listen back to Attempt 1. Check for one specific problem (e.g., forgotten target word, too many "um/ah" sounds).
3. **Attempt 2**: Record again. Focus on fixing that one problem.
4. **Attempt 3 (Optional)**: If you're feeling confident, record a "premium" version.

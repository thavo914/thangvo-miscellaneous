# Day 3: Artificial Interlocutor Simulation (60 min)

## Rules for AI Interactions
- Level: **B1-B2 Intermediate**.
- No mid-conversation corrections (request feedback at the end).
- If you get stuck, use a "bridge" phrase: *"What I mean is..."* or *"How do you say [concept]?"*

---

## Round 1: Discussion with a Curious Peer (15 min)
**Role**: The AI is your friend who also likes reading but isn't sure about "bibliotherapy".
**Prompt**:
> "I'd like to practice my English speaking/chatting. You are my curious friend. We are discussing the BBC episode 'The soothing power of books'. I think reading literature is much more therapeutic than just scanning news on my phone. What do you think? Ask me 3 challenging questions about my reading habits and why I think books help me relax. Use B1-B2 level English. Don't correct me during the chat, but at the end, list 3 target words I used correctly and 1 grammar mistake I made."

---

## Round 2: Expert Interview (15 min)
**Role**: The AI is Professor Philip Davis, the expert from the episode.
**Prompt**:
> "You are Professor Philip Davis, an expert in bibliotherapy. I am a student interviewing you. I will ask you 3-4 questions about how literature makes our brains 'come to life' and the role of the left and right hemispheres. Answer based on the BBC episode 'The soothing power of books', but feel free to expand. After the interview, give me feedback on the naturalness of my questions and suggest 2 more professional ways to ask about brain activity."

---

## Round 3: Devil's Advocate (20 min)
**Role**: You argue for bibliotherapy; the AI argues it's just a "placebo" and that reading is old-fashioned.
**Prompt**:
> "Let's have a debate. I believe 'bibliotherapy' is a scientifically proven way to improve mental health. You are the 'Devil's Advocate'—you think reading books is outdated and that modern technology like VR or apps is much better for relaxation. We will have 4 exchanges. Challenge my points. Use the word 'stimulus' and 'scanning' in your arguments. After 4 rounds, grade my ability to use linking words and my vocabulary range. Suggest 2 more natural phrases I could have used to defend my position."

---

# Lesson Overview: The soothing power of books

## Metadata
- **Title**: The soothing power of books
- **Source**: BBC Learning English (6 Minute English)
- **CEFR Level**: B1-B2 (Intermediate)
- **Topic**: Bibliotherapy - how reading literature helps mental health and brain activity.

## Learning Objectives
| Category | Objective |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Listening** | Identify differences between scanning and reading literature in natural speech. |
| **Vocabulary** | Master 6 target words related to reading and neurobiology. |
| **Pronunciation** | Master connected speech (catenation/elision) in a technical explanation. |
| **Speaking** | Discuss personal reading habits and views on "bibliotherapy". |
| **Thinking** | Directly associate reading concepts with English terms (no translation). |

## Target Vocabulary
| Word | POS | Meaning | Episode Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **scanning** | noun/verb | reading rapidly in order to find specific facts | "Above all, that it’s not to do with scanning..." |
| **literature** | noun | written works (novels, poetry, plays) with artistic value | "...when they’re reading literature." |
| **hemisphere** | noun | half of a sphere (usually the earth or the brain) | "The brain begins to work... from a different hemisphere..." |
| **coming to life** | idiom | starting to be activated after a quiet period | "...you can see the brain coming to life..." |
| **scalp** | noun | skin under the hair on your head | "...you put electrodes on the scalp..." |
| **stimulus** | noun | something that encourages activity in people or things | "...as it takes in this new stimulus." |

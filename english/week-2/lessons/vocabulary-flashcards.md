Back: **scanning** /ˈskænɪŋ/ | noun/verb | Reading rapidly in order to find specific facts.

Episode sentence: "Above all, that it’s not to do with **scanning**, with quick reading, when they’re reading literature."

## Card 2 - literature

Front: What is the term for written works, such as novels, poetry, and plays, that are considered to have artistic value?

Back: **literature** /ˈlɪtrətʃə/ | noun | Written works including novels, poetry and plays which are considered to have artistic value.

Episode sentence: "But the real therapy happens when a group reads **literature** – written works such as novels, poems or plays..."

Your sentence: __________________________________________________________________
*(Context: In the tech world, reading technical literature is essential for staying updated.)*

[Pronunciation drill: The 't' in the middle is often softened. Listen for /ˈlɪtrətʃə/ not "liter-a-ture".]

## Card 3 - hemisphere

Front: What do you call one half of a sphere, especially when referring to the left or right side of the brain?

Back: **hemisphere** /ˈhemɪsfɪə/ | noun | Half of a sphere, usually the earth or the brain.

Episode sentence: "The brain begins to work from different parts, from a different **hemisphere** and it gets excited..."

Your sentence: __________________________________________________________________
*(Context: Data scientists sometimes discuss which hemisphere of the brain is more active during complex coding.)*

[Pronunciation drill: Hemi-sphere. The 'ph' makes an /f/ sound.]

## Card 4 - coming to life

Front: What idiom describes something starting to be active or energized again after a period of being quiet or inactive?

Back: **coming to life** /ˈkʌmɪŋ tuː laɪf/ | idiom | Starting to be activated after a quiet period.

Episode sentence: "...you can see the brain **coming to life** and it’s that life that is important in terms of these reading groups."

Your sentence: __________________________________________________________________
*(Context: The project is finally coming to life after months of planning.)*

[Register note: This is a very natural and evocative phrase for describing progress.]

## Card 5 - scalp

Front: What is the medical/anatomical term for the skin on the top of your head, underneath your hair?

Back: **scalp** /skælp/ | noun | Skin under the hair on your head.

Episode sentence: "...you put electrodes on the **scalp** and it measures electricity..."

Your sentence: __________________________________________________________________
*(Context: The VR headset was a bit tight on my scalp during the demo.)*

[Grammar warning: Countable noun. Use "the scalp" or "my scalp".]

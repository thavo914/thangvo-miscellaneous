## ⚡ Section 0: Block 1 - Warm-up Reflex & Active Listening (30 Mins)

### ⏱️ Speaking Reflex: The 60-Second Random Object Pitch (15 Mins)
* **Goal**: Build spontaneous fluency under pressure without pausing >2 seconds.
* **Instructions**:
  1. Pick a random item on your desk (e.g., your coffee mug).
  2. Set a timer for 60 seconds.
  3. Speak non-stop about the object. Use circumlocution if you forget a word.

#### 💡 Worked Example Script (60-Second Object Pitch - Coffee Mug):
> *"Right here in front of me is my ceramic coffee mug. I bought it about two years ago during a business trip. It's dark blue, slightly heavy, and holds about 350 milliliters of coffee. What I really love about this mug isn't just its appearance, but the routine it represents. Every morning, filling this mug reminds me to slow down and **keep things in perspective** before staring at code all day. Sometimes, when a server crashes or a query takes too long, I take a sip from this mug and tell myself to let minor stress be like **water off a duck's back**. It's amazing how a simple mug can help you stay grounded!"*

### 🗣️ Active Listening: Audio Echoing / Real-Time Shadowing (15 Mins)
* **Goal**: Sync acoustic hearing directly with speech output.
* **Instructions**:
  1. Play Dialogue 1 from `lesson-overview.md`.
  2. Begin echoing the speakers' words out loud with a **0.5-second delay** (like an acoustic echo).

#### 💡 Worked Demonstration (Echoing Technique):
* *Audio*: "The joy isn't in reaching the goal..."
* *Your Echo (0.5s later)*: "...The joy isn't in reaching the goal..."
* *Audio*: "...it's the process of getting there."
* *Your Echo (0.5s later)*: "...it's the process of getting there."
* *Key Focus*: Do NOT pause the audio. Keep talking continuously while listening.

## 🎙️ Section 1: Block 2 - IELTS Speaking Practice Questions & Model Answers (90 Mins)

### Part 1: Introductory Questions & Model Answers

#### Question 1: How do you usually handle stress when working on a tight deadline?
* **Model Answer (Band 6.5-7.0)**:
  > *"When facing tight deadlines, I try to **keep things in perspective** by breaking down complex tasks into smaller manageable steps. In data engineering, unexpected bugs happen all the time, so I've learned not to **take things personally** when code fails. Staying calm helps me focus on the solution rather than panicking."*

#### Question 2: Do you prefer setting long-term goals or focusing on daily habits?
* **Model Answer (Band 6.5-7.0)**:
  > *"I definitely prefer focusing on daily habits. While long-term goals give direction, **the joy isn't in reaching the finish line, it's the process of growing** every day. Building consistent daily routines makes achieving long-term objectives much more natural."*

#### Question 3: What is something new you have tried recently outside your comfort zone?
* **Model Answer (Band 6.5-7.0)**:
  > *"Recently, I volunteered to present a technical architecture design in English to our regional management team. I was quite nervous at first, but **the more you step out of your comfort zone, the easier it gets**. It really boosted my confidence!"*

---

### Part 2: Cue Card Prompt & Worked Model Response

```text
Describe a time when you stepped out of your comfort zone to achieve something.
You should say:
- What the situation was
- What challenges you faced
- How you handled your fear or uncertainty
and explain what you learned from the experience.
```

#### 💡 Worked Model Response (Band 6.5-7.0):
> *"I'd like to talk about an experience from last year when I volunteered to deliver a technical live demo to our company's executive stakeholders. 
> 
> At the time, I had mostly worked behind the scenes as a backend data engineer, so speaking in front of senior management in English was way outside my comfort zone. My biggest challenge was the fear of choking during live Q&A or stumbling over technical terminology. 
> 
> To handle my uncertainty, I prepared thoroughly and reminded myself that **what I really wanted wasn't just to stay safe, but to grow professionally**. During the presentation, when a senior director asked a critical question about pipeline latency, I took a deep breath, treated the feedback as **water off a duck's back**, and answered clearly. 
> 
> From that experience, I realized that **it's amazing what can happen when you let go of that fear**. Stepping out of my comfort zone proved to me that growth comes from facing challenges directly."*

---

### Part 3: Analytical Discussion Questions & Model Answers

#### Question 1: Why do many people find it difficult to keep things in perspective when facing setbacks?
* **Model Answer (Band 6.5-7.0)**:
  > *"I think many people tend to fixate on immediate failure because of high expectations. In fast-paced modern workplaces, it's easy to **take things personally** when things don't go as planned. However, if people step back and look at the bigger picture, they'd realize that setbacks are just temporary learning opportunities in the overall process."*

#### Question 2: How has social media influenced the way people measure their personal fulfillment today?
* **Model Answer (Band 6.5-7.0)**:
  > *"Social media often encourages people to compare their behind-the-scenes struggles with other people's highlight reels. This makes people feel that **what they really want is superficial validation** rather than genuine personal growth. To stay happy, it's crucial to focus on one's own progress rather than external applause."*

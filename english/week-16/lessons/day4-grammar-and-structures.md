## 🧠 Section 1: Block 2 - Tech Grammar Focus (90 Mins)

### 1. Passive Voice in Present Perfect (`has/have been + P.P.`)
- **Quote**: *"The pipeline issues have been resolved by our on-call engineer."*
- **Explanation**: Focuses on completed actions or ongoing state changes where the outcome matters more than who performed it. Essential for incident status updates.
- > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Pitfall**: Omitting *been* in Present Perfect Passive.
  > *Incorrect*: The pipeline has fixed by the team.
  > *Correct*: The pipeline has been fixed by the team.

#### 💡 Real-Life Technical Examples:
1. *Data Engineering*: The missing active user events **have been backfilled** successfully into Postgres.
2. *Casino Operations*: Real-time payout transaction logs **have been verified** against bank statements.
3. *Workplace*: Stakeholders **have been notified** about the scheduled maintenance window.

---

### 2. Cleft Sentences for Emphasis (`What... was...`)
- **Quote**: *"What caused the discrepancy was late-arriving data from the mobile app."*
- **Explanation**: A cleft structure splits a sentence to place strong emphasis on the core root cause (*What + clause + was/is + Noun/Clause*).
- > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Pitfall**: Incorrect verb agreement after *What*.
  > *Incorrect*: What caused the issues were late data.
  > *Correct*: What caused the issues was late data.

#### 💡 Real-Life Technical Examples:
1. *Data Engineering*: **What triggered the alert** was an unindexed foreign key in our staging database.
2. *Casino Operations*: **What delayed the player payout summary** was an upstream API timeout.
3. *Workplace*: **What restored stakeholder trust** was our transparent post-mortem report.

---

### 3. Future Continuous for Scheduled Plans (`will be + V-ing`)
- **Quote**: *"We will be backfilling the missing hours throughout the afternoon."*
- **Explanation**: Used to describe an action that will be in progress at a specific future time or as part of an established schedule.
- > [!WARNING]
  > **Common Pitfall**: Using Simple Future when describing an ongoing activity at a specific time.
  > *Incorrect*: At 3 PM, I will fix the pipeline.
  > *Correct*: At 3 PM, I will be fixing the pipeline.

#### 💡 Real-Life Technical Examples:
1. *Data Engineering*: At 4 PM today, **we will be deploying the new Spark job** to production.
2. *Casino Operations*: The infrastructure team **will be monitoring server traffic** during the weekend tournament.
3. *Workplace*: **I will be updating the team channel** as soon as the backfill finishes.

## 📐 Section 2: Sentence Structure Templates (5 Patterns)

### Template 1: `I totally understand your concern about + [Noun/Verb-ing]`
* **Communicative Function**: Empathetically acknowledging stakeholder worry.
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday*: I totally understand your concern about the flight delay.
  2. *Data Engineering*: I totally understand your concern about the missing active users metric.
  3. *Workplace*: I totally understand your concern about the project deadline.

### Template 2: `What caused the discrepancy was + [Noun Phrase / Clause]`
* **Communicative Function**: Pinpointing the technical root cause clearly.
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday*: What caused the delay was unexpected morning traffic.
  2. *Data Engineering*: What caused the discrepancy was an unannounced upstream API change.
  3. *Workplace*: What caused the miscommunication was an outdated email thread.

### Template 3: `We are currently in the process of + [Verb-ing] + to ensure + [Clause]`
* **Communicative Function**: Explaining current remediation actions reassuringly.
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday*: We are currently in the process of updating our system to ensure privacy.
  2. *Data Engineering*: We are currently in the process of backfilling records to ensure data completeness.
  3. *Workplace*: We are currently in the process of reviewing pipeline logs to ensure zero data loss.

### Template 4: `Rest assured, we will have + [Noun Phrase] + resolved by + [Time]`
* **Communicative Function**: Providing a firm, reassuring commitment to stakeholders.
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday*: Rest assured, we will have your room ready by 2 PM.
  2. *Data Engineering*: Rest assured, we will have the entire pipeline fully resolved by 3 PM today.
  3. *Workplace*: Rest assured, we will have the dashboard updated before the executive meeting.

### Template 5: `Going forward, we'll implement + [Noun Phrase] + so that + [Clause]`
* **Communicative Function**: Outline preventive measures to maintain trust.
* **Examples**:
  1. *Everyday*: Going forward, we'll set up automated reminders so that we don't miss appointments.
  2. *Data Engineering*: Going forward, we'll implement a new monitoring alert so that we catch these delays early.
  3. *Workplace*: Going forward, we'll schedule weekly syncs so that everyone stays aligned.

## 🔍 Section 4: Spot the Mistake (Error Correction)

Rewrite the incorrect sentences:
1. *Incorrect*: I am looking to the issue and will bring you speed soon.
2. *Incorrect*: What caused the lag it was an unindexed table in MySQL.
3. *Incorrect*: Rest assured, we will resolved the bug by end of day.

<details>
<summary>🔑 Click to View Answer Key</summary>

1. **Correct**: *I am **looking into** the issue and will bring you **up to** speed soon.* (Fix phrasal verbs: *look into*, *bring up to speed*).
2. **Correct**: *What caused the lag **was** an unindexed table in MySQL.* (Remove redundant pronoun *it*).
3. **Correct**: *Rest assured, we will **have** resolved the bug / **have the bug resolved** by end of day.* (Causative / Modal structure fix).
